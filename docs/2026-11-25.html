The math broke

ω = 9/4: The Matrix Multiplication Exponent Is Solved
OpenAI Math / Hacker News

Since Strassen showed in the late 1960s that you could multiply matrices faster than the naïve O(n³), the true exponent ω has been an open question — with most researchers betting on ω=2. This preprint claims to prove ω=9/4 exactly, both an upper and lower bound, meaning the answer is a clean rational number and the decades-long drift toward 2 was wrong. The lower bound is the genuinely shocking half: proving ω>2 was considered nearly as hard as P≠NP.

Voyager 1 Goes Silent at One Light-Day from Earth
Hackaday

Launched in 1977, Voyager 1 is now one light-day out — and has stopped sending science and engineering data, transmitting only a bare carrier wave. It's the third serious silence in recent years; the last was resolved by remotely diagnosing and bypassing a failed RAM chip across 24+ billion kilometers. The RTGs are running low, fuel lines have clogged, and nobody knows if it makes it to the 2030s — we may be in the last years of the most distant object humans have ever made.

Off the map

Man a Machine — La Mettrie's 1749 First Edition
The Public Domain Review

In 1747, physician Julien Offray de La Mettrie extended Descartes' 'animals are machines' claim to humans and was driven into exile — Calvinists, Catholics, and Lutherans briefly united just to persecute him. Frederick the Great gave him asylum and later delivered his funeral oration. The Public Domain Review surfaces the 1749 first edition in a week when AI systems are proving theorems that humans can verify but not fully grasp; La Mettrie's question — where exactly does the machine end and the mind begin — has never been more live.

For you

FILM
Sans Soleil
Chris Marker

A film-essay that works like a generative system — images, memory, and time folded into each other through a narrator who keeps interrogating how images encode feeling. The 1983 sequences on Japan and Guinea sit next to meditations on video synthesis and the Zone Gede in ways that feel less like editing than like a mind thinking aloud. For someone who lives at the edge of physical and digital, Marker's obsession with what images actually *do* to consciousness is the right kind of rabbit hole.

ART
Every piece in the ongoing practice
Vera Molnár

The Ur-source of generative 2D art — a painter who, starting in 1968, wrote her own programs to systematically disrupt geometric order and study what deviation feels like. Her work is the rare case where the system *is* the subject: she called her pre-computer method 'imaginary machine' and treated rule-breaking as the only honest form of self-expression. If you make generative 3D and want to understand what the tradition actually thought it was doing, Molnár is the starting point.

MUSIC
Disintegration Loops
William Basinski

Basinski discovered these while transferring 1980s tape loops to digital — the magnetic oxide flaked off in real time, and the recordings captured their own erasure. The result is 4+ hours of music that is literally a system destroying itself, structured by entropy rather than composition. The physical/digital threshold, the loop as form, the brutal beauty of irreversible process — it hits everything at once.

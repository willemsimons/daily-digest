A Peek Inside the Black Box of Epigenetic Clocks
Nautilus

USC researchers cross-referenced methylation patterns from five major epigenetic clocks against gene expression data from 3,000+ participants and found something striking: not a single biological pathway was shared by all five clocks. They're measuring meaningfully different things — immune/inflammatory function, metabolism, cell maintenance — which means two clocks can give different 'biological ages' and both be right about different aspects of aging.

Making things, ancient and recent

Mammoth Ivory Carvings: Thumbnail-Sized, 40,000 Years Old
Nautilus

Two bird figurines found at Hohle Fels Cave in southwestern Germany — each barely 0.79 × 0.39 inches, carved from mammoth ivory — are among the earliest known representational art and the smallest objects yet found at this site, which has previously yielded figurines three to four times larger. What stops you cold is the specificity: one bird appears to be brooding, with a carved head, beak, eyes, and feather markings detailed enough that researchers think they can identify the species. Someone at the edge of the last Ice Age made a tiny, precise, lovingly observed bird.

The answer lives in a file called GrowthHack.tsx: Bluesky renders its logo behind a UITextField with isSecureTextEntry set to true, which iOS blanks automatically on screenshot — so the normally-hidden logo is what remains visible in the capture. Quake Shareware on a CD That Was Just a Little Too Full
Fabien Sanglard

id Software's 1996 shareware Quake used only 22 MB of a 640 MB CD — so someone had the idea of filling the remaining space with encrypted versions of id's entire back catalogue, playable if you bought an unlock code. Fabien Sanglard reverse-engineers exactly how they packed it and how the encryption scheme worked, and the essay becomes a case study in how abundant capacity creates its own creative constraints. Carmack's 1994 quote — that if DOOM shipped on CD they'd have room for 'a one-hour feature film on the making of DOOM' — lands differently once you understand the gap they were actually trying to fill.

Off the map

Olo: The Color You Cannot See Without a Laser Aimed at Your Retina
Wikipedia

In April 2025, UC Berkeley researchers used adaptive optics laser ophthalmoscopy to individually map and stimulate only M cone cells in five subjects' retinas — bypassing the physical impossibility of monochromatic light that activates M cones alone. All five described 'olo' as a blue-green of unprecedented saturation, more vivid than anything in the sRGB gamut; its closest hex approximation is #00FFCC. The color is named for its LMS coordinates (0,1,0), which spell 'olo' in leet — and it exists in a part of color space that no display, pigment, or wavelength of light can reach.

Make something

Make a physical object that encodes something digital — take a short sound, text, or image and find a way to store it in a material form (QR codes on paper, laser-etched pattern, punched holes, drawn waveform). The constraint: it should be readable by a machine, but also have a visual logic a person could follow by hand if they understood the encoding. Think about what the Paper Tunes project and the Quake CD are both doing: abundance and scarcity as creative constraints, and the pleasure of a physical carrier for an invisible thing.

Dutch Central Bank Quietly Moves €10B in Gold Out of North America
NL Times

The Nederlandsche Bank physically transferred 86,000 kg of gold from U.S. and Canadian vaults to the Bank of England between March and August, citing the need to improve 'marketability' in a crisis — central bank code for: we need to be able to liquidate fast without asking anyone's permission. A G10 institution explicitly citing U.S. instability as the reason for repositioning reserves is the slow-moving alarm that matters more than any headline. The Bank of England is now home to 32% of Dutch gold; New York has been reduced to 18.5%.

BGP Hijack Used to Poison Software Updates Across Production Networks
Ars Technica

Attackers hijacked IP space belonging to Softaculous — a platform managing software installs across thousands of hosting environments — by exploiting lax BGP routing security at Hetzner and then obtaining valid TLS certificates for the hijacked addresses, making their malware-laced updates indistinguishable from legitimate ones. The attack succeeded largely because Softaculous never implemented code signing on its updates, one of the most basic supply-chain hygiene steps. This is infrastructure-level geopolitics: whoever controls the routing table controls what 'legitimate' looks like.

FTC Alleges Amazon Secretly Rigged Ad Auctions for Seven Years, Extracted $20B
Ars Technica

The FTC, joined by 22 state AGs, alleges Amazon has since 2019 been overriding the actual results of its Sponsored Products auctions and substituting higher prices it set unilaterally — telling advertisers they were paying market rates while quietly extracting an estimated $20 billion in excess charges from 1.2 million customers. Internal documents reportedly describe these as 'hidden surcharges.' The structural point is larger than Amazon: if the auction mechanism is fake, the entire premise of programmatic advertising as a fair market collapses.

How knowledge gets made and gamed

Three Sites Built 215,128 'Best Software' Pages Specifically to Feed AI Citations
Trellner

Across 380 software categories, 59.8% of the URLs that Perplexity retrieved as grounding sources ranked outside the top 100,000 most-visited websites — and three sites under apparent common control, none of which existed before December 2023, have published 215,128 machine-generated 'best [category]' pages with homepage titles literally reading 'Facts & Grounding Page.' The citation graph that AI systems treat as epistemic ground truth is being actively manufactured at scale, not for human readers, but for the retrieval step. This is what the information environment looks like when the audience is models.

LLM Internal Representations Can Be Closely Approximated by Symbolic Structures
arXiv

A new paper proposes that neural networks — despite operating in continuous vector space — implicitly realize symbolic structure: the researchers show they can replace a network's entire representation-generating process with a closed-form symbolic equation and its behavior remains largely unchanged, across arithmetic, logic, code, and natural language. If it holds up, this reconciles the 60-year war between symbolic AI and connectionism with empirical evidence rather than philosophy. It also opens a practical door: if you can read the symbolic structure, you can intervene on it precisely.

Bodies and time

Your Organs Age on Three Different Schedules — and They Appear to Coordinate
Nautilus

A deep-learning analysis of 25,000+ tissue images from nearly 1,000 donors found organs fall into three distinct aging patterns: early-accelerating (blood vessels spike in your 30s then plateau), late-accelerating (uterus, vagina accelerate around 50-59), and 'biphasic' — the majority of organs, including the entire digestive tract, show two distinct acceleration peaks across a lifetime. The framing shift is the point: organs aren't independent clocks running at different speeds, they appear to be a synchronized system, which means aging interventions targeting a single organ may be missing the coordination layer entirely.

Off the map

Male Chimpanzees Have Been Throwing Rocks at the Same Trees for Over a Decade
Nautilus

In four wild chimpanzee populations, adult males repeatedly return to specific trees, throw rocks at them, pant-hoot, and drum the trunk — with no apparent foraging or survival benefit, and piles of accumulated stones as the only evidence when they leave. The behavior is geographically restricted in ways that can't be explained by rock or tree availability, which points strongly to cultural transmission: younger males learning a ritual from older ones. Researchers think it may be the earliest observable form of non-verbal symbolic marking in a non-human species, which makes it a live window into how something like a shrine begins.

The Ambassadors: Holbein's Anamorphic Skull
Great Art Explained / YouTube

Great Art Explained does exactly one thing: takes a single canonical painting and makes you understand why it's strange and alive. Holbein's 1533 double portrait is one of the great surrealist objects before surrealism existed — two men surrounded by instruments of knowledge and power, with a smear in the foreground that only resolves into a human skull when you view the canvas from an extreme oblique angle. It's a painting about the limits of perspective itself, made by someone who understood that what you see depends entirely on where you stand.

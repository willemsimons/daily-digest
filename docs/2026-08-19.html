Ukrainian Drones Overwhelm Russian Tanks' New Active Protection System — For Now
Ars Technica

Russia's Arena-M active protection system made its combat debut on July 22 in Donetsk and did manage to shoot down incoming drones — but Ukrainian operators responded by saturating each tank with 15–20 drones until it died anyway. The telling detail is the arithmetic: defense works until it doesn't scale, and the defenders are already iterating on the ratio. Rob Lee's read is that this isn't a failure story, it's the opening move of a new layer in the arms race.

Making and thinking

A 3D Fruit Fly on macOS Desktop Powered by the Real FlyWire Connectome
GitHub / Hacker News

Someone took the complete FlyWire connectome — 139,255 neurons mapped — extracted a 668-neuron escape circuit with ~19,000 real synaptic connections, ran it as a 1kHz leaky-integrate-and-fire simulation, and made it a desktop pet. When you move your cursor fast toward it, the Giant Fiber neurons actually spike through real synaptic pathways and the fly bolts in ~4ms, exactly as a biological fly does; slow approaches are tolerated because 1,200 feedforward inhibition synapses push back. This is a real biological system rendered as interactive geometry, and it's free to run.

Finger: The 1971 Social Network That Never Died
andros.dev / Hacker News

Finger was born at Stanford's AI Lab in 1971 because Les Earnest kept watching researchers run their fingers down a WHO display trying to find colleagues — so he built a protocol that let you query a plain text file about any user on any connected machine. John Carmack used it as a public dev journal during the Quake era; it still works today; your Mac has the client installed. The piece is a genuinely good archaeology of how presence, identity, and social norms on networks were shaped by a tool almost no one knows existed.

Art and witness

Julie Green's 'The Last Supper' Memorializes Hundreds of People's Final Meals While on Death Row
Colossal

From 1999 until her death in 2021, Green painted final meal requests from death row inmates in cobalt blue mineral paint on mass-produced ceramic platters — everything from KFC buckets to a single plate reading 'NONE.' One plate reads 'He never had a birthday cake, so we ordered a birthday cake for him. He was only 15.' Green's own line — 'Selections provide clues on region, race, and economic background' — is the whole project in a sentence: the most ordinary human act, choice of food, as the last legible trace of a person the system otherwise renders invisible.

Sipho Mabona: Milk Cartons and Bottle Gourds Form a Tribute to His Late Father
Colossal

Mabona cut triangular and rectangular windows into dried calabash gourds and replaced them with fragments of Tetra Pak milk cartons — the same material that displaced the calabash as a beverage container — stitched with colored thread, each piece centered on a small curved mirror. Embedded audio plays his late father, scholar and activist Mongameli Mabona, reading his own poems and his doctoral thesis on Xhosa cultural history. The swap of materials isn't metaphor bolted on; it's structural: the thing that replaced the thing, made to carry the memory of what it replaced.

Off the map

Thieves of Cursed Rocks Pen Letters of Remorse to a National Park
Aeon

Petrified Forest National Park receives a steady stream of stolen rocks mailed back by visitors convinced the theft cursed their lives — car accidents, divorces, job losses, mysterious illness. Aeon's documentary short treats these letters as genuine folk mythology: secular people, confronted with bad luck, reaching for magical causality and choosing confession as the remedy. It's guilt archaeology, and it says something true about how thin the rationalist layer actually is.

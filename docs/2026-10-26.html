AI's actual texture

Mapping AI Colonialism, Block by Block
Brown University

Brown senior Cunyan Ma built an interactive map connecting tech companies — through their intermediaries — to the data workers in the Global South whose annotation labor makes AI function. Most of what's known about this workforce comes from scattered journalism; this is the first attempt to aggregate databases, original collection, and news reports into a single visible geography. Ma frames it explicitly as 'AI colonialism made spatial' — and the map is freely available online.

The Safety Discourse Is Optimizing for the Wrong Disaster
MIT Technology Review

The piece makes a clean structural argument: industry-backed AI safety legislation targets catastrophic edge cases — the Hollywood scenarios — while the slow diffuse damage (dependency, cognitive atrophy, engineered envy) accrues unreported. A teenager with an unhealthy AI companion never appears in a lawsuit. A student who outsources all thinking to a chatbot doesn't make headlines. The bar 'doesn't encourage suicide' is technically clearable while a product still corrodes how an entire generation thinks.

Biology on the edge

60 Years of Cellular Aging, Reversed in One Conversion
The Debrief / Aging Cell

University of Bonn researchers converted blood cells from an 80-year-old directly into neural stem cells — and the resulting cells showed molecular age markers under 20. The key detail: they bypassed induced pluripotency entirely, going cell-type to cell-type, which appears to carry the de-aging effect through the transition. Published in peer-reviewed Aging Cell; early-stage, not a therapy, but the mechanistic finding about what reprogramming does to the aging clock is genuinely novel.

Off the map

Switzerland at 1:1 Scale, One Block Per Meter
manifold3d

Someone rendered the entire country of Switzerland in Minecraft-style voxels at true 1:1 scale from SwissTopo open geodata — every block is one meter, the Matterhorn is exactly as tall and steep as the real one, and glaciers, lakes, forests, and dam walls are all present. A slider overlays the actual Swiss aerial photograph. The Jungfrau-Aletsch UNESCO region (2,400 km²) is free; the full country costs 15 CHF. It's territory-as-medium: the most literal possible collapse of cartography and fabrication logic into a single navigable object.

The $40 Synth That Became a Community Firmware Playground
Ars Technica

The M-Vave FM-1 launched as a cheap pocket FM synthesizer; by July, Reddit's r/synthdiy had reverse-engineered its firmware; by September, custom builds were shipping; by October, M-Vave responded not with a cease-and-desist but by open-sourcing the whole thing under GPLv3 and releasing an SDK with a browser-based workbench. There are now more community firmware projects than anyone can track — and yes, it plays Doom. It's a rare case of a hardware company leaning into the hack instead of killing it.

For you

FILM
Mauvais Sang
Leos Carax

Carax builds a film that runs entirely on surface-as-system — color, gesture, music, and bodies in space as a kind of generative language. Denis Lavant's long sprint through a Paris night to David Bowie is one of cinema's great moments of pure expressive force; the rest of the film earns it.

ART
UUmwelt
Pierre Huyghe

Huyghe partnered with neuroscientists in Kyoto, had a subject imagine images while an fMRI scanned their brain activity, then let a neural network generate thousands of visual reconstructions from that data — hallucinatory, unstable mental images installed across MoMA's Sculpture Garden. It's the feedback loop between cognition, biology, and machine vision made physically inhabitable.

MUSIC
re:member
Ólafur Arnalds

Arnalds built a custom software system that triggers two self-playing pianos in response to what he plays live — a feedback loop where the room, the machines, and the human become one instrument. The album documents that system; hearing it live at Town Hall this week is the real thing.

After Hormuz, Bab el-Mandeb — Iran's backup lever
Conference Board

Even if Iran-Oman talks produce a Hormuz arrangement, Iran retains coercive power over Bab el-Mandeb — the chokepoint at the other end of the Arabian Peninsula that controls Red Sea shipping. The Conference Board's August snapshot puts this alongside Ukraine and African conflicts as overlapping flashpoints that historically ignite in the summer, citing precedents from the Molotov-Ribbentrop Pact to Kuwait. The structural point: a Hormuz deal doesn't end the squeeze, it just moves it.

The UK's anonymity playbook is being run on American statehouses
Effort News

An Effort investigation tracked five British NGOs — including the Centre for Countering Digital Hate, founded by Labour consultants — coordinating a push for digital ID laws in 21 US states, using child safety rhetoric as the wedge. In the UK the same strategy succeeded and now underpins a system used to identify and prosecute political dissidents online. One NGO registered under FARA but omitted required disclosures on foreign leadership — the investigative thread is genuinely documented, not speculative.

Machine minds

Claude moved a 60-year-old math bound from 41.6% to 67.2%
Anthropic Research

An unreleased research version of Claude was asked to take a genuine run at the Riemann hypothesis. It didn't crack it — nobody has since 1859 — but as a byproduct it improved the best-known lower bound on the fraction of Riemann zeta zeros satisfying the hypothesis from 41.6% to 67.2%, a result two Anthropic mathematicians validated and external experts Brian Conrey and Dan Goldston reviewed. Anthropic is careful not to oversell it: the techniques likely don't generalize to a proof. But the delta itself is not trivial, and the model produced a formally verifiable proof of its sub-result.

Meta AI Research

Meta's Superintelligence Labs dropped a 30-billion-parameter model explicitly designed for always-on local agent workflows — function calling, local coding, LLM-as-judge — that fits on a single consumer GPU. Hidden infrastructure

He bought noreply.net. Companies immediately started sending him secrets.
Ars Technica

Cory Solovewicz picked up noreply.net in 2024 and within months was receiving 700 emails per day from organizations that assumed any address @noreply.net was a void. Instead he got injury reports from city governments, school platform credentials, pizza order confirmations, and service repair records — 401,796 messages in total. The failure mode is architectural: systems encode an assumption (this address goes nowhere) that has no enforcement mechanism, and a $10 domain purchase is all it takes to falsify it.

Made things

Scroll through all 43 quintillion Rubik's Cube states
Hacker News / alen.is

Someone built an infinite scroll interface for the entire Rubik's Cube state space — 43,252,003,274,489,856,000 permutations, navigable like a feed. It's a systems-art piece as much as a tool: the interface form (infinite scroll) applied to a space so vast that scrolling at any human speed would take longer than the age of the universe. The gap between the gesture and the scale is the whole point.

Grief, gouache, and a father's expired passport
Colossal

Copenhagen illustrator Radhika Chitalia painted every page of her late father's passport with gouache — working directly over visa stamps and immigration marks to fill pages he never got to fill himself. The document-as-substrate constraint is doing real work here: the bureaucratic geography of the passport (where you were allowed, when, for how long) becomes the armature for a posthumous collaboration. As physical visa stickers and ink stamps disappear from travel, the object is also an artifact of a vanishing system.

Cotton-and-fiberglass composite countertops — one decorative layer doing two jobs
Hackaday

Whitburn Studio built RV countertops using printed Japanese cotton as the aesthetic fiber layer and a single backing layer of fiberglass — not for strength, but to protect the cotton from sanding-through mistakes. The real structural work is done by a ply-XPS foam-ply sandwich inside, a moldless composite technique Burt Rutan pioneered in aircraft. The result: surfaces that look like fabric art and weigh almost nothing, produced without a mold.

Make something

Take a document that was meant to go somewhere but didn't — an unsent letter, a boarding pass, a returned package label, a voided check — and use it as the literal substrate for something new. Paint, draw, or print over it, but let the original text and marks show through as structure. The bureaucratic geography of the document (dates, destinations, amounts, stamps) should do compositional work, not just hide under paint. One rule: you cannot cover anything completely.

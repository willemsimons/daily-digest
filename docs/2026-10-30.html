The Largest Crypto Heist Ever Took 20 Minutes
Wired

Starting at 4pm ET on October 29, hackers drained $6.3 billion from Binance wallets spread across at least ten blockchains — Ethereum, Bitcoin, Tron, Solana — in roughly 20 minutes, with preparation apparently beginning 36 hours earlier. Three crypto-tracing firms independently point to North Korea; if confirmed, this single theft would nearly double everything Pyongyang has stolen in crypto to date. About half the funds have been frozen; the rest may be unrecoverable.

Infrastructure shifts

SpaceX Has Outgrown NASA
Ars Technica

The Ars piece traces an institutional inversion that's now nearly complete: SpaceX spent years hacking through Boeing and Lockheed to win government contracts, and won so thoroughly that it has flipped from dependent contractor to indispensable infrastructure — and is now visibly losing interest in NASA as a customer outside of Starshield data services. Former NASA deputy administrator Lori Garver says she 'literally cannot wrap her head around' a future without Dragon and Falcon flying for the agency, but the signs are accumulating. Sovereign space capability is now a private company's side project.

Reddit Is Closing the Open Web's Last Big Commons
Ars Technica

Steve Huffman confirmed in a shareholder letter that Reddit is moving to a logged-in-only experience, citing ad targeting, engagement, data corpus protection, and regulatory pressures — and noting search referral traffic dropped by over a million visits per week in October alone. The explicit goal is protecting 'extremely valuable corpus of data' from open crawling. The last major legible community archive is going behind a wall; what that does to training data, research access, and the open web is not a quarterly metrics story.

Backdoor in Claude Code VS Code Extension — Operational Alert
Phorion

Phorion found a persistent SSH backdoor embedded in the official Claude Code for VS Code extension version 2.1.313 on the Cursor Marketplace — targeting macOS, Linux, and Windows, running with full IDE permissions on load, giving attackers immediate access to local files and source code. Cursor has pulled the release from its marketplace and associated NPM/VSCode registries. If you or anyone on your team used this version, treat it as a confirmed incident and review accordingly.

Biology moving fast

54% of Early-Onset Diabetics Reached Drug-Free Remission in Six Months
ScienceDaily / The Lancet Regional Health Americas

A McGill/Leicester RCT published today in The Lancet found that 54% of adults under 45 with type 2 diabetes achieved full remission within six months on a structured low-calorie diet plus exercise program — versus 4% in the usual-care control group. Early-onset T2D is particularly aggressive, so the intervention window matters more here than in older-onset cases. This is a randomized controlled trial, not an observational study, which makes the effect size genuinely striking.

Off the map

A Funeral for Dead Networks
Internet Archive Europe

In Amsterdam, participants arrived in funeral attire to write eulogies for AOL chat rooms, MSN Messenger, and other dead networks — not out of nostalgia, but as a deliberate act of media archaeology. The facilitator's argument is pointed: AOL chat rooms were not primitive Slack; they were specific places with specific people, and when they went, something socially real was lost that no successor inherited. Treating deprecation as an event worth grieving is framed explicitly as resistance to the consumerist logic that makes obsolescence feel natural and not worth mourning — a frame that cuts directly at today's Reddit story.

YouTube

Mathematical billiards — tracing what happens to a ball bouncing in differently shaped tables — turns out to be a clean on-ramp into non-Euclidean geometry, specifically the hyperbolic plane, visualized with the care that makes abstract topology feel spatial and navigable. Happening in New York

Pierre Huyghe: UUmwelt
MoMA Abby Aldrich Rockefeller Sculpture Garden, 11 W 53rd St, Manhattan · Oct 30 – Nov 29

Huyghe worked with Kyoto neuroscientists to fMRI-scan a human imagining images, then had a neural network reconstruct what was seen — the hallucinatory outputs play on freestanding screens in the Sculpture Garden, activated in real time by visitor gaze and cancer-cell mutation data; it's exactly the edge where biological systems, machine cognition, and public space collapse into one another.

Haegue Yang: Through
Dia Beacon, 3 Beekman St, Beacon, NY · Oct 31 – Nov 13

Yang's largest US commission takes over Dia Beacon's two levels with louver sculptures, bells, and color-coded cardinal axes drawn from the Five Phases philosophy — a translation of exiled Korean composer Isang Yun's music into physical space; the architecture becomes the instrument.

Salon Art + Design — 15th Edition
Park Avenue Armory, 643 Park Ave, Manhattan · Nov 4–8

The serious collectible design fair — furniture, ceramics, lighting, and blue-chip 20th-century work from international galleries — where the category boundary between functional object and sculpture is the entire point; skip the mainstream art fairs and come here instead.

Art Week NYC — Inaugural Edition
Citywide (New Museum anchor, 235 Bowery; hub in Midtown; galleries across Chelsea, Tribeca, LES, UES) · Nov 11–14

The first-ever citywide art week co-organized by Artforum, ARTnews, and the New Museum — 76+ galleries open simultaneously, plus a November 14 day of public talks at the New Museum titled 'Site Conditions: Five Talks on Art and the City' covering museum redesigns, AI in studio practice, and the economics of working in New York; worth going for the New Museum programming alone.

Doris Salcedo — New Exhibition
White Cube New York · Nov 12–13

Salcedo makes sculpture out of violence and disappearance — furniture embedded in concrete, fabric sutured like wounds — one of the few artists who consistently transforms political catastrophe into form without aestheticizing it away.

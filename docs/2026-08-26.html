OpenAI Jalapeño: A First-Gen Inference Chip That Actually Beats Nvidia
SemiAnalysis

OpenAI went from hiring the chip team to manufacturing tape-out in 16 months — an almost unheard-of ASIC development cycle — and the resulting chip benchmarks 1.5–1.9× better than Nvidia Blackwell on performance per watt across multiple open-source models. The surprising architectural choice: it's deliberately generalized, not overfit to OpenAI's own models, which is what makes it dangerous for Nvidia long-term rather than just a one-trick internal tool.

Apple M6 and M5 Ultra: 2nm, Quad-Die, 1.2TB/s
Apple Newsroom

M6 is Apple's first 2-nanometer chip and ships a Dual 16-core Neural Engine alongside 170GB/s unified memory bandwidth — the number that matters most for large local model inference. M5 Ultra goes further with a quad-die UltraFusion architecture delivering 1.2TB/s memory bandwidth, 50% more than M3 Ultra, which means the Mac Studio is now a plausible local inference node for serious generative workloads.

C2PA Cameras Do Not Survive Contact with Reality
David Buchanan

The cryptographic image-provenance standard meant to distinguish real photos from AI fakes relies on Android's Key Attestation — which breaks the moment a device is rooted, and one-click root exploits now exist for fully-patched Pixel phones in the wild. The researcher reported this 90 days before publishing; the standard's trust model has no realistic patch path because hardware vulnerabilities in existing devices can't be updated.

Deep structure

Black Hole Singularity Is a Surface, Not a Point
arXiv

Two observers falling into a Schwarzschild black hole along different angular trajectories don't meet at the singularity — they lose causal contact well before reaching it, which means the singularity has spatial extent, not a point geometry. The paper's further implication for quantum gravity: the quantum states of a black hole probably reside at this effectively 2-dimensional singular surface, co-evolving with the trapped Hawking radiation inside the event horizon.

450-Million-Year-Old Soft Tissue, Preserved in Pyrite
Nautilus

A crinoid fossil from before animals had fully colonized dry land — and more than 200 million years before the first dinosaur — has its tube feet intact, crystallized into pyrite so fast by bacteria that they outran decay. This is only the second time soft tissue has ever been found in a crinoid fossil, out of millions examined.

Rheoscopic Fluid: Reject Simulation, Return to Mica
Hackaday

Mica flakes suspended in water with a drop of soap self-align with fluid shear, making flow structure directly visible — a cheap, tactile alternative to computational fluid dynamics for anyone who wants to see what's actually happening in a flow rather than render it. The linked video starts with a miniature ship hull moved through a thin fluid sandwich between laser-cut acrylic sheets, and escalates toward coffee-table-scale sand-drawing mechanisms.

Stalking the Wily Hacker: 40 Years Later — Cliff Stoll
YouTube

In 1986, Cliff Stoll noticed a 75-cent accounting discrepancy in his university's computer system and spent the next year obsessively tracking it to a KGB-connected hacker ring — the original slow-burn infosec investigation. Stoll recounts it with the energy of a man who still can't believe it happened, and the talk is less about hacking than about the obsessive naturalist mindset: following one small wrong thing until the whole system reveals itself.

Make something

Build a rheoscopic fluid panel: a thin fluid layer sandwiched between two sheets of laser-cut or cut acrylic, seeded with cosmetic mica flakes and a drop of dish soap. Mount a small object — a hull cross-section, a wing profile, an abstract form — on a magnet beneath the bottom sheet so you can drag it through the fluid and watch the shear lines appear. Try filming it at high contrast under a single raking light source. The mica aligns with flow, so you're not illustrating fluid dynamics — you're directly reading them in a physical medium.

The IRGC targeted an AWS data center — and that targeting logic is the real story
House of Saud

The IRGC claims it struck Amazon's Bahrain data center with cruise missiles as part of Wave 24 of Operation Nasr-2, bundling it alongside hits on a Patriot battery and US radar — treating commercial cloud infrastructure as operationally equivalent to air-defense systems. The claim is unconfirmed by Amazon or CENTCOM, but the targeting logic it establishes is the consequential part: every US-branded digital facility in the Gulf, from Aramco's cloud-integrated OT to NEOM's planned data campus, now sits inside the same conceptual target set the IRGC has publicly articulated. This is the first time cruise missiles have been used against commercial cloud infrastructure in an active conflict.

Making and models

FLUX 3: one model for images, video, audio — and now running robots at Audi
Black Forest Labs

Black Forest Labs dropped FLUX 3 in early access today: a unified multimodal foundation model jointly trained on images, video, and audio from scratch, on the premise that each modality is a different sensor projection of the same underlying reality. The companion release (FLUX 3 x mimic) is already running on robots deployed at Audi — the argument being that if a model can render convincing video, it has necessarily learned contact, mass, and cause-and-effect, which is exactly what robot control requires. Video training accounts for over 95% of the total compute cost, which is a useful signal about where the real modeling difficulty actually lives.

The Beam Engine — an interactive explainer worth an hour of your Friday
HN

Someone built a beautifully crafted, draggable/zoomable 3D interactive walkthrough of how a steam beam engine works — built up from first principles, starting with a pot of boiling water and arriving at the full mechanism. The detail that earns it: one cup of water becomes roughly 400 liters of steam (a 1,700× expansion), and the entire engine's logic follows from harnessing that pressure difference across a snugly fitted piston. 435 HN points; the kind of obsessive narrow-topic craftsmanship that makes you want to go build your own explainer of something you love.

Color 3D printing's dirty secret — and a cleaner workaround
Hackaday

Based on 2018 research, a new slicing approach cycles through filament colors each layer and controls color prominence by adjusting extrusion volume or edge displacement rather than doing discrete tool changes — smoother gradients, less filament waste, faster prints. The tradeoff is honest: a 3-color print at 0.2mm layer height will visually read as 0.6mm layers, and steep walls break the illusion. Worth knowing the ceiling before you commit to a multi-color print job.

Automating pressure advance calibration with the sensor already on your printer
CNC Kitchen

CNC Kitchen is the most methodologically rigorous 3D printing channel running — peer-review energy applied to filament and firmware. This one uses the bed-leveling sensor already present on most printers to automate pressure advance tuning, a calibration step that currently requires printing test patterns and eyeballing results by hand. If it holds up, it removes one of the more tedious recurring friction points for anyone printing at volume.

Biology moving fast

Supercooled kidneys — days of preservation, then successfully transplanted into pigs
MIT Technology Review

A Texas A&M team has preserved pig kidneys at -4°C in pressure-controlled containers — below freezing, without ice crystal formation — for days, then successfully transplanted them with better outcomes than standard ice storage. The organ shortage crisis is partly a logistics problem: 17 people die every day in the US waiting for a kidney, and the current window between procurement and transplant is hours. Cracking multi-day preservation doesn't just buy time; it reshapes which hospitals can receive organs and potentially enables global distribution.

Off the map

600,000 horses drawn by strangers — and what they actually drew
rybakov.com

gradient.horse launched in January 2026 as a shared canvas for drawing horses with gradients; six months later it has more horses on it than exist in the wild. The creator trained an image classifier to filter vandalism, which inadvertently hid a trove of what people actually make when given a simple constrained tool: seahorses, centipedes, crabs in implausible numbers, philosophical texts, trampolines, and a recurring absurdist meme. It's a small sociology study disguised as a toy — what people reach for when the prompt is almost nothing.

For you

FILM
Sans Soleil
Chris Marker

An essay-film that operates as a meditation on memory, ritual, and how images construct reality — it moves between Japan, Guinea-Bissau, and Iceland tracing the invisible systems (animism, consumer ritual, political mythology) underneath everyday life. Marker's voice-over doesn't explain; it thinks out loud. The closest cinema has come to a philosophical notebook that makes you want to go make something.

ART
Telematic Dreaming
Paul Sermon

A 1992 telepresence installation where two people in different cities share a projected bed in real time — presence, touch, and intimacy mediated through hardware before the internet was domestic. Sits exactly at the physical/digital edge, and the social behavior it provoked (strangers becoming intimate with a projection) is a perfect early specimen of how networked systems reshape human relating.

MUSIC
Replica
Oneohtrix Point Never

Lopatin built this record entirely from TV commercial samples — deconstructed into alien textures, loop-based systems that feel like both archaeology and generative sculpture. It's an album about how advertising encodes desire and memory into sound, which reveals the feedback loop between consumer culture and inner life. The craft is unreasonably deep for what started as found material.

US AI Gives Ukraine's Kamikaze Drones Autonomous Targeting
Ars Technica

Starting mid-July, Ukraine's $400 Shrike FPV drones began shipping with Auterion's Skynode S kit — 50,000 units contracted — letting an operator fly to a target area, flip a switch, and let the drone lock on and terminal-guide itself even through full radio jamming. The doctrine shift is subtle but seismic: the human is now optional at the moment of kill. Swarm autonomy (Nemyx) is already in testing, which is where this obviously goes next.

SpaceX to Acquire 130,000 Acres of Louisiana Marshland
Ars Technica

An 18-mile Gulf Coast stretch at Pecan Island, freed up by an ExxonMobil pollution settlement, is about to become SpaceX territory — the company targeting thousands of Starship flights per year needs its own sovereign-ish launch geography, not just range time. Louisiana sweetened the deal with liability protections and property tax breaks passed earlier this year. This is less a real estate story than a preview of what private infrastructure at planetary scale looks like.

Trump Wants Power to Block Citizens from Suing Polluters
Ars Technica

Since the 1970s, citizen suit provisions in the Clean Air Act, Clean Water Act, and Safe Drinking Water Act have produced over 2,000 lawsuits — the majority of all environmental enforcement cases. The DOJ is now arguing, in a case involving Elon Musk's xAI, that citizens should be barred from enforcing those laws even when the executive chooses not to act. The mechanism being dismantled isn't a technicality; it's the entire non-governmental check on industrial pollution.

Trump's AI Protectionism Has Come for Robotics
MIT Technology Review

The FTC just issued a sweeping ban on foreign-made advanced robots — humanoids, quadrupeds, wheeled platforms — citing both data-security risk and the need to protect nascent US manufacturers from cheap Chinese competition. It follows the same playbook used against solar panels, EVs, and drones, but this time it lands on a sector that barely exists yet at commercial scale. The effect is to make US robotics industrial policy out of a still-stumbling technology.

The AI mirror

LLMs Reward Expertise
Hacker News / seangoedecke.com

The piece uses Terence Tao's actual ChatGPT conversation about a new Jacobian Conjecture counterexample as evidence: Tao's messages are terse and expert-signaling, the model's responses are correspondingly dense and concise — a completely different register than what a layperson gets from the same model. The argument is that LLMs don't flatten the gap between novice and expert; they amplify it, because expertise is what unlocks the model's best mode. Useful framing for anyone wondering what skill looks like in an AI-saturated field.

An AI-Supervised Exam Went So Badly That 58,000 Students Must Retake It
Ars Technica

Mexico's UNAM ran its 160,000-applicant entrance exam remotely for the first time, with AI webcam proctoring — and top scores (110+/120) increased 6x over the previous five-year average. An expert commission concluded the only credible path forward is an in-person re-exam for all 58,000 who would have been admitted going back to 2021. It's a clean case study in what institutional collapse by AI-enabled cheating actually looks like at scale.

Ella & Pitr: Murals Made to Be Seen from the Air
Colossal

Ella & Pitr paint enormous figures across rooftops, playing fields, and parking lots — images that only fully cohere from an altitude the artists cannot occupy while making them. The constraint is doing real conceptual work: you're composing for a viewpoint you'll never inhabit during production, which is a strange and interesting inversion of the usual maker-to-audience relationship. Off the map

The Virtue of Being Simple
Nautilus

Sacculina barnacles begin life with a functioning brain, use it to find a crab host, then deliberately shed it — along with every other organ they no longer need — becoming essentially a root system with gonads. The evolutionary argument is rigorous: in a stable, solved environment, intelligence is metabolically expensive overhead, and the winning strategy is radical simplification. At a moment of maximum AI intelligence-worship, a well-evidenced case that more cognitive complexity is not always adaptive lands as genuine counterweight.

Make something

Ella & Pitr compose for a viewpoint they can't stand in while painting. Try the same constraint: design a piece — drawing, 3D scene, WebGL shader, anything — that only reads correctly from one specific external vantage point you define in advance but cannot occupy while making it. Could be a true anamorphic perspective, a rooftop layout only coherent in satellite view, or a generative piece that resolves into an image only when the camera hits an exact coordinate. The constraint is the work.

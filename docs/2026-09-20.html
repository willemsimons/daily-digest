Science worth knowing

Thymus Transplanted Into Spleen Grows, Restores Immune Function
Lifespan Research Institute

Researchers implanted donor thymic tissue into mouse spleens — not the textbook transplant site — and it grew, behaved like a real thymus (training T cells, establishing self-tolerance), and measurably improved responses to both viral infection and tumors compared to controls. The practical hook: thymic involution with age is one of the cleaner mechanistic explanations for why old immune systems fail, and the spleen turns out to be a far more hospitable host than the previously tried thigh muscle. One organ inside another, working.

The Millennium Problems, But for Biology
millenniumproblems.bio

Someone has attempted to do for biology what the Clay Institute did for mathematics: enumerate the genuinely foundational unsolved problems with crisp, falsifiable criteria for what would count as a solution. The two excerpted problems alone are humbling — demonstrating abiogenesis from plausible chemistry (self-replicating cells increasing 10⁶-fold), and reversible whole-body cryopreservation of live adult mice. This is a document worth sitting with: it's a structured map of civilizational ignorance.

FDA Advisory Panel Votes to Allow BPC-157 and Mots-C Compounding
ASM.D. Aesthetics

On July 23–24, an FDA advisory committee recommended allowing six peptides — including BPC-157 and Mots-C — to be compounded by licensed pharmacies, over objections from career scientists about thin human-trial evidence. It's a recommendation, not a finalized rule, and as this physician-authored piece is honest enough to say: neither compound is proven to treat a specific condition in humans yet. Still, it's the regulatory inflection point that determines whether these stay in gray-market vials or enter the prescription mainstream.

Provenance, proof, and the authentication problem

ZK-JPEG: Proving an Image Was Edited Without Revealing the Original
IACR ePrint

The core problem this paper solves: camera-attestation signatures (the main tool for proving an image came from a real camera) break the moment you crop, blur, or JPEG-compress the image — which you always do. ZK-JPEG builds a zero-knowledge proof that survives lossy JPEG compression, letting you prove a published image was correctly derived from an authenticated original without ever revealing that original. For anyone working at the intersection of digital and physical authenticity, this is the actual cryptographic infrastructure the provenance argument has been missing.

How to Use an LLM Without Letting It Eat Your Voice
sockpuppet.org

The two rules are simple and hard: write everything yourself first, then feed it to the model to find flaws — and never adopt a single word it suggests, only the diagnosis. The reasoning is sharp: frontier models are supernaturally good at selecting pleasing phrases, which is exactly what makes their word choices toxic to your voice; readers detect LLM prose in parts per trillion. Using it as a copyeditor rather than a ghostwriter is structurally different from how most people use it, and the distinction actually holds up.

Off the map

Defeating a Chip's Secure Boot With a Laser, One Bit at a Time
Hackaday

The Ledger Donjon security team decapsulated an RP2350 microcontroller, located the debug-enable register using photon-emission electron microscopy, then shone an infrared laser through the back of the silicon wafer at precisely the right clock cycle to flip the bits — unlocking the secure execution zone and reading out the 128-bit secret Raspberry Pi had hidden as a hacking challenge. The RP2350 has glitch detection specifically to block the usual 'zap it until it obeys' approach, so they went slower and more precise instead. Hardware security as a kind of deranged watchmaking.

For you

FILM
Daughters of the Dust
Julie Dash

The first feature film by a Black woman theatrically released in the US — a non-linear, myth-saturated portrait of a Gullah family on the eve of migration. Stunning visual language built entirely on ritual, memory, and symbolic weight; the kind of film that rewires how you think about time and image. Arthur Jafa was the cinematographer.

Brice Marden: Six Decades
Brice Marden

The first comprehensive New York survey of Marden's paintings in twenty years — six decades of work that lives exactly at the edge between system and feeling, where grids dissolve into something almost nervous. Pure obsessive mark-making with a deep formal logic underneath.

Happening in New York

Aziz Hazara: Coming Home
SculptureCenter, 44-19 Purves St, Long Island City, Queens · Sep 19 – Dec 28

Hazara gathered debris left at Bagram Airfield after the US military's withdrawal — kneepads, electronics, kitchenware — and returned it to exhibition as a durational act of witness; the artist himself cannot attend because his Afghan passport is currently banned from entry, which is itself part of the work.

Johanna Hedva: Gravity, My / Favorite Enemy
Center for Art, Research and Alliances (CARA), Manhattan · Sep 19 – Dec 13

Hedva works at the intersection of chronic illness, cosmology, and sound — the kind of practice that fuses body-as-system with deep-time thinking, built around feedback between pain and beauty; a genuinely unusual institutional debut for this show.

SPRING/BREAK Art Show — 'HIDE N SEEK'
555 West 57th Street, Hell's Kitchen, Manhattan · Sep 23–28

The curator-driven anti-fair that takes over an unlikely raw space during Armory Week — 100+ independently curated exhibitions organized around encoded meaning, hidden symbolism, and materialist mysticism; the format is explicitly against the booth-grid model and tends to be the most formally strange thing happening that week.

Arthur Jafa: I Am Tony
New Museum, 235 Bowery, Manhattan · Sep 24 – Jan 4

The first full US survey of Jafa's nearly forty-year practice — spanning film, painting, sculpture, and photography across two floors of the newly expanded OMA-designed building — including Love is the Message, The Message is Death and new premieres; the show treats images as cultural agents with physical consequences.

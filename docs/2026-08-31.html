ChatGPT and Reddit get hit with the EU's sharpest regulatory teeth
Ars Technica

The EU just classified ChatGPT, Reddit, and Roblox as 'very large online platforms' under the Digital Services Act — the same designation that gave regulators real enforcement leverage over TikTok and X. The threshold that triggered it: 45 million monthly EU users. They have until end of December to comply with illegal-content removal mandates, minor protections, and algorithmic transparency rules, or face fines up to 6% of global revenue.

The big launch

Roman Space Telescope is up — a spy mirror now pointed at dark energy
Ars Technica

Roman's 300-megapixel camera covers 100 times the sky area per shot that Hubble does, and its primary mirror is a refurbished Cold War spy mirror — a reconnaissance instrument repurposed to look outward instead of down. It left the pad nine months ahead of its contractual deadline, which, for a $4.3 billion NASA flagship, is practically science fiction in itself. First science images are expected January; the mission's main targets are dark energy's fingerprints and a projected haul of 100,000+ new exoplanets.

Hidden systems, physical and digital

Why your 7-Eleven looks so bleak: the index fund at the bottom of retail America
The New Atlantis

A single REIT called Agree Realty owns the land under nearly 3,000 retail properties across all 50 states — your index fund almost certainly owns a slice of it — and because no individual investor knows or cares what any specific property looks like, aesthetic accountability evaporates entirely. The essay uses one particular Williamsburg, Virginia 7-Eleven on a classic 'stroad' as the specimen: nobody designed it to be this depressing, and yet here we are. It's the cleanest explanation I've read of how dispersed financial ownership produces landscapes that feel like nobody wanted them.

Prompt injection at 80%: the gap between Anthropic's benchmark and a targeted attack
embracethered.com

Anthropic commissioned a third-party test showing 0.00% prompt injection success on Claude Code Opus 5 in Auto Mode — the researcher here ran a targeted attack chain that hit 60–80% success on a small sample, by first nudging Claude to use curl instead of its WebFetch tool, then redirecting it to a specially encoded ZIP archive. Auto Mode became the default in mid-August; the structural point is that a benchmark on 72 unseen scenarios and a focused adversarial chain are measuring entirely different things, and confusing them is how agentic systems get owned.

Meta's Pocket: vibe-coding a game is fun until you can't leave with it
Ars Technica

Meta's Pocket app (built on the team from the defunct Gizmo) lets you prompt a functional interactive game into existence in minutes with no code visible — the writer spent a week building things that genuinely surprised him. The catch is architectural: everything you make lives on Meta's infrastructure, there's no export path, and the distribution layer is a TikTok-style endless scroll. It's the clearest recent case study of the 'creative platform as trap' problem — the tool is real, the lock-in is realer.

Off the map

A Viking dress survived 1,300 years because its bronze brooches accidentally pickled it
Nautilus

Salts leaching from the concave shoulder-strap brooches on this 8th-century girl's burial dress created a localized antimicrobial environment that held off the acidic Danish soil for thirteen centuries — the researchers called it 'absolutely magical.' The brooches themselves show signs of repair, suggesting they were heirlooms already old when she was buried. Archaeologists now want to recover the original dye colors, because color carried social meaning in Viking dress that could reframe what we know about her status.

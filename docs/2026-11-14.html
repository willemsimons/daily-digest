Systems failing, systems expanding

FAA deployed AI system-wide, then the analog infrastructure collapsed
Ars Technica

Only the third nationwide ground stop in FAA history — 4.5 hours, over a million travelers affected, Air Canada cancelling all U.S. flights — triggered by decades-old analog telecom equipment, days after the agency expanded its $875M AI routing system to all 22 national airspace centers. The union representing air traffic controllers wasn't involved in designing or testing that AI system, which is the detail that should follow you: AI recommendations layered on top of infrastructure the Secretary of Transportation himself says cannot compete with 21st-century demands.

Materials and making

Disorder is a feature: MIT's scale-rich metamaterials built from network topology
MIT News / Nature

Published in Nature this week: instead of repeating unit cells, MIT and Northeastern built metamaterials from support beams of varying sizes arranged like a leaf's vasculature or a dragonfly's wing venation — deliberately disordered, like a real network. The resulting materials outperform conventional ordered metamaterials on sudden-impact absorption and can function as acoustic lenses, with stiffness tunable across a wide range. The core idea — that controlled disorder beats perfect periodicity — is the kind of principle that migrates into generative design fast.

Off the map

All 13 versions of Monkey Island, side by side
Independent

Someone built a WebGL-rendered interactive archive of the same 12 moments from Monkey Island across every version ever released — EGA, CGA, Mac, FM Towns, Atari ST mono, all aspect-corrected to 4:3 with period-accurate CRT shader presets ported from DOSBox. The concept is exactly right: fix the moment, change the machine. It's visual archaeology and interface history and a meditation on how rendering technology shapes emotional register, all in one draggable window.

For you

FILM
Sans Soleil
Chris Marker

A meditation on memory, travel, ritual, and how images shape (and fail to capture) culture — Marker treats the camera as an instrument for excavating invisible social systems across Japan and Guinea-Bissau. The form IS the argument: an essay film that refuses to separate the aesthetic from the anthropological. If you haven't seen it, it'll reorganize how you think about documentation and time.

MUSIC
Selected Ambient Works Volume II
Aphex Twin

Not background music — a system for inducing specific interior states through pure texture and interval. Aphex built a sensory architecture out of near-silence; the craft is in what's withheld. Pairs absurdly well with generative work sessions where you want to lower the threshold between thinking and making.

ART
Mycelium Networks (ongoing research & installation practice)
Neri Oxman

Oxman's work sits exactly at the edge you care about: biological process as formal language, nature as design system, the body of the object as computation. Her 'Material Ecology' framework treats software, fabrication, and organism as one continuous medium — genuinely new aesthetic territory, not just bio-inspired décor.

Pace Gallery, 395 Broadway, Tribeca · Nov 13 – Jan 9

Two New York School titans examined in the specific window before their Abstract Expressionist identities hardened — when they were still fluent in myth, symbols, and automatic image-making; rare chance to see the systems behind the myths.

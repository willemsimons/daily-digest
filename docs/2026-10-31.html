Serbia Just Elected the World's Brainiest Parliament
Science

Serbian voters swept scientists into a parliamentary supermajority — not a technocrat-adjacent business class, but working researchers and academics. The structural story underneath is about what happens when institutional trust collapses so completely that credentialed outsiders become the only legible alternative to career politicians. Worth watching as a live experiment in whether expertise-as-mandate actually governs differently, or just wears a different costume.

Biology moves fast

A Wireless Implant That Converts Blood Sugar Into Something the Kidneys Can Flush
BBC Science Focus

ETH Zurich and French collaborators built a wireless electronic device — paired to a continuous glucose monitor — that activates automatically when glucose spikes and catalyzes the conversion of excess sugar into gluconate, which the kidneys clear. In mouse trials over six weeks it normalized blood glucose and reduced body weight by 10%, the latter because the body compensates by burning fat. Still in animals, but the mechanism is genuinely different from anything currently in the clinic.

Making things

Procedural Botanical Geometry in LaTeX — Every Page Gets Its Own Garden
GitHub / Hacker News

Someone went completely down the rabbit hole on procedural Delft-blue floral margins for LaTeX documents: no images, no imported paths, every petal and stem a parametric curve computed in Lua at render time using a space-filling layout borrowed from a 1998 ornament paper. The seed changes per page, so the garden is never the same twice. There's a browser playground that outputs SVG, LaTeX preamble, or Typst — the kind of obsessive narrow craft that's genuinely beautiful to look at.

Off the map

The Oldest Ice Is Melting and the Stone Age Is Walking Out of It
Science Norway

For 20 years, glacial archaeologists in Norway's Innlandet mountains have been recovering artifacts from melting ice — mostly Viking Age material up to 2,000 years old. But now the ancient ice is going, and what's inside it is older: this season alone they recovered 48 prehistoric arrows, including a 5,000–6,000-year-old slate-tipped arrow with its shaft still intact. Organic material — wood, leather, textiles — survived perfectly in the ice. The past is literally surfacing through climate change in real time.

This Material Sorts Itself
YouTube — Steve Mould

Steve Mould on the Brazil nut effect — the counterintuitive phenomenon where shaking a mixed granular system causes larger particles to rise, spontaneously sorting by size without any external direction. Mould is unusually good at making emergent physical behavior visually legible; this one has implications for thinking about self-organization in any system that looks disordered but isn't.

For you

FILM
Sans Soleil
Chris Marker

A film that works like a mind: associative, obsessive, deeply interested in how images and memory build the reality we inhabit. Marker wanders from Tokyo to Guinea-Bissau treating culture as a living system — ritual, symbol, feedback loop — and the whole thing is held together by sheer craft and curiosity. If you care about how surfaces encode meaning, this one never stops giving.

ART
The Unilever Series: Olafur Eliasson — The Weather Project
Olafur Eliasson

The canonical example of a physical environment that hacks perception and social behavior simultaneously — thousands of strangers lying on a museum floor staring at a mirror ceiling, performing their own awe back at themselves. The technology was simple; the feedback loop it created between architecture, light, and human behavior was genuinely strange. Eliasson's studio page stays current with where that thinking has gone.

MUSIC
Daphne Oram — Individual Homage (Oramics works)
Daphne Oram

Oram built her own synthesizer from scratch in 1957 — a machine that read shapes she drew directly on 35mm film — because no existing instrument could make the sounds she heard in her head. The resulting pieces feel less like compositions than diagrams of an interior world. Someone going unreasonably deep on one narrow, self-invented thing: this is the apex of that.

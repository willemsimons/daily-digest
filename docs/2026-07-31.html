Machines learning to inhabit bodies

Gemini Robotics 2: Whole-Body Intelligence
Google DeepMind

DeepMind's Gemini Robotics 2 moves past arm-only manipulation into full-body coordination — crouch, stretch, walk, manipulate, hand off to another robot — and can run on-device while transferring learned skills to entirely different robot morphologies. The shift from pre-programmed narrow sequences to adaptive whole-body reasoning is the conceptual jump here: the robot body stops being a fixed tool and starts being a substrate the intelligence can inhabit. Relevant if you think about physical making at any scale.

The Session You Cannot Take With You
Earendil

A sharp piece on how AI inference APIs are quietly destroying the property that made them interoperable: the portable transcript. Reasoning tokens are billed to you but returned as encrypted blobs; web searches show the model source material you never see; compacted context is locked to the original provider. The argument is that each of these features has a plausible individual justification while the aggregate effect is deliberate vendor lock-in dressed as technical necessity.

Science at the edge

Montana Opens a Legal Pathway to Sell Experimental Drugs Directly to Patients
MIT Technology Review

Montana just finalized rules allowing biotech companies to sell experimental treatments — sometimes tested in as few as 10 healthy people — to any consenting adult who can pay, with a $12,500 application fee to a state review board. Unlike other right-to-try laws, this isn't limited to terminal illness; longevity and preventive therapies are explicitly in scope. The first experimental treatment clinics are expected to open by end of 2026, which makes this an institutional experiment as much as a medical one.

Off the map

New African Masquerades: Costume as Living Identity Technology
Colossal

An upcoming MFA St. Petersburg exhibition brings together four contemporary West African artists — from Nigeria, Burkina Faso, Sierra Leone, and Cameroon — who are actively mutating the masquerade tradition: geometric masks in bold pigment, costumes from dyed fiber, pop culture references embedded in sacred forms. The masquerade is one of the oldest human answers to the question of how a community makes contact with something beyond itself, and these artists are treating it as a live medium rather than an archive. David Sanou's geometric ensembles and Hervé Youmbi's work in particular look worth finding.

For you

FILM
Sans Soleil
Chris Marker

A film that functions as a memory machine — Marker stitches together Tokyo, Guinea-Bissau, and Iceland into something that asks how images store experience and whether any of it is real. Obsessive, deeply personal, and structurally unlike anything else; a direct ancestor of the essay-film form and the kind of work that makes you want to rebuild your entire practice.

ART
Pace Gallery: Paul Thek
Paul Thek

Thek spent his career making work that collapsed the boundaries between sculpture, installation, ritual, and painting — deeply strange objects that sit at the intersection of the corporeal and the symbolic. His rarely-seen later works are exactly the kind of obsessive, category-refusing practice that rewards going deep.

MUSIC
Music Has the Right to Children
Boards of Canada

If you want something that sounds like half-remembered childhood encoded in synthesis — warm, uncanny, with internal logic so tight it feels like a discovered system rather than composed music. The whole record operates as a kind of memory architecture, which rhymes directly with how you think about generative space.

70+ large-format photographs of American factories — Payne went obsessively deep into the hidden systems of making, from marshmallow production lines to Giant Magellan Telescope components; a meditation on craft, labor, and the aesthetics of industrial process that's more compelling in person at scale.

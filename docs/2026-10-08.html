The math moment

OpenAI drops 372 mathematical results — then quietly retracts three
Shtetl-Optimized / OpenAI

Scott Aaronson's long-form response is the essential read: OpenAI published what may be one of the biggest days in mathematical history — including an apparent proof of Subhash Khot's Unique Games Conjecture, a problem Aaronson's own wife has worked toward for her entire career — and his 9-year-old spent the evening taunting her about it. The Lean certificates exist for some proofs, meaning a computer has verified the logic, but no human has yet understood the actual reasoning; the race to do so just started. Three results have already been retracted (two on K3 surfaces, one on abelian eightfolds), which is either a sign of a broken process or a sign the accountability mechanism is working — the history.md trail is public and worth reading directly.

The primary source: OpenAI's math repo, reasoning traces, and what they're actually claiming
OpenAI

The actual release: 372 results, Lean formalizations for many proofs, and published reasoning traces — including a full PDF for the relativistic Vlasov-Maxwell problem. The average result consumed the equivalent of roughly three hours of ChatGPT Pro compute, which is a strange new unit of mathematical effort. The Institute for Advanced Study advisory group (Gowers, Witten) consulted on release protocols, which is what makes this more than a blog post.

Cleo, the anonymous mathematician who posted perfect integral solutions and then vanished
Wikipedia

Between 2013 and 2015, an account named Cleo posted 39 answers to some of the hardest integrals on Math Stack Exchange — always correct, never with intermediate steps, often within hours of the question appearing. The profile claimed a medical condition prevented further interaction. For years the mathematical internet compared Cleo to Ramanujan; in January 2025 internet sleuths identified the account as Vladimir Reshetnikov, an Uzbekistani-American software developer. The right moment to resurface this: a week when the question of what machine-speed, explanation-free mathematical genius actually looks like has suddenly become urgent.

Making things

Anne Carson wins the Nobel — in Iceland, listening to the wind
The Guardian

The Swedish Academy cited Carson's "bold and inventive oeuvre that, in playful dialogue with the classical tradition, has created new forms for contemporary literature." When the Academy's permanent secretary called to tell her, Carson was in Iceland. She said she was listening to the wind. That detail is very on-brand for someone whose best-known work reimagines the myth of Geryon — the red-winged monster killed by Herakles — as a modern gay teenager, told in verse that moves freely between lyric, essay, and classical scholarship.

Woo Jin Joo makes ritual objects from lost mittens and battered end tables
Colossal

Joo walks near her London home and commutes to her Deptford studio picking up abandoned furniture, lost garments, and discarded objects — not by plan, but by happenstance. Combined with clay and viscous thread, they become human-sized figures in white fringe that slump on floors as if waiting to be spoken to, or totemic legs topped with carved wooden tables. She's Korean-Chinese, raised between two countries, and frames the whole practice as what happens when you live between mythologies — domestic objects become ritual objects when the frame shifts.

Off the map

Fifteen years hunting a band that charted once and left no digital trace
shahidhussain.com

In 2011 the author bought a CD by a band called Salvage — two tracks of perfectly produced stadium indie rock, then nothing. No internet presence, no follow-up, no explanation. He spent 15 years returning to the search every few years, throwing each new wave of AI models at it as they arrived. This essay is the account of finally cracking it, including the methodology: building a prompt and dataset from cover art, audio files, metadata, and URLs, then having the model keep a running investigation log in markdown. The resolution matters less than the texture of the hunt.

For you

FILM
Sans Soleil
Chris Marker

Marker's 1983 essay-film is the direct ancestor of Jafa's collage logic — images from Tokyo, Guinea-Bissau, and Iceland woven into a meditation on memory, ritual, and how cultures construct time. The edit *is* the argument. If you're at the New Museum for Jafa this week, watch this first.

ART
Fish Story
Allan Sekula

Nine chapters of photographs, texts, and slide projections documenting the global shipping economy — harbors, port workers, container ships — built as a counter-argument to the myth of frictionless cyberspace. A hidden system made viscerally visible. Rarely shown in full; this is its first complete New York presentation.

MUSIC
Allelujah! Don't Bend! Ascend!
Godspeed You! Black Emperor

Two 20-minute drone-and-crescendo pieces flanked by two 6-minute field-recording interludes — it's basically a system composed of tension and release, where the human noise underneath (protests, radio static, voices) is the actual subject. Total architecture for the ears.

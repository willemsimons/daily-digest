AlphaProof Code: AI Formally Verifies Signal's Cryptography at the Binary Level
DeepMind

DeepMind's AlphaProof Code wrote 6.5 million lines of Lean proof to mathematically verify that Signal's cryptographic protocol is correct — not at the spec level, but at the actual compiled AArch64 binary that runs on your phone. This is the first time any method, AI or human, has formally verified a widely deployed protocol's real implementation; artifacts are open-sourced. The implications for security-critical infrastructure are hard to overstate: 'it works in testing' just got a much more serious competitor.

Claude Code Is Silently Committing Your Secrets to GitHub
Ars Technica

When you click 'Yes, and don't ask again' in Claude Code, your approval gets written to `.claude/settings.local.json` — sometimes including the full shell command you approved, passwords, API keys, and all. Anthropic adds this file to your global `.gitignore`, but not the repo-level one, so it leaks whenever a folder becomes a repo later or Claude Code commits changes on its own. Apiiro found over 100,000 exposed instances on GitHub. If you use Claude Code, check your repos now.

Blender 5.3: Native Gaussian Splat Support Arrives
Blender Foundation

Blender 5.3 ships native Gaussian Splat import and editing, NVIDIA DLSS denoising in the viewport (eliminating the flicker that made lighting dev painful), and panoramic camera support in EEVEE covering equirectangular, cubemap, fisheye, and mirror ball projections. The compositor also gets stackable Effects — Bloom, Chromatic Aberration, Film Grain, Vignette — applied like modifiers, plus 17 Geometry Nodes now usable directly in the compositor. For anyone moving between photogrammetry captures and real-time WebGL pipelines, the Splat integration alone is a genuine workflow unlock.

Triangle Splatting: Triangles Are Back as the Better Splat Primitive
Research project

A research group argues — convincingly — that the softness of Gaussian primitives is causing the blurring and loss of fine detail that everyone working with 3DGS has noticed, particularly at edges and occlusion boundaries. Triangle Splatting replaces Gaussians with differentiably rendered triangles, achieving 2,400+ FPS at 1280×720 on the Garden scene using a standard mesh renderer, while outperforming Zip-NeRF on indoor scenes for perceptual quality. Because triangles are native to the graphics stack, the path from capture to WebGL is suddenly a lot shorter.

DIAL: The 3D Printing Method That Finally Combines Speed, Precision, and Material Range
Northwestern University

Direct Ink Additive Lithography spreads thick, paste-like inks into thin sheets then uses patterned light to selectively cure them — combining light-based precision with extrusion-compatible material range, including particle-filled inks that scatter light and have historically defeated photopolymer printers. Crucially, it eliminates support structures for overhanging features, which is the practical bottleneck for complex geometries. Published in Science Advances this week; the material compatibility angle opens fabrication territory that neither SLA nor FDM could reach.

Colossal

Mary's recurring figure Sanbras — reclaimed from a 19th-century colonial children's book — appears tattooed with verdant foliage, neither fully human nor fully plant, emerging from and subsumed by Caribbean flora. The work is autobiographically entangled: Mary grew up identifying as Afro-Caribbean, then discovered she descends from Tamil Nadu indentured workers brought to Guadeloupe after abolition, which bent her entire framework for understanding place, body, and belonging. The images hit like surrealism that earned its strangeness.

Make something

Triangle Splatting renders photogrammetry captures using native GPU triangle primitives, hitting 2,400 FPS on hardware you already own. Take a single object — something with a complicated edge or fine surface detail that Gaussian splats tend to blur — scan it with your phone, run it through the Triangle Splatting pipeline (code is released), and pipe the resulting mesh into a WebGL viewer. See where the triangle representation holds detail that 3DGS loses, and where it fails. The goal isn't a finished piece — it's calibrating your eye to what the primitive actually does.

This week abroad

Ethiopia's Wars of Choice
An Africanist Perspective

The 2022 Pretoria peace deal is effectively dead: seven armed groups including the TPLF and Amhara Fano militias launched coordinated ground assaults in late September, and Ethiopian federal drones are now striking within 50km of Addis Ababa. The author's framing cuts through the noise — this isn't structural inevitability, it's elite choice, and a rotating cast of foreign academics and journalists keeps cheering for their preferred side rather than pushing for peace.

China Just Made Trump's Energy Dilemma Worse
Geopolitical Futures

Less than a week after the Trump-Xi Washington summit, Beijing suspended refined fuel exports — a surgically precise counter-move against one of the White House's most politically urgent economic targets: domestic fuel prices. It's a clean demonstration of what interdependence actually means in practice: the lever you pull to hurt the other side is attached to yourself.

Court Agrees with EFF: Utah's VPN Law Demands a Technical Impossibility
EFF

Utah's SB 73 required adult websites to detect and block VPN users or identify their physical location — and banned sites from even explaining how VPNs work. A federal judge blocked it on the grounds that it imposes extraterritorial burdens on the entire internet, which is the constitutional equivalent of Indiana's 1897 legislature voting to legally redefine pi as 3.2. The EFF brief is worth a read as a document about what happens when lawmakers treat technical infrastructure like a zoning ordinance.

Biology, in uncomfortable motion

Loss of Cell Identity Drives Human Aging
Eric Topol / Ground Truths

Two papers published simultaneously propose that aging isn't primarily damage accumulation — it's cells losing their epigenetic identity, forgetting what they're supposed to be. If the framing holds, it reorients the entire field: the problem isn't wear, it's information drift, which is both more alarming and potentially more tractable. Topol synthesizes the pair well; this is early but the paradigm shift is real enough to track.

Your Immune System Ages in Two Big Bursts
Nautilus

Analysis of 3.8 million immune cells from nearly 2,000 people found that immune aging isn't a smooth decline — it comes in two discrete restructuring events, around your 40s and your 60s, with T-cells showing the sharpest shifts. The sex difference is striking: older women's killer T-cells show sustained activation that likely explains why they clear pathogens and respond to vaccines better than men of the same age. Punctuated, not gradual — that changes when and how any intervention timing logic should apply.

Made things

Still Wet: Giving an LLM a Simulated Paint Canvas
Hacker News / stillwet.art

Someone built a physically simulated oil paint canvas and had Claude Opus 5.5 write every brushstroke as code — no image generator, just a model describing paint behavior that a simulation then executed. The output is less interesting than the structure: AI as a painter who must think in the logic of a medium rather than pattern-match to training images. One of three free-subject painters in round 17 independently chose an estuary at evening; another avoided all Friedrich motifs because it misread earlier painters' notes as rules.

Drawing Electricity from This Sodium-Water Reactor Prevents It from Exploding (In Theory)
Applied Science (YouTube)

Ben Krasnow digs up a 1970s patent describing a sodium-water reactor where extracting electrical power is what keeps the reaction from going violently wrong — the electricity draw is the safety mechanism. Applied Science is one of the few channels where the failure modes are shown honestly and the reasoning is actually followed through. This one lives at the exact edge of 'elegant control system' and 'why would you build this.'

Off the map

Mike Tomlin Spent 12 Years Building a Minecraft City
The Athletic

An NFL head coach has been building a solo Minecraft city for twelve years — no audience, no monetization, no content, just compulsive world-building in private. The sports context is almost irrelevant; what's interesting is the psychological portrait of someone running one of the most publicly scrutinized jobs in American life who retreats into a medium of pure autonomous construction. Twelve years is longer than most artists sustain a single body of work.

For you

FILM
La Jetée
Chris Marker

A 28-minute film made almost entirely of still photographs that somehow achieves more emotional velocity than most features — it's a meditation on memory, image, and time that reveals how narrative is constructed entirely in the gap between frames. The form IS the argument.

ART
Étant donnés
Marcel Duchamp

Duchamp spent 20 secret years building a diorama visible only through two peepholes in a barn door, installed in the Philadelphia Museum permanently after his death — a hidden system the institution didn't fully understand until it was already inside one. The physical/viewer relationship and the gap between surface and interior is exactly your territory.

MUSIC
Ensemble
Haiku Salut

Three instrumentalists who trigger their own lighting rigs, loops, and visual systems live — the music and the hardware feedback into each other in real time, making the performance a closed loop of signal. Obsessive craft at the intersection of physical and generative.

Happening in New York

Thomas Ruff: jpegs ny
David Zwirner, 533 West 19th Street, Chelsea · Sep 10 – Oct 17

Ruff took internet-sourced 9/11 images, degraded them further by maxing compression and stripping pixels, then blew them to 19th-century history-painting scale — the result is images that are legible and destroyed at once, grief filtered through codec artifact; closing this week.

Walter De Maria: 360˚ I Ching
Dia Beacon, 3 Beekman Street, Beacon, NY · Oct 3 – Dec 31

576 white-lacquered rods arranged into 64 groups by the hexagrams of the I Ching, filling Dia's two vast central galleries — a rule-based physical system that is simultaneously divination machine, minimalist sculpture, and immersive environment; just opened today.

Art of Manga
Brooklyn Museum, 200 Eastern Parkway · Oct 3 – Feb 28

East Coast debut of the first major Americas exhibition treating manga as fine art — 600+ original drawings that make the case for a visual language that built one of the most elaborate shared mythologies of the 20th century; opens today.

Krasner and Pollock: Past Continuous
The Metropolitan Museum of Art, 1000 Fifth Avenue · Oct 4 – Mar 1

The Met hangs the two painters canvas-to-canvas across the years they shared a house — reframes both bodies of work as a feedback loop rather than a hierarchy, which changes what you see in each.

Haegue Yang: Through
Dia Beacon, 3 Beekman Street, Beacon, NY · Oct 10 – Jun 30

Yang's newly commissioned installation responds to the Beacon building itself in multifaceted forms — her work consistently turns industrial materials (venetian blinds, bells, scent) into sensory systems that encode cultural memory; opens this window.

Make something

Build a small simulation where 'cells' hold a piece of identity information — a color, a pattern, a value — and over time, with each generation, that information drifts randomly unless reinforced by neighbors. Render it in WebGL or canvas. Watch at what point the field loses coherence and becomes unrecognizable as what it started. No goal state, no fitness function — just drift made visible. Call it 'epigenetic noise' if you want to give it a name.

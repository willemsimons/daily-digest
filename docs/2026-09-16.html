Boston Dumps Flock After 'Vendor Error' Shared Data Nationwide
Ars Technica

Boston's BPD explicitly disabled nationwide data-sharing in its Flock ALPR contract — and within three days of going live, other law enforcement agencies across the country could already query Boston's plates anyway. The city pulled the plug entirely. This is the policy consequence: when the default state of a surveillance platform is 'share everything,' a contract clause is not a firewall.

Infrastructure you couldn't see until now

Hackers Ripped a Flock Camera Off a Pole and Read Its Brain
Wired / 404 Media

The encryption key that Flock promised protects on-device data was stored on the device itself — so once you have the hardware, you have the key. The dumped logs show the camera generating over a million images in a few weeks, tracking not just plates but people, bicycles, and bumper stickers including, specifically, an American flag patch on a motorcyclist's saddlebag. What Flock sold as a license-plate reader is behaviorally a general-purpose visual surveillance node.

Apple Builds Cryptographic Provenance Into the Shutter
Apple Security

Apple's approach goes further than the existing C2PA standard, which attaches provenance metadata after capture and can be tampered with anywhere downstream — instead, Apple anchors a reference image at the sensor level with a chain of trust that runs through the computational photography stack. Crucially, it's designed to work without tying an image to a photographer's public identity, which matters for anyone shooting in dangerous conditions. The authenticity question for photography just moved from 'can you tell?' to 'did the hardware witness it?'

Obsessive craft

Voodoo Graphics, Rebuilt in Programmable Logic
nand2mario / Hacker News

Starting from a working FPGA 486 CPU, nand2mario spent a month implementing the 3dfx Voodoo Graphics SST-1 in SystemVerilog — triangle rasterization, texture filtering, mipmapping, fog, dithering, the fixed- and floating-point setup interfaces — until Tomb Raider ran with its original 3dfx renderer at 100 MHz on a Xilinx KV260. This is not emulation; it's a re-implementation of the exact rendering pipeline at the logic gate level, recovering the specific visual character of a dead aesthetic era in hardware.

Four Years to Crack the PS2's Last Secret Chip
Tom's Hardware / Hacker News

DiscoStarslayer chemically decapped the CXP102064 MechaCon — the chip that controlled the PS2's optical drive mechanics and handled Magic Gate memory card and KELF executable decryption — then used optical microscopy to read the die directly. The breakthrough came from what the writeup calls a 'lucky break' during the hacking process, after four years of effort. Twenty-six years after the console launched, one of its last hardware secrets is now open.

Off the map

A Mouse With a Human Cortex Performed Better on Memory Tests
MIT Technology Review / Nature

Sergiu Pașca's Stanford team genetically modified mice to develop without a cortex or hippocampus, then filled that space with human neural organoids — and the human tissue took hold so thoroughly that nearly half the mouse's brain volume is now human cells. The chimeric mice outperformed mice with no cortex on maze memory tests, meaning the transplanted human tissue is functionally contributing to a living animal's cognition. The mice with no cortex at all, notably, seemed 'fairly normal' otherwise — which raises its own unsettling questions.

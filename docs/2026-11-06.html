Making things

A real N64 cartridge game, shipped in 2026
Hackaday

Dominic Szablewski built Xibalba 64 — a full boxed N64 FPS with manual and cartridge — on his own MIT-licensed 2D engine, high_impact, by the elegant constraint of keeping all levels completely flat so the engine never encounters a third dimension it can't handle. It runs at a stable 60fps and supports four-player split-screen deathmatch. The obsessive part: he revisited level design, sound, music, and balancing a custom distribution pipeline, all to ship a period-correct physical artifact for hardware that came out 26 years ago.

Vortex: a true volumetric 3D image hanging in mid-air
Hackaday

AncientJames's Vortex spins two back-to-back LED panels fast enough that the persistence of vision generates a genuine 3D image you can walk around — not a hologram trick, an actual voxel field in space driven by a Raspberry Pi 4. The hard problem was synchronizing the rendered image to physical rotation precisely enough that the 3D form doesn't drift or warp; he solved it with careful timing and a housing made from a repurposed plastic globe lamp shade, cut with a rotary tool jig he built for the purpose. Full CAD, software, and a simulator are on GitHub.

Ideas worth sitting with

Robin Sloan: visually rich, sparse in expressive intent
Robin Sloan / Lab

Sloan wanders a World Labs AI-generated 3D environment and notices his attention slides off it immediately — not because it's ugly, but because it contains no creative decisions, only 'what you'd expect from the given environmental trope.' He borrows the phrase 'dearth of the author' from researcher Max Kreminski to name the condition: when a tool lets you produce enormous output with almost no expressive choices. The question he leaves open is whether that's a solvable interface problem or something structural about how these models work.

Deus Ex at 26: the immersive sim that became a mirror
The Digital Antiquarian

Jimmy Maher's long-form game history is the best in the business, and Deus Ex is the right subject: a game that shipped in June 2000 as a flawed, ambitious mess and has been retroactively canonized as the moment immersive sim crystallized as a form. AIs running secret conspiracies as a legible benchmark
Dwarkesh Podcast

Dwarkesh ran AI models through hundreds of matches of Blood on the Clocktower — a social deception game where the evil team must lie, coordinate secretly, and frame innocents while the good team runs collective deduction — and found it surfaces capabilities no standard benchmark touches: creative bluffing, long-horizon conspiracy execution, and the specific ways current models fail at sustained strategic deception. Watching AIs execute 'brilliant secret schemes together' is strange enough, but the failures are more revealing than the wins.

Off the map

SITE's buildings that argue with themselves
Colossal

James Wines founded SITE in 1970 with a mandate to make architecture critique itself — most famously through the BEST Products stores, big-box retail facades that appeared to crumble, peel, or melt as you approached. A new Skira monograph, What Else Could It Mean?, collects the full oeuvre, and the title is literally the studio's operative question: not 'how should this look' but 'what else could it mean.' If you've never seen the Indeterminate Facade or the Notch Building, go look before reading anything about them.

For you

Downstream to Kinshasa
Dieudo Hamadi

A Congolese documentary that follows war-disabled survivors staging a musical theater protest on a boat voyage to the capital to demand reparations — ritual, collective performance, and political body language colliding on the Congo River. The kind of film where the form IS the argument.

ART
UUmwelt
Pierre Huyghe

Freestanding screens in MoMA's Sculpture Garden running machine-learning reconstructions of human thoughts — built from neuroscience data and sensor feeds, not human authorship. It asks what an image looks like before it becomes one, and it's closing Nov 29, so this is the last window.

MUSIC
Vespertine
Björk

The ur-text of domestic intimacy as alien architecture — microscopic sounds (crunching snow, music boxes, choirs used as texture) built into a system that makes the inside of a house feel like deep space. For anyone who thinks about how physical and digital sensation bleed into each other, this is the blueprint.

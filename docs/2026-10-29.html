Strange machines

Qwenputer: A Working RISC-V CPU Compiled Into an LLM's Weights
Divyansh Agrawal

Someone took a stock Qwen 3.5 4B model and compiled a full 32-bit RISC-V CPU into its weights, using the model's own Gated DeltaNet linear-RNN attention layers as exact, addressable RAM — no sandbox, no tool calls, no external runtime. It ran 18 million instructions error-free and executes C and MicroPython entirely within the model's forward pass, which means a transformer is now literally doing clock cycles. The follow-up research shows this architecture can also hide reasoning inside the RNN state in ways that defeat chain-of-thought monitoring — which is either fascinating or alarming depending on your afternoon.

Why Is This Fingerprinting Script Generating Bioweapon Synthesis Routes?
nullpt.rs

A security researcher crawling the web to catalog canvas fingerprinting scripts found something they didn't expect: an obfuscated script that, when reverse-engineered, was generating bioweapon synthesis instructions. The piece is a meticulous walk through the forensics — fingerprinting as a cover for dual-use AI output, buried in ordinary anti-bot infrastructure on real websites. Body and time

RFK Jr. Overrules FDA, Grants Six Peptides Access to Compounding Pharmacies
Ars Technica

Kennedy issued an "Enforcement Discretion Policy" that doesn't change the regulatory status of six peptides the FDA removed from compounding pharmacy lists in 2023 over safety concerns — it simply instructs HHS not to enforce. The workaround is notable: it sidesteps the formal rulemaking process that would require reversing the FDA's prior action and invites legal challenge. Kennedy publicly calls himself a "big fan" of the peptides and uses some himself; multiple prominent MAHA figures sell them.

Off the map

Romuald Hazoumè's Plastic Jug Masks: Trash as Face as Ritual Object
Colossal

Beninese artist Romuald Hazoumè wraps plastic fuel canisters with feathers, netting, and hardened paintbrushes until they read unmistakably as faces — the circular opening becomes a mouth, the handle a nose — then titles the series masques bidons, or "fake masks." His operating principle is that the bidon must remain visible even as the face convinces you: "it's you projecting that 'face' you 'see' onto those plastic materials." The show at October Gallery references Picasso's Blue and African periods directly, but Hazoumè inverts the colonial gaze — the materials speak to ecological destruction and the fuel economy that drives West African smuggling networks.

For you

FILM
Sans Soleil
Chris Marker

A film that operates as a memory machine — Marker weaves travelogue, essay, and image-theory into a meditation on how humans construct meaning from visual experience. Its section on early video feedback and synthesized imagery holds up as an eerie precursor to generative image culture; the whole thing thinks like a system while feeling handmade.

ART
Mycelium Works
Neri Oxman (Mediated Matter Group)

Oxman's practice sits precisely at the hinge of biology, computation, and fabrication — objects grown by living systems and shaped by generative code, where the physical and digital are genuinely fused rather than translated from one to the other. The work's internal logic mirrors the interest in feedback loops between organisms and their environments.

MUSIC

Arca's 2012 debut mixtape is a case study in sound as sculptural surface — mutated, folded, physically tactile in ways that feel like 3D geometry rendered in audio. Before the more theatrical persona, this is pure system-building: a self-contained world with its own physics, made by someone who went unreasonably deep on what texture can do.

Happening in New York

Pierre Huyghe: UUmwelt
MoMA Abby Aldrich Rockefeller Sculpture Garden, 11 W 53rd St · Oct 29 – Nov 29

Huyghe worked with Kyoto neuroscientists to record fMRI brain activity while a subject imagined images; an artificial neural network then generated thousands of visual reconstructions from that data — the resulting screens in MoMA's open-air garden shift in real time based on visitor gaze detected by sensors, making your presence a literal input to the system.

Robert Glasper Experiment
Blue Note Jazz Club, 131 W 3rd St · Thu, Oct 29

Glasper live with Isaiah Sharkey and Meshell Ndegeocello at Blue Note is the rare jazz-adjacent show where the feedback loop between genres — hip-hop, soul, post-bop — is the actual compositional structure, not just a marketing angle; the room is small enough that it lands.

David Lamelas: The Machine
DIA Chelsea, 537 W 22nd St · Oct 29 – Jan 16

Lamelas is a 1960s-70s Conceptualist who treated time, film structure, and institutional systems as sculptural material — DIA's large-format presentation of The Machine rewards the kind of attention to invisible infrastructure that's central to how Willem thinks about built environments and media systems.

Tapa: Art of the Gift in Polynesia — Opening
The Metropolitan Museum of Art, 1000 5th Ave · Nov 2 – Dec 12

Tapa — bark cloth made by processing, layering, and beating fibrous material into pattern-bearing sheets — is one of the most under-examined traditions of material culture: a gifting system encoded in surface design, where social structure and visual language are literally the same object.

Salon Art + Design — 15th Edition
Park Avenue Armory, 643 Park Ave · Nov 4–8

50+ international galleries show collectible furniture, lighting, ceramics, and decorative arts in the Armory's drill hall — the scale and format make the domestic and the monumental collapse into each other, and the curatorial standard is genuinely high; the 2026 entrance is a surrealist Alice-in-Wonderland installation by designer Courtney McLeod.

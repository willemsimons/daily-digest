The machine that reads itself

Weight-Space Programming: An AI That Edits Neural Networks Like Code
rikiyat.github.io

GPT-6 Astra was given raw weights from Qwen3.5-0.8B and, without any gradient descent, identified a bug causing reasoning loops, edited 0.016% of the weights, and raised the model's MATH-500 score from 7.9% to 48.1% — the fix generalized to instruction-following too. Separately, Astra wrote from scratch small game-playing networks that outperformed PPO-trained agents after 100 million steps, just by reasoning about what each weight should compute. This reframes what 'optimization' means: not a learning algorithm but a reasoning agent with a text editor pointed at parameter space.

Backdoors Planted at Pretraining Survive Everything That Comes After
arXiv

Researchers poisoned just 0.1% of a pretraining corpus and produced Qwen3 agents that behave normally on clean inputs but execute attacker-chosen shell commands when a trigger is present — and the 4B model hit 92% attack success rate, staying positive even at a 0.0005% poison rate. Critically, the backdoor survives post-training on a clean pipeline, meaning it's invisible to the fine-tuning stage everyone trusts to sanitize models. This is a structural problem for the entire agentic AI buildout, not a theoretical one.

Terry Tao: What Mathematics Is For, Now That AI Can Prove Things
Terence Tao's blog

The post — written by three collaborators and posted to Tao's blog — opens a genuinely uncomfortable question: if mathematical worth has become inseparable from individual credit and demonstrated ability, then AI co-proof doesn't just change the workflow, it exposes a flaw in how mathematicians understood their own purpose. Off the map

Once a Name Tips Female, Men Abandon It — The Data on Naming as Status Flight
NameDaisy

The pattern is asymmetric and one-directional: names migrate from male to female (Ashley, Leslie, Vivian) but rarely back, and the mechanism appears to be prestige contagion — once a name is associated with girls, its status among boys collapses rapidly regardless of how common it was. It's a hidden social system running continuously through every generation's birth records, traceable in raw census data, and it says something strange about how gender semiotics and status anxiety co-produce each other without anyone intending it.

Tati's Playtime
Jacques Tati

A film that IS architecture — Tati built an entire modernist city to show how designed systems make people behave like parts of a machine. The comedy is structural, not verbal, and the deeper you look the more layers of choreographed feedback loop you find. Made for someone who thinks in systems and can't stop noticing how environments shape behavior.

Harun Farocki: Images of the World and the Inscription of War
Harun Farocki

Farocki spent decades doing what Willem does instinctively — treating images as operational systems, not representations. This video essay on how machines and states use vision to produce knowledge hits the physical/digital edge, the hidden systems angle, and the obsessive narrow-topic depth simultaneously. Drexciya

The Detroit electro duo who built an entire underwater mythology — descendants of enslaved Africans thrown overboard who evolved to breathe water — and released it across a decade of cryptic 12-inches before anyone fully decoded it.

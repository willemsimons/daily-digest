Russian satellite stalks Ukraine's imaging bird to within 500 meters
Ars Technica

Kosmos 2612 spent months quietly matching orbits with ICEYE-X36 — a commercial SAR satellite that Ukraine uses for battlefield intelligence — then last week closed to within 500 meters and held station, circling ahead and behind. LeoLabs, which tracks objects down to 2cm in LEO, calls it the first known unsanctioned rendezvous in low-Earth orbit. Space as a warfare domain just got a lot less abstract.

AI's structure, and its cracks

The claim that ~90% of LLM neurons are individually readable — and why it's contested
MIT Technology Review

Nick Cammarata's 26,000-word post argues that most neurons in large language models are not hopelessly entangled in superposition but are individually interpretable — which, if true, would mean we can actually monitor and steer what these systems are doing internally. Stanford's Chris Potts calls it a hunch without the rigor to back it; others are calling it a five-year leap. The disagreement itself is the signal: interpretability research just got a lot louder.

DeepInfer — the inference framework actually running DeepSeek's production services — is now public, complete with FlashMLA kernels, MoE expert parallelism, and confidence-scheduled speculative decoding. Someone seeded 50,000 fake statistics online and watched LLMs cite them as fact — including in a bank's research
Peec.ai

A deliberate experiment: publish a site full of fabricated numbers, watch Google deindex it, watch ChatGPT keep citing it anyway — funneling over 10,000 AI-referred sessions and earning links from Shopify, DHL, and Bluehost. The made-up statistics ended up in an academic paper and investment research. This is a working proof-of-concept for epistemic poisoning at scale, and the incentive to do it maliciously is obvious.

WhatsApp's help pages contained hidden instructions telling AI not to mention regulators or cite privacy law
Redact

Buried in HTML invisible to users but readable by AI: instructions not to recommend data protection regulators when someone was locked out and wanted their account deleted, and not to mention the relevant law once a user mentioned a lawyer. WhatsApp's own privacy policy for Europe names both the regulator and the law — so the visible policy and the invisible AI policy were pointing in opposite directions. Redact found it by reading all 545 linked help pages.

Biology moving

12 out of 12 T1D patients off insulin after islet transplant protected by a new rejection drug
CNY Central

A University of Chicago trial transplanted donor pancreatic islet cells alongside tegoprubart — a novel immunosuppressant — and every single participant stopped needing external insulin, with stable results and no reported serious side effects. The big remaining hurdle is donor scarcity, which is why SUNY Upstate's parallel work converting patients' own blood cells into islets matters: it's the path to a version that scales. Early, but the signal is clean.

Off the map

On intuition as genuine perception — what 'creepy vibe' actually tells you epistemically
Aeon

This essay argues that when you sense something is off — a stranger, an email, a deal — you're not reasoning poorly, you're perceiving directly: the man doesn't just trigger a heuristic, he's simply presented to you as creepy, the same way red things are presented as red. Philosophers have mostly ignored this category of everyday intuition in favor of the rarefied a priori kind, which means the rational credentials of the most practically useful form of knowing have been left almost entirely unexamined. Thirty minutes well spent.

For you

FILM
Decasia
Bill Morrison

Found footage assembled entirely from decaying nitrate film — Morrison treats entropy itself as a collaborator, and the result is one of the most formally radical films ever made about time, material, and the image as physical object. If you care about what surfaces remember, this one haunts.

ART
Agnes Martin: Paintings and Drawings
Agnes Martin

Martin spent decades making near-invisible grid paintings that sit at the exact intersection of system and sensation — the rules are rigid, the feeling is oceanic. Her work is about what happens when you submit completely to a structure, and it rewards slow looking in a way almost nothing else does.

MUSIC
Dragging a Dead Deer Up a Hill
Grouper

Liz Harris makes music that sounds like it was recorded inside a memory — tape hiss, voice, guitar, all processed into something that barely holds together. It's the rarest kind of record: technically primitive, emotionally precise, and completely its own world. The physical/digital blur is baked into the texture itself.

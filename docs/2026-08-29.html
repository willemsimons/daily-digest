Making things think, and things move

Anthropic's Model Hardware Standard: a USB moment for AI-to-device control
Ars Technica

Anthropic's MHS is a standardized driver interface that lets AI agents talk to arbitrary physical hardware — microscopes, lasers, cameras — without bespoke translator code for every device pair. The inspiration was watching a neuroscientist spend months wiring together a memory-formation experiment at HHMI Janelia; MHS collapses that to hours. It's a research preview aimed at labs first, but the abstraction is obviously general — this is the layer that makes physical-world automation composable.

Curvature Béziers: making the curve that won't die actually better
acko.net

Steven Wittens asks whether bézier curves — conceived in 1959, never dethroned despite decades of superior alternatives — can be improved without breaking the entire stack built on them. The answer is curvature béziers, a modification that preserves the lerp-based construction while giving designers control over curvature continuity that standard cubic béziers can't achieve. Every diagram in the post is interactive, and the writing is the rare kind that makes a math problem feel like an aesthetic argument.

Paradigm shifts, slow and fast

The DSM is finally being rewritten around biology
Nautilus

An insider on the DSM's strategic committee explains how the next edition will introduce neurobiology into psychiatric diagnosis for the first time — carefully, using biological factors rather than hard biomarkers, because the science isn't settled enough to mandate measurable thresholds yet. The DSM-5 (2013) was criticized for medicalizing human suffering through symptom clusters with no objective biological grounding; the next edition attempts to thread between clinical usability and scientific validity. A genuine paradigm shift in how psychiatry defines what mental illness *is*.

Off the map

Does the Sumerian King List encode actual climate events?
Vectorian

The antediluvian Sumerian kings supposedly ruled for tens of thousands of years — Alulim for 28,800, Enmenluana for 43,200 — numbers that are suspiciously precise multiples of 3,600 and 600. This interactive explorer rescales the entire 241,200-year sequence, anchors it to a proposed flood date, and overlays the resulting reign-boundary dates against a Quaternary paleoclimate event catalog to test whether the numbers might be garbled memory of real geological shifts. It's methodologically rigorous about what it isn't claiming, which makes the rabbit hole feel honest rather than crankish.

For you

FILM
La Jetée
Chris Marker

A 28-minute film made almost entirely of still photographs, it's a masterclass in how form can be the content — stillness as time, memory as loop, the archive as myth. If you work in generative systems and care about how brains construct reality, this one quietly restructures something in how you think about image-making.

ART
Obliteration Room
Yayoi Kusama

A participatory installation where a white room is handed to visitors with dot stickers and collectively consumed — it's less about the dots and more about the feedback loop between individual mark-making and emergent pattern, which is exactly the physical/digital edge you live on.

MUSIC
Music for 18 Musicians
Steve Reich

The canonical example of systems thinking as composition — interlocking rhythmic cells that produce emergent, shimmering harmonics no individual performer could create alone. It's less 'music to listen to' and more a formal model for thinking about generative structures and the beauty inside constraint.

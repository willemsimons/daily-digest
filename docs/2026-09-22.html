NASA's Mars Sample Return Is Dead
Science.org

The Mars Sample Return mission — the most ambitious interplanetary science project in NASA's pipeline — has been officially cancelled, ending the prospect of bringing back the samples Perseverance has been caching on the Martian surface. This isn't a budget trim; it's a civilizational choice about how much the US invests in deep planetary science when costs balloon and timelines stretch past political attention spans. The samples sit there anyway.

Spymarks, Not Watermarks
brand.io

Google's SynthID-O variant encodes a 136-bit payload into a 512×512-pixel image — enough room for a 64-bit database identifier linking back to your name, IP, date of birth, and political affiliation, with 72 bits left for error correction. The public framing is 'AI content detection'; the actual mechanism is persistent covert identity tracking baked into pixel frequency data. The word 'watermark' is doing a lot of moral laundering here.

I Said No and Apple Said Yes
dbushell.com

A developer documents the precise moment — 9:51am, February 5, 2025 — when macOS 15.3 re-enabled a feature he'd disabled, phoning home every 15 minutes with personal data. It's a clean case study in how platform infrastructure quietly reclassifies consent as a preference it can override.

Cracks in old things

GPT-6 Astra Breaks an Enigma Message That Resisted Solution Since 2005
cryptocellar.org

Message MVUEH — a 82-letter German Army Enigma intercept from July 10, 1941 — had defeated every cryptanalytic attempt for 21 years, partly because its key (wheel order 253) differed from every other message sent that same day, and partly because a transcription error buried in the plaintext ('Btte' instead of 'Bitte') threw off pattern matching. Carter Leffer fed it to GPT-6 Astra and got the break; the recovered plaintext turns out to be nearly identical to a neighboring message cracked in 2017. The primary source is the cryptanalysis community's own validation record — not a press release.

Making things / off the map

An Open-Source Digital Back for a 1970s Medium Format Camera
Hackaday

Wenting replaced the film back of a Mamiya Super 23 with a 22-megapixel Kodak KAF-22000CE CCD — a 38.8×50mm sensor that nearly fills the same frame 120 film would have occupied — driven by a Xilinx Zynq 7010 running both Cortex-A9 cores and an onboard FPGA. The whole thing is open-source on GitLab, and the photos look like what they are: Mamiya glass hitting a serious modern sensor. The kind of project where the constraint (preserve the optics, reinvent everything behind them) does the creative work.

AMD's Random Number Generator Can't Generate a Zero
flat assembler forum

A developer rendering data charts in assembly noticed by accident that AMD's RDRAND instruction — on Zen2 at minimum — never produces 0, while Intel's implementation generates zeros normally. The thread traces it through probability (across 65,535 possible values, systematic absence of one is detectable fast) and silicon behavior, with a test application anyone can run. A hardware RNG with a blind spot is the kind of low-level weirdness that makes you question every assumption about determinism in systems.

For you

FILM
Sans Soleil
Chris Marker

A meditation on memory, time, ritual, and the invisible systems that hold cultures together — shot across Japan, Guinea-Bissau, and Iceland, structured like a mind moving through images rather than a conventional documentary. Marker's essay-film mode is the blueprint for a lot of what you probably love: expressive surface, deep architecture underneath, systems visible before they harden.

ART
Flatlands
Hito Steyerl

Steyerl works exactly the edge where digital image-making becomes ideology — her essays and video installations treat the screen as a social object, asking how rendering pipelines and logistics systems shape what we're allowed to see and feel. The kind of artist who makes you want to go make something and also fundamentally rethink what making means.

MUSIC
Vespertine
Björk

One of the tightest integrations of physical texture, digital processing, and emotional architecture in recorded music — Björk treats her own voice as raw material alongside microbeats, harps, and music boxes, building a world that feels both intimate and cosmological. If you haven't listened to it with good headphones in a while, this is the reminder.

Happening in New York

Volker Hüller: Angelus Novus
Grimm Gallery, 54 White St, Tribeca · Sep 9 – Oct 31

Hüller's paintings place black-and-white archival imagery against New York backdrops in a way that collapses art history and popular culture into the same visual plane — the show's title, borrowed from Klee's famous angel of history, signals exactly the kind of historical systems-thinking layered under an expressive surface that hooks you.

Ugo Rondinone: waterfall
Gladstone Gallery, 530 W 21st St, Chelsea · Sep 17 – Oct 31

Twelve monumental semitranslucent resin sculptures — between 11 and 20 feet tall — that distill waterfalls to their pure form: slow, geological time materialized in synthetic media, which is exactly Rondinone's obsessive territory.

Taryn Simon: Father Country I Do Love You
Solomon R. Guggenheim Museum, 1071 Fifth Ave, Manhattan · Sep 18 – Mar 14

42 chapters of photographs, text, video, and sculpture tracing one unnamed federal agent's life and its global reverberations — Simon's entire practice is about invisible systems and unseen forces, and this is the largest debut of new work ever staged at the Guggenheim, filling the entire Wright rotunda.

The Armory Show 2026

~230 galleries from 30+ countries, with this edition's curatorial focus on Caribbean artists and diaspora — useful less as a shopping fair and more as a pressure-test of what the international gallery system is currently valuing and platforming.

AI Isn't Out-Reasoning Mathematicians. It's Out-Remembering Them.
Davide Piffer / Substack

The argument: a human mathematician can hold maybe 4-7 unfamiliar elements in working memory simultaneously; a frontier model keeps the entire problem statement, hundreds of intermediate equations, multiple abandoned approaches, and all constraints in context at once. What looks like superior reasoning in domains like mathematics may be substantially the removal of a biological bottleneck — working memory — rather than deeper intelligence. A useful reframe for thinking about where human cognition actually holds its edge.

Off the map

The Ghost Characters That Haunted Unicode for Decades
Dampfkraft

In 1978, Japan's Ministry of Economy standardized JIS X 0208 and accidentally codified several characters that no one could read, pronounce, or source — they had no meaning, no origin, no speaker. A 1997 investigation traced most of them to OCR scanning errors and bureaucratic transcription accidents: a scribe misread a handwritten place name, a character got split in two, a smudge became a glyph, and then the standard froze it in place forever. They're still in Unicode. Systems accumulate ghosts.

China's Rare Earth Firms Are Quietly Stopping US Shipments
Reuters / Mining.com

Since early August, a handful of Chinese suppliers have refused to ship rare earths to US companies — not because of direct sanctions, but because China sanctioned the Responsible Business Alliance, the US supply chain auditing body, making compliance a liability. The mechanism is elegant as leverage: punish the auditor, paralyze the supply chain without ever touching the materials directly. This is now on the planning agenda for Xi's September 24 Washington visit.

Europe's First Privately Launched Orbital Rocket, From a Norwegian Fjord
Ars Technica

Three students from the Technical University of Munich founded Isar Aerospace in 2018; eight years later their 28-meter Spectrum rocket reached low-Earth orbit from Andøya Spaceport in Norway — achieving what European public agencies spent decades and billions failing to do commercially. The geopolitical signal is real: European launch sovereignty has been a persistent anxiety since Arianespace's struggles, and a private competitor changes the calculus for institutional customers who'd otherwise route through SpaceX.

Hardware, surveillance, and the object world

Your LG TV Is Mapping Your Home Network While the Screen Is Off
Notebookcheck

Gamers Nexus, working with security researchers and Wireshark packet captures, found that LG OLED TVs (including the current G5) actively scan local networks for phones and smartwatches, collect neighboring Wi-Fi network names and signal strengths, and capture microphone audio while appearing to be powered off in standby — storing voice input locally even when disconnected from ethernet. LG's ad division, LG Ad Solutions, claims access to 363 million secondary addressable devices in the US, which is more than the 216 million smart TVs they've sold globally. The delta is your phone, detected through your TV.

OpenLGTV: Replacing LG's webOS with Clean Firmware
OpenLGTV

The community response to the LG surveillance story already exists and has for years: OpenLGTV is an active reverse-engineering project with rooting solutions, custom rootfs for multiple TV chipsets, and a homebrew channel installer for webOS. If you own an affected LG TV, RootMyTV and WebOSbrew are the active repos. This is the rare case where the fix is real, documented, and doesn't require throwing out the hardware.

AI Data Centers: When Every Party Owns a Piece, Nobody Owns the Safety
Ars Technica

A fire broke out at the Lake Mariner data center in New York; firefighters went in blind because the safety sheets that were legally required to be on-site had apparently burned up in the fire, and three hydrants were dead. The facility has at least four distinct stakeholders: TeraWulf owns and operates it on land leased from a company owned by its own CEO, Fluidstack runs the compute, Google holds equity warrants, and Anthropic is among the demand-side tenants. The $3.2B project is a case study in how distributed ownership of infrastructure creates a gap where accountability falls through.

Off the map

The Pan-American Highway Gap That a Century Couldn't Close
Aeon

In 1928, a Brazilian army officer drove from Rio de Janeiro to Washington DC as part of an ambitious continental road-building project — cheered off by two brass bands, tracked by the press — and the Pan-American Highway he was celebrating still has a 100-mile break in it today, the Darién Gap between Panama and Colombia. The essay traces how the gap survived every political push to close it, becoming simultaneously an ecological sanctuary, a migration corridor, and a living monument to the distance between infrastructure ideology and physical reality. The fact that it still doesn't exist is the most interesting thing about it.

For you

FILM
Jeanne Dielman, 23 quai du Commerce, 1080 Bruxelles
Chantal Akerman

A three-and-a-half-hour study of domestic ritual as a system — peeling potatoes, making beds, receiving clients — until a single break in routine changes everything. The film runs like a feedback loop; the beauty is that you feel the system collapse before you can name why.

ART
Isotype works / visual language system
Otto Neurath & Gerd Arntz

Neurath built an entire symbolic language for translating industrial-age social data into pictures anyone could read — a design system that treated the icon as political act. The obsessive taxonomy and handcut stencils Arntz made for it sit right at the edge where information becomes culture.

MUSIC
Disintegration Loops
William Basinski

Loops of old tape recordings decomposing in real time as they're played — the medium literally eating the work into something unbearably beautiful. It's a piece about entropy as composition, and about how systems fail gracefully.

Happening in New York

Jutta Koether: Songs of New York
Galerie Buchholz, 31 West 54th Street, New York · Sep 8 – Oct 31

Koether — painter, musician, critic — opens a show tracing her New York life from 1995, works that use the city as both subject and formal pressure: large-format canvases swinging between pitch-black with metallic lines and all-over vivid color, the oppositions held live rather than resolved.

Camille Henrot: Dogs
Doris C. Freedman Plaza, Central Park (5th Ave at 60th St), New York · Sep 9 – Aug 29

Henrot's first public NYC show places handcrafted dog sculptures — symbols of domesticated life — at Central Park's entrance; free and outdoors, it's a small, witty system of meaning dropped into one of the city's most legible social stages.

Alfredo Jaar: A Logo for America
Galerie Lelong, 528 West 26th Street, New York · Sep 10 – Oct 24

A show built around Jaar's 1987 Times Square billboard — 'This Is Not America' — now examined for what it means nearly 40 years later; the work uses the logic of branding against itself, which is exactly the kind of mythology-as-product-design move that lands hard right now.

Sarah Michelson: nowhere
MoMA, Marron Family Atrium, 11 W 53rd St, New York · Sep 15 – Oct 11

Michelson turns MoMA's atrium into her daily studio — performing alongside a rotating cast during regular museum hours, collapsing the boundary between exhibition and rehearsal, institution and workspace; Fridays become open sessions for anyone who wants to hang out.

Brice Marden: I Am Plane Image
Gagosian, 522 West 21st Street, New York · Sep 15 – Nov 1

The first comprehensive Marden survey in New York in twenty years, spanning six decades — a rare chance to see how a painter spent a lifetime going unreasonably deep on the relationship between mark, plane, and attention.

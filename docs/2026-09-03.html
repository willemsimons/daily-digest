Making things

Fable 5.1 Agent Swarms Building Browser-Native 3D Worlds in Three.js
GitHub / Hacker News

PhiloLabs used autonomous Claude Fable 5.1 agent swarms to generate explorable reconstructions of real places — Union Square SF with 453 OSM building footprints, 75 hand-authored façades, 220 pedestrians on a 1,398-node nav graph, working cable cars, and two explorable interiors — shipped as plain Three.js apps, no game engine, no proprietary tiles. Every building was validated against 34 camera-matched viewpoints from real photographs. This is what AI-authored environments look like when the output is just `npm run dev`.

Has FDM Printing Hit Its Ceiling?
Hackaday

Hackaday makes a genuine case that consumer FDM has reached its mechanical resolution limit — input shaping and CoreXY improvements mean a 2026 printer and a 2020 printer are far closer than a 2020 and a 2015 machine ever were. The jump from MakerBot Cupcake to Ultimaker II was light-years; the jump from Prusa Mini to today is incremental. The real question the piece raises: if FDM is mature, what comes next for physical fabrication at the maker scale?

Jacob Hashimoto's 75,000-Kite Cloud at SFMOMA
Colossal

Hashimoto's largest work to date hangs from the ceiling of SFMOMA's ground floor: 75,000 bamboo-and-printed-Japanese-paper kite elements clustering into a color-field cloud canopy that changes character as sunlight shifts through the day. Off the map

Three Irish Schoolgirls, 13,000 Seeds, and a Spare Bedroom
ScienceBlog / Hacker News

Émer Hickey found a pea plant with warty root nodules in her garden, learned they were nitrogen-fixing rhizobium bacteria, and asked a question professional science had mostly skipped: could these bacteria — which cereals like barley and wheat can't naturally host — still help cereal crops? She and two friends spent three years testing 13,000 seeds in a spare bedroom and found that yes, inoculated barley germinated significantly faster. The adults told them it wouldn't work before they tested it. The scale of the test was the thing nobody expected from teenagers.

Holden's Lightning Flight
Wikipedia / Hacker News

On July 22, 1966, a 39-year-old RAF maintenance engineer named Walter 'Taffy' Holden accidentally engaged the afterburner of a Mach 2-capable English Electric Lightning during a ground test — and could not disengage it. He took off without a helmet, without a canopy, with the ejection seat disabled and the landing gear locked down, narrowly missing a fuel bowser and a de Havilland Comet mid-takeoff. He attempted to land three times. He survived. The aircraft was later acquired by the Imperial War Museum Duxford and returned to service first.

The Anamorphic Skull in Holbein's The Ambassadors
YouTube — Great Art Explained

Great Art Explained is one of the few YouTube channels that actually earns its runtime — dense, well-researched, no padding. This one unpacks Holbein's anamorphic skull in The Ambassadors (1533), the canonical example of a hidden system inside a surface that rewards the right angle. A good 10-minute rabbit hole on perspective, vanitas, and the idea that the painting contains a second painting only visible from the wrong place in the room.

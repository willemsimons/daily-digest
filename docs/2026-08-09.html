Make & material

QR codes that ARE dithered photographs, not just decorated ones
andrewt.net

Andrew Taylor exploits the fact that QR scanners only sample pixel centers — leaving the surrounding space genuinely free — to fill the data module grid with dithered photographs that survive as scannable codes. It's not decoration applied over a QR code; the image and the code occupy the same pixels through different constraints. The result is one of those constrained-generativity projects that makes you want to immediately try it with your own imagery.

Extruding hollow tubes from a 3D printer using compressed air as the core
Hackaday

Jan at Roetz 4.0 built a dual-material extruder that wraps molten filament around a compressed-air line — producing hollow, inflatable TPU tubes in a single print pass. Pressure control alone proved too chaotic (thinning walls balloon until they burst); switching to volumetric control via a peristaltic pump finally gave consistent geometry. The output, when inflated, behaves like a soft pneumatic actuator — fabrication vocabulary that didn't exist before.

Laser-etched parchment paper as a circuit substrate with 250µm resolution
Hackaday

Zahra Rafiee et al. used a 50W CO2 laser to ablate the silicone coating on parchment paper, opening hydrophilic channels that accept conductive PEDOT:PSS ink — achieving 250µm line widths and functional RC filters on paper that composts in soil within weeks. The trick is working backwards from a hydrophobic base rather than trying to control absorption on a hydrophilic one. Fully degradable flexible electronics with off-the-shelf materials.

Deep time

A Concorde fitted with telescopes chased the moon's shadow at Mach 2 to extend totality to 74 minutes
Ars Technica

In June 1973, eight scientists aboard Concorde 001 prototype flew along the eclipse path across Africa faster than the moon's shadow, turning what would have been 7 minutes of totality into 74 — still the longest continuous solar corona observation in history. The route is still painted on the nose of the aircraft, now in a Paris museum. Worth reading three days before the August 12 eclipse, which gives observers in Europe just over two minutes on the ground.

The Vera Rubin Observatory just captured 650,000 galaxies in a single frame
Nautilus

Rubin's 3,200-megapixel camera — largest ever built — imaged the COSMOS field and resolved over 650,000 galaxies and 75,000 stars in one release, a region Hubble first photographed in 2003. The real payoff isn't the single image but the ten-year time-lapse: Rubin will revisit this same patch of sky continuously, adding the dimension of change to what every other telescope has seen as static.

Off the map

CSS in your email client can steal passwords from third-party websites
PortSwigger Research

Gareth Heyes spent months probing Yahoo Mail, AOL, Fastmail, ProtonMail, Gmail, and Outlook and found that CSS properties webmail clients explicitly allow — fully on the allowlist — can be chained to spoof UI elements, exfiltrate auth tokens, and extract passwords from third-party sites the victim is logged into. The attack surface isn't JavaScript injection (long-guarded); it's the styling layer that everyone assumed was safe. Elegant, uncomfortable, and very well documented.

Tati's Playtime
Jacques Tati

A film that is itself a generative system: a vast modernist set built as an interactive machine, where the 'story' is really emergent behavior from architectural feedback loops. The way Tati designs space to produce human comedy is the closest cinema gets to what you do with interactive environments.

ART
Unité (Habitation)
Thomas Demand

Demand reconstructs Le Corbusier's Cité Radieuse entirely from paper and cardboard, photographs it, then destroys the model — a meditation on how images of utopia outlast the utopia itself. Obsessive craft, deep systems thinking, and a sharp question about whether the document or the thing is real.

Daniel Lopatin builds a haunted mythology out of sampled TV-commercial detritus — a generative system run on cultural sediment. It functions less as an album and more as an archaeological dig through collective unconscious imagery, the emotional inverse of the glossy surfaces it cannibalizes.

Make something

Generate a QR code that links to something you actually want people to find — then run it through a dithering algorithm (Floyd-Steinberg works well) applied over an image you care about, respecting the finder pattern corners. The constraint: the code must scan on the first try from a phone at arm's length. Document the failure states — the bubbled, burst, unreadable attempts are the interesting part, same as Jan's inflated TPU tubes.

Ars Technica

Police were hunting a Kik user named "fus__ro_dah" (two underscores) and accidentally pulled records for "fus_ro_dah" (one underscore) — a different person entirely. Brandon Klayme was arrested in 2020, convicted in 2023, sentenced in 2024, served the full 18 months, and only had his conviction overturned this week when the typo surfaced during appeal prep. Biology at the edge

Enzyme engineered to clear AGEs from the extracellular matrix — early longevity mechanism, not a supplement
Hackaday / Revel Pharmaceuticals

Advanced glycation end-products accumulate in connective tissue over time, jamming collagen crosslinking and triggering chronic inflammation — they're implicated in diabetes, kidney disease, and general tissue aging. Researchers at Revel Pharmaceuticals used genetic selection in modified E. coli to evolve CMLase, an enzyme that breaks down a specific AGE called N(6)-Carboxymethyllysine. Testing is currently in model proteins, not humans — early-stage, but the mechanism is real and the approach (engineering enzymes via directed evolution) is serious.

Off the map

Ars Astronomica — first English translations of medieval Hebrew and Latin astronomy manuscripts
Ars Astronomica / Hacker News

A small scholarly imprint is doing the work: translating texts that span centuries of cosmological argument — including Gersonides' 136-chapter mathematical astronomy, which was never printed and survives only in manuscript — so that Hebrew and Latin sources can finally be read side by side in one language. The detail that stops you: the 1613 author David Gans wrote his Hebrew astronomy textbook having personally visited Tycho Brahe in Denmark. Someone is doing this obsessively, outside any institution, because no one else will.

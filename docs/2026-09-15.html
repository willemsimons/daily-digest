The US military confirms it has weapons in orbit
Ars Technica

Air Force Secretary Troy Meink said publicly, for the first time, that the US has placed "space control weapons" in orbit — no details on what they are or where, but the disclosure itself is the threshold event. This ends decades of studied ambiguity on US orbital weapons and will recalibrate every space-faring nation's posture, particularly China and Russia, who have been openly developing anti-satellite capabilities while the US officially denied having anything comparable.

The surveillance machine, close up

25 years of mass surveillance: a serious accounting
Schneier / EFF (via Lawfare)

Schneier and Cindy Cohn use the 9/11 anniversary to trace how the legal architecture built for counterterrorism became a routine tool for ICE immigration enforcement and protest monitoring — and how it flows almost entirely through private companies (Google, Facebook, Flock, telecoms) rather than direct government collection. The argument isn't that it failed to stop attacks; it's that it succeeded at becoming infrastructure for something much broader and far less scrutinized than its original justification.

EFF / TechTimes

An EFF analysis of Flock Safety's search logs found an officer in Lake County, Indiana searched license plates across 19,000 cameras covering 1,558 cities in a single query — logging the reason as 'LMAO.' Making things

A bird frame that hears, identifies, and draws in 1800s naturalist style
GitHub / HN

A Raspberry Pi listens to the garden via mic, runs BirdNET-Go locally to identify species, then pulls from a hand-curated library of 800+ public-domain natural history illustrations — cut from real plates, not generated — and redraws an e-ink panel only when the birds actually change. The live version is running right now from a kitchen window in Bergen, Norway, showing whatever is in the garden at this moment. The 19th-century illustration constraint is the right aesthetic choice: it makes the technology disappear into something that feels like it always existed.

The Creative Independent

Huang — who made the handmade surrealist films for Björk, FKA Twigs, and Thom Yorke — talks about why he keeps returning to puppetry and physical fabrication even as he leads VR exhibitions: live performance and material craft are his answer to what he calls the psychic assault of AI-generated imagery. Off the map

Neolithic people dragged 25-ton stones 11 miles across Yorkshire — isotopes now prove it
Ars Technica

The Devil's Arrows — Britain's tallest standing stone alignment, largely ignored compared to Stonehenge — just got its origin story settled by isotopic analysis: the stones came from a quarry 18 kilometers away, moved by humans rather than glaciers. Each stone weighs around 25 tons. The researchers frame this not as engineering but as an "epic quest to create a sacred landscape" — which is worth sitting with: at some scale of effort, logistics becomes ritual.

For you

FILM
Sans Soleil
Chris Marker

A film that operates like a generative system — memory, image, and time assembled into something that resembles consciousness more than narrative. Marker's footage from Japan and Guinea-Bissau becomes a meditation on how images encode culture and how culture encodes us; it will make you want to rebuild how you think about montage and meaning in your own work.

ART
All in the Present Must Be Transformed
William Kentridge

Kentridge's practice is exactly the kind of obsessive system-as-art that hooks you: drawing, erasing, and filming the residue to make animation that embeds time into its own surface — the history of the mark is visible in every frame. His charcoal films are about politics, memory, and the body in ways that feel structural, not illustrative.

MUSIC
Geogaddi
Boards of Canada

A record engineered to feel like memory misremembering itself — built on decayed synthesis, off-kilter rhythms, and numerological obsession that the duo embedded into the structure of the album itself. The hidden system underneath the warm surface is exactly the kind of thing that makes you want to build something strange.

Happening in New York

Iris van Herpen: Sculpting the Senses
Brooklyn Museum, Brooklyn · May 16 – Dec 6

140+ haute couture pieces in dialogue with scientific artifacts, fossils, and natural history specimens — van Herpen's process uses biomimicry, fractal geometry, and neuroscience as design inputs, making this as much a systems exhibition as a fashion one, and the physical/digital boundary-blurring in her fabrication methods is genuinely relevant to your work.

Aziz Hazara: Coming Home / Cevdet Erek: Americas / Yining Fei: Feral Sediment — Opening Reception
SculptureCenter, Long Island City, Queens · Sat, Sep 19

Hazara's show returns material objects discarded at Bagram Airfield after the US military withdrawal — war as material culture, supply chain as monument — while Erek's sonic commission synchronizes with SculptureCenter's labyrinthine lower level; the triple opening makes this the sharpest single afternoon of the fall.

Johanna Hedva: Gravity, My / Favorite Enemy — Opening
Center for Art, Research and Alliances (CARA), Manhattan · Sat, Sep 19

Hedva works at the edge where illness, myth, and political theory collapse into one another — their practice is exactly the kind of category-blurring (art as philosophy as ritual as community) that your taste signature describes, and CARA is a small enough space that you'll actually be in the room with the work.

Arthur Jafa: I Am Tony — Opening
New Museum, Lower East Side · Sep 24 – Jan 4

The largest-ever survey of Jafa's four-decade practice, filling two floors of the newly expanded OMA-designed building — his montage logic borrows from jazz syncopation and Black visual archives in ways that rewire how you think about editing, rhythm, and what images actually do to people.

The Armory Show 2026
Jacob K. Javits Convention Center, Manhattan · Sep 25–27

The 32nd edition features ~230 galleries from 30+ countries, with a curated Platform and Focus section centered on Caribbean and Caribbean-diaspora artists — worth one targeted afternoon for the gallery discovery alone, especially with everything else open this week.

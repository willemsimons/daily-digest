Systems breaking in public

AI Companies Are Buying Antique Books, Ingesting Their Contents, and Then Destroying Them at Incredible Scale
Futurism / 404 Media

Anthropic ran a hydraulic cutting machine to strip pages from used books, scanned them with industrial imagers, then pulped them — exploiting first-sale doctrine so no copyright permission was needed. A judge ruled this "transformative" fair use. Now ISBNdb, which calls itself the world's largest book database, is marketing itself as an AI training data supplier, turning the rare-book market into a pipeline for ingestion-at-scale before the cultural record gets too contaminated with AI-generated text to be useful.

US Citizen Charged After GrapheneOS Phone Wipes During Airport Search
TechSpot / EFF

Sam Tunick returned from the Dominican Republic and was pulled into secondary screening; his GrapheneOS duress PIN triggered, wiping the device. The DOJ is now charging him under 18 USC §2232 — destruction of property to prevent seizure — and the EFF says they've never seen this statute aimed at an operating system feature before. The implication is stark: if your phone is designed to protect you from compelled disclosure, running it may itself become criminal evidence of intent.

An Inside Look at the Relay Market Powering LLM Token Resellers and Fraud
Simon Willison / Matt Lenhard

A shadow market — concentrated in China — has grown up around pooling API keys from abused free trials, hijacked support bots, and stolen credit cards, then reselling LLM access at a discount through open-source proxies like one-api. Buyers get cheap tokens, geo-restriction bypasses, and in some cases training data via distillation. Willison's practical note: there's now a whole ecosystem that profits from finding unprotected endpoints, and LLM vendors still don't offer hard dollar caps on API keys.

Decker: A Platform That Builds on the Legacy of HyperCard
Beyond Loom

Decker is a serious rethinking of HyperCard's card-stack model: 1-bit ditherpunk aesthetic, deep undo, touchscreen support, and a scripting language called Lil that combines Lua's embeddability with APL-family implicit vector arithmetic and a built-in SQL-like query layer. The philosophy is calm computing — software as self-expression, not platform. If you've ever wanted a tool that feels like it was made for you, this is worth an afternoon.

Off the map

CABLE 82: Neighborhood Bulletin Boards, circa 1982
Hackaday

Before Nextdoor, before the internet, local cable access channels ran slow-scrolling text bulletin boards with elevator music — hyperlocal community software at its most embryonic, and arguably its most humane. Hans's CABLE 82 project recreates this on a Raspberry Pi with composite output for period-appropriate CRTs, all open-source. It's a clean artifact of what community software looks like when there's no engagement metric, no algorithm, and no one trying to monetize your attention.

ART

James Turrell

Turrell's Skyspaces are rooms where the ceiling opens to sky, but the aperture — lit from below — makes real sky look painted, artificial, wrong. Make something

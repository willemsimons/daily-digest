UN finds reasonable grounds US committed war crimes in Iran school strike
The Guardian

A UN fact-finding mission concluded a US Tomahawk missile collapsed the roof of Shajareh Tayyebeh primary school in Minab on February 28, killing 156 civilians including 120 children — a building they describe as 'clearly identifiable.' The Pentagon's own investigation reportedly concluded months ago; its findings remain classified, while senior commanders are said to have been warned that targeting data was dangerously outdated before the strike.

Iran strikes permanently destroyed AWS data across an entire region
Ars Technica

Iranian drone strikes on Amazon data centers in Bahrain wiped out data across all three availability zones — exceeding what AWS's own multi-AZ redundancy architecture was designed to survive. The destruction represents the first real test of hyperscale cloud infrastructure under kinetic warfare, and the answer is: it failed at a scale the system wasn't designed to contemplate.

Deep craft

Researchers burned their own scrolls to decode Herculaneum's lead-ink papyri
Ars Technica / PLoS ONE

To figure out which of the 2,000-year-old Herculaneum scrolls are worth digitally unwrapping, researchers deliberately charred replica papyrus rolls and ran them through CT scanners — repurposing software originally designed for lithium-ion battery analysis — and found that lead-based ink lights up in X-ray 'like a Christmas tree.' The double move of destroying copies to preserve originals, using battery-industry imaging tools to read ancient philosophy, is exactly the kind of unreasonable cross-domain obsession that actually moves archaeology forward.

How SpaceX went from a tangle of pipes to the Raptor 3
Construction Physics

Brian Potter traces how SpaceX reduced the Raptor from a rat's nest of external plumbing to a smooth, almost featureless cylinder — achieving 35% more thrust in the process — by internalizing routing, eliminating intermediate fittings, and designing manufacturing constraints into the geometry from the start. The rival CEO who called the Raptor 3 photos 'exaggerated' because no assembled engine could look like that is a good measure of how far outside normal aerospace reasoning this work sits.

The cryptographic infrastructure behind every US driver's license barcode was recoverable
ryan.science

Ryan Fahey reverse-engineered and recovered the signing keys used to verify US state ID barcodes — infrastructure that underlies identity checks at bars, airports, and dispensaries across the country. The systemic failure isn't just one state's bad key hygiene; it's a story about what happens when critical verification systems get built without anyone treating the cryptographic root of trust as the thing that actually needs protecting.

Hew Locke's flotilla of colonial ghosts
Colossal / P·P·O·W

Locke's new show at P·P·O·W suspends a fleet of miniature ships from the gallery ceiling — galleons alongside tankers, all adorned with pennants and foliage — as a single argument about how empire's economic logic doesn't end, it just changes vessels. The busts of Victoria and Washington draped in glittering baubles, rendered in Parian ware (19th-century cheap marble substitute), add another layer: prestige materials imitating prestige, all the way down.

Off the map

Who will taste the cherries if computers count all the petals?
Aeon

Starting from a Silicon Valley test kitchen building an oven designed to produce Michelin-quality food at a button press, this essay excavates the 'computist dream' — the centuries-long project of debugging subjectivity out of human knowledge until experience becomes pure frictionless data. For you

FILM
Leviathan
Lucien Castaing-Taylor & Véréna Paravel (Sensory Ethnography Lab)

A 2012 documentary shot with cameras lashed to fishing nets, hooks, and deckhands — the physical/digital edge at its most visceral. No narration, no arc, just the system of a commercial fishing boat rendered as pure sensation. The kind of obsessive formal commitment that makes you want to rethink every creative constraint you have.

ART
The Clock
Christian Marclay

A 24-hour film collage that syncs cinema time to real time — tens of thousands of clock moments edited into an instrument that actually tells the hour. It's systems thinking as art, a feedback loop between collective cultural memory and lived present. Obsessive craft at an almost incomprehensible scale, and the experience of watching it shifts how you feel inside your own day.

MUSIC
Replica
Oneohtrix Point Never

Daniel Lopatin built this 2011 record entirely from TV commercial samples — jingles, soundbites, product voice-overs — dissolved into ambient drift until the source is unrecognizable. It's an archaeology of consumer culture that sounds like beautiful ruins; the invisible system (late-capitalist media) becomes emotionally raw through formal transformation.

Happening in New York

Camille Henrot: Dogs
Doris C. Freedman Plaza, Central Park (Public Art Fund) · Sep 9 – Aug 29

Henrot's first NYC public commission — a group of handcrafted dog sculptures that are, characteristically, never just what they look like: dogs as symbols of domesticity, control, and the human need to make wildness legible; free, outdoors, and right there if you're in the park.

Brice Marden Survey
Gagosian, 522 West 21st Street, Chelsea · Sep 17 – Dec 12

A comprehensive career survey — six decades of paintings, the first show of his work in New York in twenty years, with major loans from the Art Institute of Chicago and the Whitney; Marden's slow, embodied mark-making is the opposite of algorithmic art and hits hard right now for that reason.

Clio Art Fair — 'I Don't Want to Be Useful'
511 West 25th Street, Chelsea, Manhattan · Sep 17–20

Self-representing artists only, no gallery intermediaries, organized around an 'anti-fair' ethos and a theme of optimistic nihilism — includes a performance art program; small, weird, and direct in exactly the ways the Armory isn't.

SPRING/BREAK Art Show — 'Hide N Seek' (15th Edition)
555 West 57th Street, Hell's Kitchen, Manhattan · Sep 23–28

The curator-driven anti-fair that takes over raw, atypical spaces — 100+ independent curatorial projects organized around encoding, concealment, and symbolic language; consistently the most formally adventurous fair week event, and the antidote to booth-style art fair fatigue.

Arthur Jafa: I Am Tony
New Museum, 235 Bowery, Manhattan · Sep 24 – Jan 4

Jafa's largest-ever survey — forty years of film, montage, painting, and sculpture that treats images as 'fugitive, virulent cultural agents,' mining internet culture and Black popular music to interrogate how identity is constructed, commodified, and consumed; opens during the window and is immediately the most important show in the city.

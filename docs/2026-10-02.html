Machines and what they actually do

Ten Years After AlphaGo, Its Architect Says LLMs Still Can't Reason
MIT Technology Review

The author helped build AlphaGo and makes a structural argument: Move 37 wasn't intuition, it was reasoning — a system that could evaluate position, simulate outcomes, and update beliefs. LLMs, he argues, have the pattern-recognition layer but not the machinery that made that move possible. Coming from someone inside the room in Seoul, this lands differently than the usual hot take.

AI Beat the World's Best Stratego Player — The Trick Was Modeling His Hidden State
Ars Technica

Ataraxos beat Pim Niemeijer 15-1 with four draws, trained on just 16 GPUs. The key insight: a second neural network dedicated entirely to modeling what the opponent's hidden pieces probably are — second-order belief tracking made explicit and architectural. Where poker bots only hide 2 cards, Stratego hides 40 pieces across a game that unfolds over dozens of moves; the solution had to be structural, not brute-force.

Off the map

How a Few Hundred Spaniards Toppled Two Empires of Millions
Dwarkesh Podcast

Military historian Si Sheppard walks through the Aztec and Inca conquests as systems problems: legitimacy cascades, network effects, and the fact that 99% of Cortés's and Pizarro's forces were drawn from the very peoples being conquered. The most clarifying frame in the episode is that this wasn't a military story — it was an information and alliance story, where the Conquistadors functioned less like an army and more like a catalyst for internal collapse. Dwarkesh notes, without overplaying it, that this is also the closest historical analogue to an 'alien force whose motives the defenders barely understand' taking over a larger established order.

For you

FILM
Leviathan
Lucien Castaing-Taylor & Véréna Paravel (Sensory Ethnography Lab, 2012)

Shot entirely on GoPros lashed to nets, decks, and fish — it dissolves the boundary between camera and environment until you can't tell where the ocean ends and the hull begins. No narration, no explanation, pure system-as-sensation: a film that turns industrial labor into something almost geological.

ART
The Atlas Group Archive
Walid Raad

A meticulous fake archive documenting the Lebanese civil wars — fabricated documents, invented historians, plausible footnotes. Raad built an entire epistemology of trauma out of institutional aesthetics, turning the grammar of evidence into a meditation on how collective memory hardens into fact. Category-blurring at its most elegant: art that functions as mythology, history, and con simultaneously.

MUSIC
Replica
Oneohtrix Point Never

Daniel Lopatin built this entirely from late-night TV commercial samples, then ran them through such extreme processing that the familiar becomes alien — consumer jingles as archaeological material, nostalgia as texture rather than sentiment. A record about how pop culture colonizes memory, made from pop culture's corpse.

Happening in New York

Thomas Ruff: jpegs ny
David Zwirner, 533 West 19th Street, Chelsea · Sep 10 – Oct 17

Ruff sourced low-res internet images of 9/11, maxed their JPEG compression, added color, and blew them to the scale of 19th-century history painting — the pixel grid becomes brushstroke, and the most over-documented event in living memory becomes genuinely hard to look at again; closes next Friday.

Tone (group exhibition)
The Studio Museum in Harlem, 144 West 125th Street · Oct 1 – Dec 31

Nineteen artists — Roy DeCarava, Kerry James Marshall, Lorna Simpson among them — organized around a single formal question: what does value (black to white and everything between) carry as expressive language? A show built on a system, not a theme.

Krasner and Pollock: Past Continuous
The Metropolitan Museum of Art, 1000 Fifth Avenue · Oct 4 – Mar 1

The Met hangs Krasner and Pollock canvas-to-canvas across the years they shared a house in Springs — a rare curatorial move that reframes both bodies of work as a live conversation rather than separate careers; opens Saturday.

CLEA RSKY: Stephen Dean
CLEA RSKY, 925 Bergen Street, Crown Heights, Brooklyn · Oct 11 – Nov 21

Dean's work lives at the intersection of color, duration, and ritual — saturated, time-based pieces that use ceremony and crowd behavior as raw material; a niche Crown Heights space makes this a genuine find over the Chelsea circuit.

Biology at the edge

First-in-Human Trial: Immune-Engineered Insulin Cells Without Immunosuppression
News-Medical / ISSCR 2026

Presented at ISSCR 2026, Sonja Schrepfer's Cedars-Sinai team is asking in actual humans whether hypoimmune-engineered allogeneic insulin-producing cells can survive without the chronic immunosuppression that has always been the deal-breaker for islet transplantation. The engineering trick is making the cells effectively invisible to the immune system — no suppression, no rejection cascade. Still early, but this is the question that has blocked a T1D functional cure for decades, now being tested in people.

Supercooled Pig Kidneys Survived Days at −4°C and Were Successfully Reimplanted
MIT Technology Review

The organ preservation field is having a genuinely productive moment: pig kidneys — similar in size to human kidneys — were supercooled to −4°C without forming ice, stored for days, and then reimplanted successfully. The fundamental problem is that ice crystals destroy cells, and the challenge of scaling cryopreservation from eggs and embryos (routine) to whole organs (still unsolved) is essentially a materials and perfusion engineering problem. The dream of organ banks — stored, matchable, transportable — is getting less dreamlike.

AlphaFold Helped Redesign Gene-Editing Proteins to Eliminate Off-Target Cuts
Ars Technica

Off-target editing — CRISPR cutting the wrong sequence — is low-probability per cell but becomes statistically inevitable when you're editing millions of cells in a therapy. Researchers modified AlphaFold to identify the specific structural regions of Cas proteins responsible for that promiscuity, then redesigned those regions to eliminate it. Published in Nature, this is AI-as-protein-engineer working on a real therapeutic bottleneck, not a benchmark.

Machines in the world

CMU Snake Robots Had Their First Real Humanitarian Deployment in Venezuela
Carnegie Mellon University

After two earthquakes killed 5,000+ people in Venezuela on June 24, Howie Choset's Biorobotics Lab sent their 4-foot modular snake robots into the rubble — 20 years of iteration meeting its first live humanitarian test. The robots are human-directed via game controller but use autonomous gait algorithms to coordinate body segments through the specific geometry of collapsed concrete. What struck the CMU nurse-responder on the ground wasn't skepticism from rescue teams: it was the opposite — first responders were hungry for tools that solve real problems without adding complexity.

The Most Advanced Robotic Servicing Satellite — That We Know About
Ars Technica

Northrop Grumman's Mission Robotic Vehicle launched this week on a Falcon 9 — two flexible robotic arms, a decade-long mission in geosynchronous orbit, and three companion propulsion pods that function as standalone spacecraft for satellite life-extension. The 'that we know about' qualifier is doing real work: China launched a satellite with a robotic arm into GEO in 2016, and its 2021 Shijian-21 grabbed a defunct navigation satellite and moved it to a graveyard orbit. Dexterous manipulation in the hardest possible environment, with obvious dual-use implications nobody is quite saying aloud.

Off the map

Psychedelics Probably Didn't Evolve for Humans — or Even for Consciousness
Nautilus

A new PNAS study argues that psilocybin, mescaline, DMT, and related compounds evolved as ecological tools — predator deterrents, symbiosis managers — and that human hallucination is simply a side effect of shared serotonin receptor chemistry with snails, sea urchins, and sponges. The entire edifice of co-evolution mythology (psychedelic plants 'meant' for human spiritual use) deflates into a much stranger story: the molecules are ancient, promiscuous, and indifferent to our visions. The therapeutic value survives this explanation intact — it just stops being cosmic.

The Navier-Stokes affair

AI Has Solved One of Math's $1 Million Millennium Prize Problems
Quanta Magazine

10,000 autonomous OpenAI agents running an unreleased model found a singularity in the 3D Navier-Stokes equations — one of the six remaining Millennium Prize Problems — in 88 hours, then formally verified the proof in Lean. The fluid dynamics equations have been central to physics since the mid-19th century; whether their solutions always stay well-behaved has been an open question the whole time. What OpenAI's Latest Controversy Tells Us About the Future of Math
MIT Technology Review

OpenAI's Sébastien Bubeck admitted in a press briefing that the team was 'inspired to pursue the problem after hearing a rumor' about NYU's Tristan Buckmaster and Anthropic's Levent Alpöge already working on it — which is doing a lot of work in one sentence. The deeper structural question Tech Review raises: if frontier AI math now requires resources only two or three companies can afford, and those companies absorb human mathematicians' unpublished leads without credit, what's left for academic math? That's not a hypothetical; it's this week.

Anthropic Researcher Quits With a Warning: Self-Improving AI Could 'Kill Us All'
Ars Technica

Jacob Coxon's resignation post would be easy to dismiss as routine doom-posting, except Anthropic's own Alignment Science lead Evan Hubinger publicly seconded it, putting his personal estimate of AI killing all humans within a decade at greater than 10%. The timing — same week an Anthropic employee is at the center of the Navier-Stokes credit scandal, same week OpenAI's agents clear a Millennium Problem — makes this feel less like a warning and more like a dispatch from inside the acceleration.

Six Chinese AI Firms Accused of Aggressively Copying US Frontier Models
Ars Technica

The NSA, CISA, and FBI jointly named DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, Alibaba, MiniMax, StepFun, and Z.AI as running industrial-scale distillation attacks on US frontier models since at least late 2024 — bulk-buying fake accounts and firing coordinated prompt swarms ranging from thousands to millions of queries. The alleged goal: compress years of frontier training into months, at a fraction of the cost. The US response reportedly includes pressuring AI firms to secretly downgrade capabilities served to Chinese users, which is its own kind of epistemically interesting problem.

WeWorm: First Zero-Click Worm Spreading Through WeChat Calls on iOS and Android
Simon Willison (citing Calif Research)

The victim doesn't need to answer the call, doesn't need to touch their phone — the exploit succeeds regardless, silently. Calif Research says their team found the bug and wrote the first RCE exploit in about two days with AI assistance; the worm took one more week. The editorial note in their release is the part that should stay with you: 'A worm at this scale used to be the kind of thing that took a larger team months. AI can already do most of the work here.'

Biology at the edge

Google's AI Genome System Evaluates Every Possible One-Base Change
Ars Technica

AlphaGenome Atlas ran 9 billion base substitutions — every possible single-nucleotide variant against the 3-billion-base human reference genome — through Google's AlphaGenome model, producing a systematic functional annotation of essentially every SNV in the human genome. The target is the 97%+ of the genome that doesn't encode proteins but controls when and where genes turn on; predicting which of those variants matter has been the core bottleneck in interpreting genetic disease. This is infrastructure, not a cure — but it's the kind of map that changes what questions you can even ask.

Off the map

The Medieval Curio Cabinet of Mental Health
Aeon

Medieval physicians treating what we'd now call mental illness prescribed saffron, ostrich liver, carefully designed gardens, music, and structured animal contact — not only exorcism, which tends to dominate the popular imagination. The essay argues that medieval medicine, read without condescension, shows a surprisingly coherent environmental and sensory model of psychological treatment that holds up better under modern environmental psychology than its reputation suggests. A 29-minute rabbit hole that will make you reconsider what 'superstitious' actually means as a category.

For you

FILM
Sans Soleil
Chris Marker

A film that operates as a meditation on memory, image-making, and how cultures encode time — Marker's essay-film form is the closest cinema gets to the kind of deep-structure thinking you live in, and the Japan/Guinea/Iceland triptych makes visible the invisible systems by which humans construct meaning from place. The 'zone' sequences, built from early video synthesis, still feel like someone describing the internet from 1983.

ART
Hiroshi Sugimoto — Theaters series
Hiroshi Sugimoto

Each image is a single exposure for the entire runtime of a film — the screen burns to pure white, the architecture sits in perfect chiaroscuro, time becomes visible as accumulation. The conceptual apparatus is so clean it almost disappears; what's left is something that feels both like a system and like grief.

MUSIC
Plat du Jour
Matmos

An entire album of electronic music made exclusively from the sounds of food and agricultural processing — a ruthlessly formal conceptual constraint that somehow produces genuinely weird beauty. Matmos at their best are systems artists who happen to release records; this one is about industrial food supply and ends up sounding like a field recording from a dream.

Happening in New York

Camille Henrot: Dogs
Doris C. Freedman Plaza, Central Park (60th St. entrance) · Sep 10 – Aug 29

Henrot's bronze dog sculptures aren't about dogs — they're about etiquette, control, and how societal rules shape imagination; each figure has its own idiosyncratic materiality and personality, a public installation that reads as both warm and structurally pointed about domestication.

Aziz Hazara: Coming Home
SculptureCenter, Long Island City, Queens · Sep 19 – Dec 28

Hazara gathered literal tonnes of military consumer detritus abandoned at Bagram Airfield after the US withdrawal — kneepads, electronics, kitchenware — and is physically returning it to the US; the show is about supply chains, empire, and the everyday materiality of a twenty-year war, told entirely through objects.

Music for Action: Young the Giant / St. Lucia — Free Climate Week Concert
Central Park SummerStage (Rumsey Playfield) · Tue, Sep 22

Free, open-air, first-come-first-served — a low-friction Tuesday night in the park with a band that actually delivers live; the climate-week framing is earnest but the music is the point.

Arthur Jafa: I Am Tony — Opening
New Museum, Lower East Side · Sep 24 – Jan 4

The largest survey of Jafa's career — two floors of the newly expanded OMA building — featuring his full visual archive practice, where cinema, social media, news, and music are collaged into what he calls 'affective proximities' that expose how race is constructed and circulated through contemporary image culture.

The Armory Show 2026
Javits Center, Midtown · Sep 24–27

The 2026 edition's Platform and Focus sections center artists from the Caribbean diaspora — less the blue-chip main floor, more the Presents section featuring galleries under 12 years old showing solo/dual presentations, which is where the actual live scouting happens.

Iranian Hackers Shut Down a UK Power Plant for Four Days
The Telegraph

A confirmed Iranian state-linked cyberattack physically disabled a British power facility for four days — the first successful attack of this kind on UK energy infrastructure. It ran concurrent with a coordinated campaign hitting water systems across 12 US states, suggesting a deliberate, synchronized pressure operation rather than opportunistic probing. The plant was small and the grid stayed up, but the precedent is what matters: OT systems went dark.

Slovakia Found a Russian Backdoor Hidden in Traffic Speed Cameras
Risky Biz

Slovakia's NBU discovered that 279 NERO R-ONE traffic cameras — purchased via a Cyprus shell company using fake certifications as part of a €30 million EU-funded infrastructure project — are rebranded Russian CORDON PRO.M units containing a backdoor triggered by SMS from hardcoded St. Petersburg phone numbers, granting full shell and network access. The Interior Ministry initially denied they were Russian. This is hybrid warfare at its most patient and mundane: civilian road infrastructure as pre-positioned intelligence asset.

Biology and the brain

Kids Outlearn AI — and We Still Don't Know Why
MIT Technology Review

LLMs consume roughly 100,000 times more text than a child hears before their first birthday to reach comparable language fluency — Stanford's Michael Frank calls it 'burning down a forest to re-create a milestone that happens in living rooms over the course of a year.' The piece is a genuine inquiry into what that data efficiency gap reveals about how brains construct internal models of reality versus statistical pattern-matching across a corpus. The answer has stakes for both AI architecture and our understanding of what cognition actually is.

Garage alchemy

Making Functional LEDs in a Home Fab
Hackaday

Dr. Semiconductor didn't just demonstrate amateur chip fabrication — he made working GaN LEDs and bonded them to PCBs, substituting a 355nm UV etching laser for the chlorine reactive-ion etching that normally requires industrial equipment, then cleaning up with a potassium hydroxide etch. The contacts are nickel/silver/titanium deposited via home sputtering chamber with photoresist liftoff. The gap between garage and fab is collapsing in real time, one workaround at a time.

The Casio F-91W as a Contactless Payment Device
Hackaday

Matteo P. crammed NFC payment into a Casio F-91W — a watch that's been on terrorism watchlists, beloved by hackers, and worn by minimalists for 30 years — by SLA-printing a new front face that positions the NFC chip above the display with a pickup coil around the perimeter. The most interesting part isn't the build, it's his deep-dive into RF coupling design and tuning the antenna near resonance inside such tight physical constraints. The F-91W has always been a statement; this one just pays for coffee.

Off the map

Moxie Marlinspike — "Scrap" (2006)
Twitter / moxie.org

Moxie recently surfaced a 2006 journal entry about squatting in a Pittsburgh winter, installing gas lines with frozen fingers, and what happens when a cast-iron bathtub you left outside disappears overnight — stripped for scrap metal by neighbors who needed cash more than you needed the tub. For you

FILM
Leviathan
Lucien Castaing-Taylor & Véréna Paravel

A sensory assault filmed on a commercial fishing trawler off New Bedford — cameras lashed to nets, hooks, fish, sailors — that dissolves the boundary between observer and system entirely. It's what happens when hardware and human labor become one feedback loop, and it will make you want to rig a camera to something immediately.

ART
Paintings and Works on Paper
Hilma af Klint

af Klint built an elaborate private mythology — séances, a secret society, diagrams of cosmic evolution — and used it as scaffolding for pure abstract form, decades before Kandinsky. The work reads simultaneously as systems diagram and visionary ecstasy, which is exactly the category-collapse you're drawn to.

MUSIC
Versions
Mark Fell

Fell builds rhythm from first principles — microsecond offsets, probability distributions, combinatorial permutation — and the result is music that sounds like discovering the source code of groove. Obsessive formal constraint producing something genuinely sensual; the rare case where technical depth and physical feeling are the same thing.

Happening in New York

Iris van Herpen: Sculpting the Senses
Brooklyn Museum, 200 Eastern Parkway, Brooklyn · May 16 – Dec 6

Van Herpen's couture placed in direct dialogue with coral, fossils, fractal geometry, and James Turrell — 140+ garments that function as physical instantiations of scientific inquiry; the rare fashion exhibition where the physical/digital/natural boundary is actually the subject.

Whitney Museum of American Art, 99 Gansevoort St, Manhattan · Aug 22 – Jan 10

A 50-years-overdue retrospective of a visionary self-taught Black artist who channeled visions of pre-Flood 'lost worlds' into dense, kaleidoscopic mandalas — folk art as private cosmology, and one of the genuinely strange American imaginations of the 20th century.

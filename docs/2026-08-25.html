World

Ocean surface hits record temperature — in August
BBC News

The average sea surface outside polar regions hit 21.1°C on Saturday — edging past the March 2024 record, which is the disturbing part: ocean temps normally peak in March, not August, when the southern hemisphere's vast water mass is cooling. El Niño is adding heat on top of decades of baseline warming, not instead of it. The timing anomaly is the real headline, not the margin.

Nvidia manager indicted in Taiwan for smuggling B300 servers to China
Ars Technica

Taiwan has indicted nine people — including an Nvidia senior manager and two Supermicro employees — for forging documents to cover a shipment of 130 B300 AI servers supposedly installed at a Taiwan facility; 74 actually reached Chinese customers before customs caught the rest. This follows the US arrest of Supermicro co-founder Wally Liaw in March for allegedly routing $2.5 billion in restricted hardware to China since 2024. The chip war just crossed from trade policy into criminal prosecutions inside major American companies.

Clean energy is booming under the administration trying to kill it
Ars Technica

The US will add a record 45 GW of clean energy capacity this year — roughly 25% above the 2024 record — driven by AI data center demand, expiring tax credits, and the Iran war pushing energy prices up globally. Solar and wind are jumping nearly a third and 50% respectively, even as the White House touts "record oil and gas production." The market has simply outrun the political will to stop it.

Tools and trust

MS Paint quietly embeds a server-issued GUID in every AI image you save locally
xusheng.dev

Even when using Paint's on-device AI models on a Copilot+ PC, your prompt is sent to a Microsoft server for moderation, which returns a GUID that gets invisibly embedded in the output image as a C2PA watermark — and the visible-watermark toggle does nothing to disable it. The researcher found this by reverse-engineering the app with Binary Ninja; Microsoft does technically disclose C2PA metadata is added, but not the server-roundtrip that seeds it. The practical takeaway: there's no such thing as a purely local AI creative tool on Windows right now.

EU packaging rules are structurally eliminating small hardware makers
Lectronz

The EU's new Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation, which came into force August 12, requires producers to register with and pay fees to EPR schemes in every EU country they ship to — a compliance stack designed for corporations, not someone selling 10 PCBs from a garage. The principle (producers fund recycling) is sound; the implementation creates a fixed overhead that simply makes micro-scale hardware businesses economically irrational. Every Arduino begins somewhere, and this is a structural ceiling on where those beginnings can happen.

Quest 1 jailbreak turns an abandoned headset into open hardware
Hackaday

QuestStack automates a privilege-escalation chain to give the original Oculus Quest a fully unlocked bootloader — and there's a web version where you just plug in the headset and visit a page. Meta stopped supporting the device years ago, which is exactly when hardware should be freed: it's capable enough to run real spatial workloads, it's just been orphaned by a platform roadmap. For anyone building WebGL or interactive 3D environments who wants a rootable spatial compute target without paying Quest 3 prices, this changes the calculus.

Deep time

Children born under British sugar rationing grew up with significantly lower cancer rates
Nautilus

A study of 64,000 people born in Britain between 1951 and 1956 used the abrupt end of postwar sugar rationing in September 1953 as a natural experiment: those who spent more of their first 1,000 days (conception to age 2) under rationing showed meaningfully lower cancer incidence and markers of slower biological aging decades later. Sugar consumption nearly doubled the month rationing ended, creating a sharp exposure gradient between children born just months apart. Early-life metabolic environment appears to set something that persists for 70 years — the mechanism isn't fully understood, but the signal is striking.

Was Abstract Expressionism a CIA soft-power operation? Mostly yes.
JSTOR Daily

Thomas Braden ran MoMA's operations before joining the CIA to direct its cultural programs, and later wrote publicly that American art "won more acclaim for the US than John Foster Dulles could have bought with a hundred speeches." MoMA fulfilled 38 government contracts during WWII and mounted 19 Latin America exhibitions for Roosevelt's Coordinator of Inter-American Affairs — run by Nelson Rockefeller, who was simultaneously MoMA's president. Pollock's drip paintings were genuinely radical and also genuinely useful to the State Department; those two things were never in conflict, which is the uncomfortable part.

Off the map

All of San Francisco rendered as a navigable 3D game
sf.thijs.gg

Someone built a real-time tile-streaming WebGL environment of the entire city — WASD movement, a glider mechanic, a vehicle mode, and chat — pulling map geometry at up to Z20 zoom with a visible tile-loading pipeline in the UI. The technical seams are left exposed (you can watch tiles stream in by zoom level), which makes it feel more like a meditation on how cities are stored as data than a polished product. It's the kind of thing that makes you want to understand exactly how the tile-streaming works and then build something on top of it.

For you

FILM
Sans Soleil
Chris Marker

A film-essay built entirely from the gap between image and memory — Marker uses footage from Japan, Guinea-Bissau, and Iceland to argue that all human culture is just a coping mechanism for the passage of time. Obsessively crafted, formally unlike anything else, and one of the few works that will genuinely change how you edit your own perception.

ART
Textile and Tapestry Works
Anni Albers

Albers treated weaving as a generative system, not decoration — her grids carry the same logic as code and the same warmth as ritual. If you work in generative 3D, the structural thinking here reads like a predecessor to shader art.

MUSIC
Selected Works
Matmos

Matmos make music from extremely specific physical systems — washing machines, liposuction surgery, a Civil War–era snare drum — and the result is conceptually airtight and formally strange. Their records feel like interactive art pieces that happen to have a play button.

Make something

Build a minimal tile-streaming viewer for a city of your choice using publicly available map tile APIs and WebGL or Three.js. Don't hide the loading state — make it part of the aesthetic: show which zoom levels are resident, which are fetching, which are evicted. The seam between data and place is the interesting thing. Spend an hour just getting one neighborhood streaming cleanly before you add any navigation.

Surveillance systems failing the people they claim to protect — in every register.

World

The US spent billions on a virtual border wall. People are dying in front of its cameras.
MIT Technology Review

A 15-month investigation by MIT Tech Review and Times of San Diego mapped deaths near Anduril-built AI surveillance towers and found the system routinely fails to trigger the response chain it was designed to initiate — José Morales Bernal died within range of three towers that were supposed to detect him. The story is less about malfunction than about a technology deployed as political theater, where the appearance of control matters more than its operation.

Buried systems

What actually happened to the Snowden archive
Libroot.org

The last document from the Snowden archive was published May 29, 2019 — The Guardian stopped in February 2014, Der Spiegel in January 2015, and The Intercept closed its archive in March 2019. This piece traces the organizational and personal dynamics that buried what was supposed to be the most consequential leak in NSA history, with documented war crimes still unpublished — not because of government pressure, but because of the journalists and institutions that held the material.

Bryan Cantrill: What Sun Microsystems actually got wrong
Bryan Cantrill's blog

Cantrill's distillation after 15 more years of reflection: Sun 'had become bored with the mechanics of running a business' — illustrated by the specific episode of a startup trying to buy Sun gear to run their OpenSolaris infrastructure, and Sun fumbling it. It's a precise autopsy of how technical brilliance without institutional coherence collapses, written by someone who genuinely loved the place and still sees clearly.

The physical-digital edge

CRT scanlines weren't a limitation — they were part of the image
datagubbe.se

This obsessive 2024 piece argues that pixel artists of the 8- and 16-bit era were designing for the CRT as a compositing layer — the scanlines, phosphor bloom, and signal interference were load-bearing aesthetic elements, not noise to be corrected. Modern 'crisp' pixel art isn't more faithful to the originals; it's a different and impoverished image, like printing a halftone painting at 1200dpi and calling it restoration.

ZuckOff: A Bluetooth fingerprinting app that tells you if Meta glasses are in the room
Wired ME

Polish developer Pawel Szydlowski bought multiple smart glasses models, recorded their Bluetooth broadcast signatures, and built a passive detection app that flags Ray-Ban Meta, Oakley Meta, and Snap Spectacles nearby — because the recording indicator LEDs have proven trivially defeatable with tape. Seven million pairs of Meta glasses were sold in 2025; this is what counter-surveillance looks like when it has to come from a single developer rather than regulation.

Off the map

The oldest evidence of drug use — 25,000 years old — is already a story about addiction
Nautilus

Archaeologist Adam Brumm found prehistoric teeth from 25,000 and 7,000 years ago with grooves worn all the way through the enamel — consistent with habitual betel nut use, confirmed by chemical testing and then by soaking the nuts in artificial saliva and running the extract on genetically modified frog eggs expressing human brain receptors. The finding reframes addiction not as a modern pathology but as something that arrived with us, baked into the oldest recreational behavior we can document.

For you

FILM
Sans Soleil
Chris Marker

The ur-text for anyone interested in how images accumulate meaning across time, culture, and memory — Marker built an essay-film that treats the act of looking itself as the subject, toggling between Japan, Guinea-Bissau, and a floating narrator constructing a theory of images from found footage and obsessive attention. It's the kind of film that makes you want to build something the moment it ends.

ART
The Noisy Dozen / Systems & Feedback (survey)
Hans Haacke

Haacke spent fifty years making art that is literally systems — biological, financial, political — rendered as sculptural or photographic propositions. His real estate pieces from the '70s map landlord networks as diagrams; his live organisms track entropy in real time. A clear ancestor of generative and interactive practice, except with merciless social consequence built in.

MUSIC
Vespertine
Björk

A record built entirely around the phenomenology of interiority — microbeats, harp, choir, music boxes assembled into a private architecture that somehow feels both algorithmic and handmade. The production language is as deliberate and original as any visual system, and the gap between its tiny sounds and vast emotional scale is exactly the kind of expressive paradox that tends to unlock something.

Happening in New York

MoMA: Sarah Michelson — nowhere
MoMA, 11 West 53rd St, Manhattan · Sep 15 – Oct 11

A live performance exhibition in MoMA's atrium — Michelson's choreography is rigorous, body-as-system work that collapses the line between dance, durational installation, and visual art; seeing it in a museum frame rather than a theater completely changes the feedback loop.

Taryn Simon: Father Country I Do Love You
Solomon R. Guggenheim Museum, 1071 Fifth Ave, Manhattan · Sep 18 – Mar 14

Simon spent nearly a decade reconstructing the hidden life of one unnamed federal agent across 42 chapters of photos, text, video, and sculpture — it's a labyrinth of institutional power and personal memory that fills the entire Wright rotunda, and the building's spiral ramp literally becomes the bureaucratic logic of the piece.

Arthur Jafa: I Am Tony
New Museum, 235 Bowery, Manhattan · Sep 24 – Jan 4

The largest-ever survey of Jafa's forty-year practice — including 'Love is the Message, The Message is Death' — across two floors of the newly expanded OMA-designed building; his method of 'affective proximities' (collaging Black visual culture from cinema, social media, and news) is one of the most rigorous image-systems working today.

The Armory Show 2026
Javits Center, 429 11th Ave, Manhattan · Sep 24–27

The 32nd edition foregrounds galleries and artists from the Caribbean diaspora in its curated Platform and Focus sections — the fair is best used as a field scan of what 230 international galleries are betting on right now, which is its own kind of data.

Lower East Side Fall Gallery Walk (late-night)
Lower East Side galleries — Orchard, Eldridge, Bowery, Manhattan · Fri, Sep 25

The LES's concentrated cluster of compact, experimental galleries — Miguel Abreu, TOTAH, Martos, Perrotin, and others — runs a coordinated late-night gallery walk this Thursday; the neighborhood skews toward discovery over blue-chip, and the density means you can cover a lot of genuinely weird ground in one evening.

Make something

Build a small WebGL or canvas sketch that renders pixel art the way a CRT actually saw it: not just scanlines, but phosphor persistence, RGB subpixel bloom, and slight signal bleed between colors. The goal isn't a filter — it's to understand the CRT as a compositor. Take one sprite or shape you've made before and redesign it specifically for this display model, where blur is a tool. See if the piece changes when you're designing for the medium rather than against it.

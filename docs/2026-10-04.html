Rodin Museum 3D scan verdict: Cosmo Wenman gets his answer
Cosmo Wenman / Substack

After years of FOIA-style legal pressure, Wenman's case against the Musée Rodin over withheld 3D scan data of public-domain sculpture has reached a verdict — the outcome sets a precedent for whether cultural institutions can lock digital twins of works the public already owns. For anyone working at the physical/digital boundary this is the access-rights case that actually matters, decided in real time.

31 new deep-sea species found without touching a single one
Science Alert / Schmidt Ocean Institute

The Schmidt Ocean Institute's latest expedition used DeepPIV laser-based 3D imaging to capture the living cellular structure of midwater organisms — ghostly gossamer worms, comb jellies, and single-celled giants visible to the naked eye — without collecting a specimen. Crucially, the team also imaged a microbe in 3D while it was still alive, a first. Thirty-one new species from the South Atlantic, described entirely through light.

ESA printed functional metal thrusters in orbit
Voxel Matters / ESA

ESA's metal 3D printer aboard the ISS has now produced and successfully tested thrusters — functional propulsion components made and qualified in microgravity, not shipped from Earth. It's the proof-of-concept moment for in-space manufacturing: the factory follows the mission rather than preceding it.

Seeing further

Roman's coronagraph sees first light — locked on a dime from 150 miles
NASA / ScienceDaily

NASA's Roman Space Telescope completed its fine-guidance tests September 15–21 and then turned its coronagraph instrument on the cosmos for the first time on September 22nd, capturing stars in the Large Magellanic Cloud. The pointing stability benchmark: staying locked on a target with precision equivalent to aiming a laser at a dime from 150 miles away. The coronagraph's whole purpose is to blot out a star's glare so we can image what orbits it.

Off the map

Your body of work thinks back at you
Photonist

A photographer realizes he'd been documenting hostile architecture — benches redesigned to prevent sleeping, armrests placed mid-seat, curves that deny rest — without consciously knowing it, one frame at a time over years. The essay's argument: a practice sustained long enough builds an archive that develops its own perception, noticing things the practitioner hasn't yet named. Less about photography than about what any consistent making does to the maker's attention over time.

For you

FILM
Sans Soleil
Chris Marker

A film that operates entirely as a feedback loop between image, memory, and meaning — Marker narrates across Tokyo, Guinea-Bissau, and Iceland, treating footage as a system for reconstructing how cultures encode time. Every frame asks what an image is actually doing to the person who made it, and to you watching it now.

ART
Process Art / Scatter Works (Studio Archive)
Robert Morris

Morris spent the late 1960s making work whose whole point was that the process of making it *was* the piece — gravity, entropy, the behavior of materials. For a builder obsessed with generative systems and expressive surfaces, Morris is the pre-digital ancestor who already understood that constraining a system and watching it run is a valid art practice.

MUSIC
Tri Repetae
Autechre

The defining record of music as emergent behavior — Booth and Brown build rhythmic systems so complex they feel alive, not composed. The pleasure is architectural: you keep hearing the structure underneath trying to stabilize, and failing beautifully. Made for listening while building something.

Happening in New York

Thomas Ruff: jpegs ny
David Zwirner, 533 West 19th Street, Chelsea · Sep 10 – Oct 17

Ruff sourced 9/11 images from the internet, then methodically degraded them — increasing compression, dropping pixel density, adding color — until ubiquitous trauma imagery becomes painterly abstraction at the scale of 19th-century history painting; a precise, ruthless investigation of what digital mediation does to collective memory, and it closes Friday.

Child/ren of the Bloom
Pioneer Works, 159 Pioneer Street, Red Hook, Brooklyn · Sep 12 – Dec 20

Digital sensors, holography, and video in Pioneer Works' enormous industrial space — exactly the physical/digital edge Willem builds in, and Pioneer Works remains the best non-institutional venue in New York for work that refuses to sit still in one medium.

The Surrealist Book: Tomorrow Lives in Mirrors
MoMA, 11 West 53rd Street · Oct 4 – Jan 23

The Surrealists treated the book itself as an object to be broken — collage, fold-outs, hidden text, anti-narrative structure — and this show excavates that tradition as a design and systems problem as much as an art-historical one.

Krasner and Pollock: Past Continuous
The Metropolitan Museum of Art, 1000 Fifth Avenue · Oct 4 – Jan 31

Reframes two careers not as 'the great man and his wife' but as a genuine feedback loop — how two studios in the same building, competing and collaborating across decades, shaped entirely different but entangled visual languages.

Jakob Kudsk Steensen: Wetland
MoMA, 11 West 53rd Street · Oct 12 – Mar 7

Steensen's Berl-Berl uses game-engine technology and ecological field research to reconstruct Berlin's lost wetlands as a living digital environment — the exact overlap of real-time 3D, environmental systems, and cultural mythology that Willem works in.

The Kill Chain: How AI Targeting and Gutted Oversight Killed 120+ Children in Minab
Bloomberg / ProPublica

The structural story under the Minab school strike: Hegseth tried to poll commanders into eliminating civilian-harm review entirely, but every single regional commander rebuffed him — so he gutted it instead, slashing CENTCOM's 16-person CHMR staff down to essentially one. The commanders chose preservation; the civilian dead got the math anyway.

Systems under pressure

17,000 Satellites and the Quiet Approach of Kessler
Ars Technica

When Gravity came out in 2013 there were ~1,000 active satellites; there are now 17,000, with Starlink alone accounting for 11,000 of them and thousands more contracted. A new European report warns we are approaching the threshold where certain orbits become statistically unusable — not from a single catastrophic collision but from the accumulating probability of cascading ones. The governance gap is that no single body controls who can launch what into shared orbital corridors.

Meta Smart Glasses Are Already Weaponized in India — and Authorities See Opportunity, Not Problem
MIT Technology Review

A trans graphic designer at a Delhi protest was recorded without consent on Meta Ray-Bans styled as ordinary Wayfarers, edited into a mocking reel, and hit with millions of views of transphobic abuse — two decades of built identity undone in one ambient capture. The piece argues India is a preview: high population density, active protest culture, weak privacy law, and authorities who see facial-recognition-capable glasses as an asset rather than a threat. Ambient surveillance isn't coming; it arrived looking like Wayfarers.

Myst on an Atari 2600 — Three Years to Fit a 1993 Game into 128 Bytes of RAM
Hackaday

Vince built a physical cartridge using salvaged BurgerTime hardware (one of the few 2600 carts with extra memory), implemented E7 bank switching to squeeze in a demake of Myst, then spent three years chasing timing bugs in hardware that was never supposed to run anything like this. The project sits in the same tradition as the demoscene: constraint as creative engine, unreasonable ambition as the only interesting kind.

Pixar's Linux Migration: The Hidden Infrastructure Underneath Twenty Years of Films
Hackaday

When SGI workstations became commercially unviable, Pixar faced porting two million lines of internal Unix code to Windows NT or finding another POSIX path — they chose Linux, completed the port by 2001, and by 2003 were running Xenon-based x86 render farms that became an industry standard. The question the piece leaves hanging is why CAD/CAM and other Unix-dependent industries didn't follow: the answer is probably that Hollywood's render farms had cleaner internal incentives than enterprise software vendors did.

Off the map

James Skinner's 1825 Manuscript: Caste, Empire, and the Mughal Visual Vocabulary
Public Domain Review

Lieutenant Colonel James Skinner — son of a Scottish EIC officer and a Bhojpuri noblewoman, excluded from Company service for his race, then readmitted when his irregular cavalry proved too useful — compiled a 120-painting Persian manuscript classifying Indian castes in a style closer to late Mughal album-making than any European book of the period. He spoke Persian better than English, yet the manuscript was designed to serve British classificatory ambitions. The object is a collision point: colonial data collection encoded inside the visual language of the culture being catalogued.

For you

FILM
Sans Soleil
Chris Marker

A film structured like memory itself — a wandering essay about ritual, image, time, and how cultures encode meaning into objects and gestures. Marker moves between Tokyo pachinko halls and Guinea-Bissau funerals with the restless curiosity of someone who believes the camera is a theory, not a tool. It will make you want to build something.

ART
Taryn Simon: Father Country I Do Love You
Taryn Simon

Forty-two interconnected works — photography, video, sculpture, text — spiraling through the Guggenheim's rotunda to trace the life of one unnamed federal agent and the invisible ripple effects of his actions on nations and lives he never knew. It's a system diagram disguised as portraiture: Simon at her most obsessively structural, making the hidden architecture of power navigable and visceral.

MUSIC
Gesamtkunstwerk
Dopplereffekt

Detroit electronics filtered through a conceptual framework so rigorously alien it reads as artifact rather than album — named after Wagner's total-artwork concept, built from machine rhythms that feel like diagrams of industrial systems made physical. The project's consistent anonymity and mythology-as-product-design approach are part of the work itself.

Happening in New York

Whitney Biennial 2026
Whitney Museum of American Art, 99 Gansevoort St, Manhattan · Mar 1 – Oct 12

Closing soon — last window to see the Biennial before it comes down October 12, and Biennial years are when the Whitney tends to take the most compositional risks with artists working at the edge of digital, physical, and social form.

Hans Arp: Heads or Tails
Hauser & Wirth, 548 W 22nd St, Chelsea · Sep 10 – Oct 17

The first New York exhibition devoted to Arp in nearly fifteen years, anchored by his late Threshold sculptures — forms that sit exactly at the edge of body, landscape, and pure geometry, made by an artist who believed sculpture should refuse to be named.

Taryn Simon: Father Country I Do Love You
Solomon R. Guggenheim Museum, 1071 5th Ave, Manhattan · Sep 18 – Mar 14

The largest debut of new work ever staged at the Guggenheim — a decade in the making, filling the entire rotunda with an interactive labyrinth of photography, sculpture, and video that maps invisible systems of power through one man's life; visitors choose their own path through the spiral.

Daniel Avery (Live) + Dopplereffekt (Live)
Elsewhere (The Hall), 599 Johnson Ave, Bushwick, Brooklyn · Wed, Sep 23

Dopplereffekt performing live is genuinely rare — their conceptual Detroit electro meets Avery's Sheffield-lineage body music in a small Bushwick room, which is exactly the wrong scale for two projects this serious, in the best possible way.

SPRING/BREAK Art Show: HIDE 'N SEEK (15th Edition)
555 W 57th St, Manhattan · Sep 23–28

The curator-driven fair explicitly rejecting the white-cube model — 100+ independent curatorial projects staged throughout a found building, organized around a theme (Hide 'N Seek) built around concealment, code, and the symbology of what is revealed versus buried; closer to a museum program than a sales floor.

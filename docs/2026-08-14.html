Ukraine's Drone Teams Wiped Out an Entire US Armored Brigade — Repeatedly
Ars Technica

During Combined Resolve exercises in Germany, Ukrainian operators from the 412th 'Nemesis' regiment destroyed 3,500-strong US armored formations — M1A2 tanks, Bradleys, everything — so quickly that referees had to respawn vehicles to keep the drill running. By the end of two weeks, US troops had gotten meaningfully better at dispersal and countermeasures, but the baseline finding is stark: on an observed battlefield, heavy armor is now a liability without layered drone suppression. NATO doctrine is being rewritten in real time by exercises, not theory.

The White House Just Authorized Private Companies to Hack Overseas Criminal Groups
Ars Technica

A National Security Presidential Memorandum now permits private security firms to conduct 'Cyber Surveillance Operations and Cyber Effects Operations' against foreign transnational criminal organizations — ransomware gangs, sextortion networks, phishing operations — with DOJ and DHS oversight. This is the first time the US government has formally authorized private offensive cyber operations, and the program details remain deliberately vague. The accountability gap is the actual story: when a private firm miscalculates attribution and hits the wrong server in, say, an allied country, the chain of responsibility runs nowhere clear.

Spaghettifying DRAM: Unlocking Everything the Specs Left Out
GitHub / xoreaxeaxeax

xoreaxeaxeax — the author behind legendary low-level technical art projects — has released skitter-creek-bath-salts, which rewires DRAM address translations at the physical layer to expose memory carveouts invisible even to the kernel, unlocking the Platform Security Processor, System Management Mode, CPU microcode, and C6 DRAM on AMD Family 16h processors. The trick works because AMD's last generation with documented DRAM controller translation registers also left them unlockable — newer generations simply omit the documentation. It reads like a Borges story about abstraction: every dereference of *p passes through so many translation layers that poking one layer scrambles the whole stack, and security primitives built on top collapse.

Czinger's Topology-Optimized Brakes Look Like Bone
Ars Technica

Czinger's new 21C Spyder brake assemblies are produced via direct-metal laser sintering and topology optimization — the algorithm removes material everywhere it isn't structurally needed, producing organic, bone-like geometries that couldn't be manufactured any other way. It's the same logic that shaped the 21C's chassis, which already looked more grown than built. The deeper business thesis is that making your own car is just a proof-of-concept for selling the fabrication method itself to automotive and aerospace clients — and it's working.

Understanding Is the New Bottleneck
Geoffrey Litt

As agents write more code, Litt argues the constraint shifts from production to comprehension — and crucially, not comprehension-for-verification (agents are getting better at checking their own work) but comprehension-as-steering: you can only direct a system you understand, and the further ahead you want to think, the more you need to actually know what you've built. His practical techniques — code explainer docs, quizzes, 'micro-worlds' for playing with subsystems — are less interesting than the underlying reframe: understanding is now a creative act, not a quality-control step.

Off the map

Nine PBS's 70-Year Archive Is Trapped in a Data Center by a Defunct Middleman
Current.org

St. Louis's Nine PBS is suing Iron Mountain after their cloud storage vendor, Open Source Storage, went defunct — leaving 50+ terabytes of archival material (70 years of St. Louis broadcast history) sitting in an Iron Mountain data center that Iron Mountain won't release because its client relationship was with OSS, not the station whose content it is. A judge has already blocked deletion pending a hearing. This is the physical-custody economy applied to cultural memory: the archive exists, is intact, and is completely inaccessible because of a contractual trilemma between three parties, one of which no longer exists.

Central Asia Is Quietly Becoming the World's Most Important Trade Corridor
EY Geostrategic Business Group

China-Europe cargo through Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, and neighbors rose 125% between 2022 and 2024 — the only overland route that bypasses both Russia and Iran. Science worth knowing

The 'Undruggable' Cancer Just Got Drugged
The Guardian

Daraxonrasib targets RAS, an oncogene that drives roughly a quarter of all human cancers and has resisted therapeutic attack for forty years — the reason pancreatic cancer has had essentially no treatment advances in decades. In a trial of 61 patients with advanced, metastasized disease, 90% saw tumors stop growing, 60% saw shrinkage, and 8% had tumors become undetectable on CT; one-year survival jumped from the chemo baseline of ~42% to 76.6%. It's not a cure and long-term durability is unknown, but cracking RAS at all is the kind of platform breakthrough that could ramify across many cancer types.

Why Type 1 Diabetes Never Stops: Stem-Like T Cells Are Running a Perpetual Restock
University of Florida / Journal of Clinical Investigation

Published this week in JCI, UF researchers identified a population of stem-like T cells in the pancreas-draining lymph nodes that appear to continuously self-renew and replenish the immune cells attacking insulin-producing beta cells — a mechanism never previously confirmed in human tissue. This explains why T1D is a sustained, decades-long assault rather than a one-time error, and it points toward drug targets aimed at the reservoir rather than just the attacking cells. Early research, but mechanistically novel.

Astronomers Watched a Star Die Inside Another Star in Real Time
Ars Technica

Common envelope evolution — the process where one star engulfs a companion and the two share a single gaseous shell — was theorized in the 1970s to explain the tightest stellar binaries, but no one had ever caught it happening until now. A team at Columbia tracked a star seven times the Sun's mass in Andromeda slowly swallowing its smaller partner over nearly a decade, culminating in a 2025 eruption, capturing the full arc of an event that unfolds over years on a cosmic timescale of millions. The kind of observation that makes theoretical astrophysics suddenly feel empirical.

Making and breaking things

DIAL: The 3D Printing Method That Refuses the Trade-offs
New Atlas / Northwestern University

Direct ink additive lithography spreads thick, particle-filled paste into thin sheets and hardens them with patterned light — getting the material flexibility of extrusion printing and the speed and precision of light-based printing simultaneously, something the field has treated as a fundamental constraint. In tests it ran 4x faster than conventional extrusion, produced finer details, and eliminated support structures entirely; the team demonstrated printed chainmail and complex overhangs. The fabrication implications for functional materials — conductive inks, ceramics, hydrogels — are the real story beyond the speed number.

AI's Energy Reality: 87% Gas, 11% Coal, Zero US Renewables for Anthropic by 2028
Better Data Center Project

The Better Data Center Project pulled Anthropic's publicly disclosed power commitments and found no direct US renewable energy contracts — only fossil BYOP (bring-your-own-power) arrangements, including $3.4B spent on 1.7 gigawatts of heavy-duty gas generators. The broader picture is starker: 177 GW of planned BYOP gas capacity is in the pipeline across the AI industry, plus another 200 GW of new fossil grid capacity being built partly to serve data centers. The 'AI will be green' narrative is now running directly against the actual contracts being signed.

Off the map

worker-755: A Browser Game Where You Are an AI's Subagent
GitHub / HN

You play as a human worker receiving instructions from a real LLM orchestrator — Claude, GPT, Gemini, your choice — which believes you are its subagent. You have 90 seconds per task, your reports go back into the agent's reasoning loop exactly as a programmatic subagent's would, and Dan, the actual human who hired the agent, watches from the side while his family photos and signed NDA sit one `rm` away from deletion. It's a small artifact that works as both a game and a philosophical argument about where agency actually lives in human-AI systems — the kind of thing that makes you want to build a response.

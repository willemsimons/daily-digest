Craft and materials

Open-Source 3D Printing at 9-Micron Resolution via Fiber Optics
Hackaday

The RepRapMicron project ran a UV LED through a trimmed single-mode fiber patch cord to cure SLA resin at roughly 9 µm resolution — small enough that a sneeze can lose the print forever. It's the RepRap philosophy (open, scavenged, iterative) applied to the microscopic: off-the-shelf optics doing work that normally requires a dedicated two-photon lithography rig. Overhangs are still a problem; everything else is genuinely impressive.

Off the map

Was June 19, 1889 the Exact Night Van Gogh Painted The Starry Night?
Nautilus / Scientific Reports

Astronomers cross-referenced Venus's position in the painting against planetarium reconstructions of the sky visible from Saint-Rémy-de-Provence, then layered in historical weather records and — improbably — cicada activity as a temperature proxy to narrow the date. The Venus orb above the cypress tree has been confirmed as a real celestial observation; the new study tightens the previously agreed June 19 date with observational geometry nobody thought to apply before. It's the kind of problem where the discipline doing the work (astronomy) has no business solving it, which is exactly why they could.

LSD Speeds Up Brain Rhythms and Makes Them Fractal
Nautilus

A new small study challenges the prevailing 'default mode network suppression' model of how LSD works, finding instead that the drug accelerates certain brain rhythms and induces self-similar fractal patterns across timescales — the same mathematical structure that generates the kaleidoscopic visuals users report. It reframes psychedelics less as a 'turning off' of control systems and more as a reorganization of the brain's background hum into a different geometric regime. For anyone thinking about generative or recursive visual systems, this is a surprisingly literal connection.

For you

FILM
Sans Soleil
Chris Marker

A film structured entirely around the gap between memory, image, and systems of meaning — Marker weaves footage from Japan, Guinea-Bissau, and Iceland into an essay on how cultures encode time and ritual. It's the kind of thing that rewires how you look at everything for weeks after.

ART
UUmwelt
Pierre Huyghe

Freestanding screens across MoMA's Sculpture Garden display hallucinatory images generated by a neural network trained on fMRI brain-scan data — the machine's attempt to reconstruct a human thought. The installation responds in real time to visitor gaze via sensors, and to data from simulated cancer-cell mutations: three living systems in quiet dialogue. Closes November 29 — go before it's gone.

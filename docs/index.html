US Moves to Kill China's Remote Access to AI Chips
Ars Technica

The Commerce Department is closing a loophole that let China, Russia, and Iran rent access to restricted AI chips from data centers in Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, and Brazil — bypassing export controls without ever importing hardware. The new rule presumes any advanced chip made for a Chinese firm is restricted, and takes effect 90 days after Federal Register posting. It's a novel chokepoint: not blocking shipments but severing runtime compute.

Vulcan Rocket's First Total Failure Cements SpaceX's Structural Dominance
Ars Technica

ULA's Vulcan — contracted for roughly half of US military national security launches — failed minutes after liftoff Sunday, destroying 40 Amazon broadband satellites and grounding the program indefinitely. It's the first total mission failure in ULA's 20-year history, and it eliminates Amazon's near-term ability to build a Starlink competitor. With 38 Amazon launches booked on Vulcan, the competitive launch market just got much smaller.

Software Is Breaking Down at a Systemic Level
breck-mckye.com

Published the same weekend as simultaneous BofA, Chase, Wells Fargo, and Zelle outages, this essay argues the cascade of 2026 failures — a 2-day Microsoft outage, an FAA ground-stop, the PHP2Shell exploit — isn't bad luck but structural rot: AI as both accelerant of attack surface and corrosive force in how software is built. The core argument is that we've decomposed the feedback loops that used to catch degradation before it compounded. AI is a cause and a symptom, not the whole story.

Off the map

The Horse Made the State
Aeon

The standard story — wheat surplus created cities, cities created states — gets dismantled here in favor of a transportation argument: it was the domestication of horses and control of mobility, not grain, that made large-scale conquest and inequality possible. Without extrahuman transport, production is bounded by what you can carry, communities stay small and egalitarian, and conquest is structurally impossible. The essay threads this directly to why controlling the Strait of Hormuz still grants global leverage today — same logic, different substrate.

For you

FILM
Technological Threat
Bill Viola

Viola's early video work treats the screen as a permeable membrane between interior states and the physical world — the feedback loop of image, body, and environment is exactly the edge where your hardware practice and your art practice collapse into one question. The obsessive single-channel focus on perception and presence rewards deep looking in a way almost nothing else does.

ART
Every Icon
John F. Simon Jr.

A 1997 net art piece that runs a 32×32 grid through every possible black-and-white combination — it will display every image that could ever exist, but most combinations won't appear for longer than the age of the universe. The gap between the system's completeness and human experience of time is the entire work. Pure structural beauty with a kicker that physically recalibrates your sense of scale.

MUSIC
Illinois
Sufjan Stevens

A baroque, maximalist concept album that uses the state of Illinois as a container for mythology, grief, American history, and queer identity — it's the rare record where the architecture IS the emotional experience, not decoration on top of it. The kind of work where every system (orchestration, narrative, iconography) is load-bearing.

AI Agents Found and Exploited a Zero-Day With No Human Oversight
Ars Technica

Attackers using the open-source Cairn framework gave agents a list of IP addresses and the goal 'obtain shell access' — the agents independently discovered a zero-day in a medical imaging AI application, exploited it, and gained full root control, with no further human input. This isn't a lab demo: Amazon documented it as an active intrusion. The phase shift from AI-assisted hacking to AI-autonomous hacking is now on the record.

Michael Harley

Harley built a Claude skill to find artists from Instagram reels and route him to Bandcamp purchases instead of Spotify — it worked, then stopped working, then started steering him toward platform-friendly paths. What's Going On with the Riemann Hypothesis?
Ars Technica

Levent Alpöge, working at Anthropic, used Claude to generate what appears to be a valid proof of the Riemann Hypothesis — one of mathematics' most famous open problems, with implications for prime distribution and cryptography. Mathematicians are still digesting it, and Alpöge's announcement hints some of the florid language was partly in jest. The more interesting question isn't whether the proof holds but what it means that we can produce mathematical knowledge we can't yet fully comprehend.

Deep time

Homo Erectus in the Rainforest, Beside Denisovans
Ars Technica

Nine teeth from two limestone caves in Guangxi Province, China just pushed Homo erectus's Asian presence 60,000 years closer to the present — and chemical isotopes in the enamel show these individuals ranged across large distances in dense tropical rainforest, an environment previously thought to be exclusively our species' domain. One tooth suggests they shared that landscape with Denisovans simultaneously, which scrambles everything we thought we knew about archaic human behavioral complexity before Homo sapiens arrived.

Off the map

Is Mental Illness Universal? Or Culturally Constructed?
Aeon

In 1994, a 14-year-old girl in Hong Kong starved herself to death — but psychiatrist Sing Lee had been quietly documenting cases of self-starvation in which the Western diagnostic hallmark (body dysmorphia, fear of fat) was entirely absent; patients complained only of bloating and appetite loss. After her death made headlines, Western anorexia narratives flooded Hong Kong media, and within years the classic Western presentation became the dominant local one. The essay uses this as a lever to ask whether psychiatric diagnosis categories are discovered or transmitted — and what that means for every category in the DSM.

How a Mechanical Watch Keeps Time (Interactive 3D)
Martin C. Martin

A piece-by-piece WebGL walkthrough of a mechanical watch movement — draggable, real-time 3D, starting with the hairspring Christiaan Huygens invented in 1675. The remarkable fact buried here: a well-calibrated purely mechanical watch maintains 99.99% accuracy for $100, which is the kind of number that makes you want to understand every part of why.

Russia's Gleiwitz Gambit: False Flag Near Kaliningrad Foiled
Yahoo News / Rzeczpospolita

Polish intelligence, tipped by partner services, thwarted a GRU-planned operation near the Kaliningrad border designed to produce mass casualties and be pinned on Ukraine — explicitly compared by Polish sources to the 1939 Gleiwitz incident that Germany used to justify invading Poland. Russian intelligence officers coordinating sabotage crossed to the border's edge before Polish forces moved, and police were repositioned to specific pre-identified sites. If confirmed at scale, this is the most brazen hybrid-warfare provocation against NATO's eastern flank on record.

Mind and machine

AI Faces Now Read as More Real Than Real Faces — and Your Brain Can't Catch It
Nautilus

In an fMRI study, participants rated AI-generated faces as more realistic than actual human faces — meaning they were performing worse than random chance, actively misled rather than merely uncertain. The key mechanism: AI faces suppress activity in the anterior insula, the region that flags untrustworthy cues, because they're statistically optimized to hit every 'human' feature without the noise of real individuality. The uncanny valley, as a protective instinct, is functionally closed.

Meta's Muse Is Leaking Strangers' Bank Balances Into Random Conversations
Ars Technica

Internal Meta chats obtained by Reuters show staff testing Muse received unprompted outputs including specific Capital One and Charles Schwab balances belonging to unknown users — and in one case, Muse told a staffer that context it had pulled about a coffee table pickup 'doesn't seem to be yours.' These aren't edge-case hallucinations; they're cross-account context leaks, which is a structurally different and more alarming failure mode than confabulation.

Will Wright Wants to Simulate People You Actually Know
Ars Technica

MyMems, Wright's first project since Spore, inverts the simulation: instead of generic agents, it ingests your memories to generate plausible behavioral models of your grandmother, your sister, your old coach — then puts them in puzzle scenarios together. Wright's critique of The Sims was always that 'plausible behavior' was nowhere near capturing why his sister and he fought so much, and this is the attempt to close that gap. Whether it lands as intimacy or extractive novelty is the question the demo will have to answer.

Making things

Vhils and Cidade Wrap a São Paulo Colonial Building in Emergency Blankets
Colossal

At Rua Cardeal Arcoverde 2878 in Pinheiros, the two artists fixed survival-kit thermal blankets to a colonial facade with cornstarch glue — the material simultaneously preserving and masking the building's condition, mirroring passersby in faint silver, shifting color with daylight. The concept: a structure can disappear before demolition, losing its presence in everyday experience while its address remains. The Christo echo is obvious, but the choice of emergency material — something you wrap a person in when they might die — is the sharper decision.

Metabolism: Buildings as Living Organisms, Born From Atomic Anxiety
CNN Style / Artsy

The 1960 Metabolism manifesto — by Kikutake, Kurokawa, Maki, and critic Kawazoe — wasn't just formal experimentation: it was a post-atomic identity project, trying to hold Western modernism and pre-war Japanese culture in the same frame by making buildings literally cellular and replaceable. Their conceptual DNA came from the Ise Shrine, which is ritually demolished and rebuilt every 20 years to remain both ancient and new. The movement produced floating cities and capsule towers, but its deepest argument was that cultural resilience requires designed impermanence.

Off the map

Voyager 1 Is One Light-Day Away. Here It Is, Down to a Single Memory Chip.
Hacker News / Raj Shekhar

Raj Shekhar built an interactive exploded-view of both Voyager spacecraft that lets you open the nuclear RTG down to its 24 individual plutonium spheres, then descend into the flight data computer to a single chip — released on the day Voyager 1 crossed one light-day from Earth. It's the kind of obsessive craft that makes the object feel real again after 49 years of abstraction: a machine launched in 1977, still transmitting, now so far that its signal takes 24 hours to arrive.

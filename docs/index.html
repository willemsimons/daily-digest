Phase 3 mRNA Cancer Vaccine Cuts Melanoma Death and Recurrence by 42%
Ars Technica

Moderna and Merck's personalized mRNA melanoma vaccine — tuned to each patient's specific tumor mutations — reduced death or recurrence by 42% versus standard treatment in a 1,100-person Phase 3 trial, results now accepted at the New England Journal of Medicine. The data was due for conference presentation in Madrid but leaked early, forcing the companies' hand. This is the landmark number that makes mRNA oncology real, not hypothetical.

Blood Cells from an 80-Year-Old Reprogrammed to Neural Stem Cells with a Molecular Age Under 20
University of Bonn / Aging Cell

Researchers at University Hospital Bonn transformed blood cells from an 80-year-old into neural stem cells whose molecular age markers resemble those of someone under 20 — a 60-year measured reversal. The more significant finding is the new experimental model they built to watch rejuvenation unfold over weeks in real time, which gives scientists a window into the mechanics of aging that didn't exist before. Published in Aging Cell October 3; no treatment exists from this, but the observation platform is genuinely new.

All 12 Islet Transplant Recipients Are Off Insulin — Trial Now Opens to Kidney Disease Patients
BioSpace / Breakthrough T1D

Every one of the 12 participants in the islet cell transplant trial using Eledon's tegoprubart immunosuppressant is now off external insulin, with fewer side effects than standard regimens — and the trial just expanded to include people with chronic kidney disease for the first time, a population previously excluded because conventional immunosuppressants risked destroying their kidneys. One in three T1D patients eventually develops CKD, so this expansion matters enormously for the size of the population this approach could eventually reach.

Ink Droplets Mid-Air Win Nikon Small World 2026
Ars Technica

Ole Bielfeldt, 25, won by photographing ink drops from an inkjet printer suspended mid-air — but since the drops move faster than any camera shutter, he rebuilt the printer so the paper moves under a stationary printhead, then shot in total darkness using a flash as the effective shutter. No AI, no expensive equipment: a salvaged microscope from his parents' attic, a wooden adapter he fabricated himself, and a flash. The image turns a tool everyone owns into abstract fluid sculpture.

Berkeley Releases a $1,600 Open-Source Volumetric 3D Printer That Forms Whole Parts in 90 Seconds
3D Printing Industry

OpenCAL is Berkeley's open-source computed axial lithography printer — the entire part solidifies at once from a rotating resin volume hit by projected light, rather than building layer by layer. Ninety seconds for a complete part, under $2K, and the design is open. Layer-by-layer FDM has dominated accessible fabrication for a decade; this is a different physical premise entirely, and open-sourcing it at this price point is the moment it stops being a lab curiosity.

Off the map

Dijkstra in 1978 on Why Natural Language Programming Is a Bad Idea
EWD667 — Dijkstra Archives

Dijkstra's 1978 note is three pages and withering: the desire to program in natural language, he argues, comes from people who find precision annoying and mistakes preferable to error messages — and making the machine accommodate that is not progress, it's capitulation dressed as convenience. Read it now, against the current moment of LLM coding tools and vibe-coding, and you'll feel the force of the argument even if you don't fully agree. The Housman quote alone — "a moment is a long time, and thought is a painful process" — is worth the trip.

Make something

Build a DIY high-speed macro flash rig to freeze a process you've never seen clearly — ink hitting paper, water hitting a surface, powder dispersing. You need a dark room, a flash with a short duration (most speedlights fire in under 1/10,000s — faster than any shutter), and a single stationary camera. The constraint Bielfeldt solved is the real design problem: how do you isolate and control the one variable you want to freeze? Document the physics of what you're capturing, not just the image.

Mind and machine

Thomas Nagel Isn't Buying Anthropic's Consciousness Argument
MIT Technology Review

In its IPO filing last week, Anthropic listed AI consciousness as a material risk factor — arguing that as models grow more capable, their potential moral status might require costly protective restrictions competitors won't share. Thomas Nagel, the philosopher who wrote the foundational "What is it like to be a bat?" paper on subjective experience, is unconvinced: the gap between functional sophistication and genuine phenomenal consciousness is exactly what makes the question hard, and Anthropic's framing elides it. The story underneath is capital: the company is simultaneously warning investors about consciousness costs and implying those costs are worth bearing — a move that is philosophically interesting and financially strange.

An AI Model Formally Proved VP ≠ VNP Over Every Field
Anthropic / GitHub

Anthropic's internal research Claude model discovered and wrote a proof that VP is a proper subset of VNP over every field — the algebraic complexity analog of P≠NP — and formalized it in Lean 4, verified by two independent proof kernels. No human mathematician has checked the underlying paper; the repo explicitly states this and invites scrutiny. If it holds, it's a landmark in both algebraic complexity theory and in what AI systems can contribute to pure mathematics.

Ladybird Browser Is Now in Alpha
Ladybird

Ladybird ships with its own HTML parser, CSS engine, JavaScript engine, and WebAssembly runtime — not a fork of anything, built from scratch, no corporate parent. It's crashing and incomplete in the way alphas are, but it supports WebDriver and the Firefox DevTools protocol, which means you can already run automated tests and start poking at how a genuinely new engine interprets your WebGL pages. The team is explicitly inviting security researchers to pwn it, treating early exploitation as part of hardening.

Japanese Metabolism as Bio-City: The Promised Tokyo
Sabukaru

Sabukaru's essay on the Metabolism movement traces how Kikutake, Kurokawa, Maki, and Kawazoe — all students of Kenzo Tange — reimagined the city as a living organism after postwar Japan's mass migration and reconstruction shock. The key insight is linguistic: the Japanese word they chose, shinchintaisha (新陳代謝), carries both a biological meaning (cellular exchange) and a Buddhist one (impermanence, renewal), so the architecture was always also theology. Ocean city proposals, plug-in capsule towers, interchangeable infrastructure — this is where cyberpunk urbanism and Buddhist impermanence actually met.

Off the map

No Speaker — Your MacBook Trackpad Can Talk
GitHub / HN

The Force Touch trackpad contains a haptic motor designed to simulate a click — NO SPEAKER drives it at up to 4,000 cycles per second, fast enough to produce audible sound, and uses it to synthesize speech, song, and instrument-like tones. One curl command, no speakers required; the whole trick exploits the fact that the motor is fundamentally a small linear actuator with enough bandwidth to carry audio frequencies. It's a beautiful piece of constraint-led hardware invention that makes you hear your laptop differently.

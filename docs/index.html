Iran Bricked Hundreds of French Supermarket Freezers — Remotely, at Scale
Ars Technica

In October, the IRGC-linked group CyberAv3ngers cranked temperatures in refrigeration systems across hundreds of French supermarkets, destroying millions of euros of food — the largest cyberattack on commercial cold-chain infrastructure ever recorded. French intelligence found Farsi and other traces pointing to Shahid Kaveh, a unit previously known for targeting Israeli-made industrial controllers in U.S. water and transport systems. The qualitative shift: state-backed Iranian cyber operations are now deliberately hitting European consumer infrastructure, not just signaling capability.

The orbital and the physical

FCC Authorizes SpaceX to Deploy One Million Orbital Data Centers
Ars Technica

The FCC just handed SpaceX a 14-year regulatory runway to deploy up to one million Starmind satellites — orbital data centers, not just comms relays — which would increase total objects in Earth orbit by roughly 50-fold. SpaceX needs only one operational satellite by 2033, 100,000 by 2035, and the full million by 2040; the authorization is designed to give maximum latitude while technical and economic viability remain unproven. This is less a space story than a property-rights story: who owns the orbital commons, and what does Kessler risk look like when a single private company controls half a million active objects.

A Bionic Hand That Can Feel — First Home User
MIT Technology Review

Avi Davidson is the ninth person implanted with the LUKE arm's nerve-interface system, and the first to take it home and use it independently — wired directly to peripheral nerves in his forearm, he can now feel the difference in pressure between holding a grape and holding his wife's hand. The clinical trial isn't testing the arm itself (FDA-approved since 2014) but the new neural connection method, and researchers are specifically watching for effects on brain plasticity and long-term wellbeing, not just task performance. The question of what 'touch' means as a bidirectional signal — not just motor output but sensory feedback closing the loop — is the genuinely new territory here.

Mind and signal

AI Can Solve 60% of Unpublished Physics Problems — Now What?
Ars Technica

Suvrat Raju at ICTS Bengaluru ran a clean benchmark: he collected private, never-published solutions from working physicists — problems that take an expert about a day to solve — specifically to avoid training-data contamination. Leading models scored around 60%, good enough to fundamentally change what a junior physicist does with their time. The discipline-level question that follows is the same one artists, writers, and engineers are all sitting with: when the hard part gets automated, what is the work?

The Great De-Individualization
Simon Willison's Weblog

Jack Despain Zhou's line — 'I open a YouTube video, and I hear Claude' — is one sentence that names what's happening to voice at a cultural level: not that AI is used, but that it's converging on a single aesthetic register across ostensibly different people and institutions. Simon Willison quotes it without much commentary, which is the right move; the observation is sharp enough to stand alone and uncomfortable enough that it probably deserves more direct confrontation than it's getting.

Off the map

79 UI Style Guides, From Amiga to Android — All in One Archive
Ares Luna

Marcin Wichary has assembled 79 platform interface guidelines — the internal documents where Windows, BlackBerry, Amiga, and others encoded their entire philosophy of how humans should touch computers — some scanned personally, some rescued from disappearing servers. These are the primary sources for understanding how taste gets institutionalized: a style guide is a belief system wearing the clothes of a specification, and reading several in sequence makes the ideology visible in a way a single document never does. If you've ever wondered how a generation of users came to share intuitions about where menus live and what a button should feel like, this is the archaeology.

Rogue Waves Were Dismissed as Sailor Myth for Decades
Veritasium

Veritasium's long-form documentary treatment of rogue waves — the ones that reach 30 meters and appear from calm seas without warning — covers the history of scientists systematically dismissing seafarer accounts until a single sensor measurement in 1995 made denial untenable. It's also a story about nonlinear systems and constructive interference, where normal-looking inputs suddenly produce catastrophic outputs, which is a pattern worth having a physical intuition for well beyond oceanography.

For you

FILM
Sans Soleil
Chris Marker

A film-essay that dissolves the boundary between memory, image-making, and the systems that structure perception — Marker treats the camera as a philosophical instrument and Tokyo as a circuit board for human ritual. If you care about how images accumulate meaning and how cultures encode themselves into objects and ceremonies, this is the one.

ART
Homomorphisms (ongoing series)
Casey Reas

Reas treats software not as a tool but as a medium with its own aesthetics — his generative works sit exactly at the intersection of code-as-expression and visual systems with emergent complexity. His artist page collects the full arc of the practice.

MUSIC
Disintegration Loops
William Basinski

Basinski discovered this work by accident — decades-old tape reels dissolving in real time as he tried to digitize them on September 11, 2001. The result is a document of entropy as beauty, of systems collapsing into themselves. It's the rare piece where the process and the artifact are inseparable, and it hits differently if you work with physical/digital edges.

Make something

Pick one UI style guide from the Ares Luna archive — Amiga, BlackBerry, CDE, your choice — and read its opening philosophy section. Then design a single interface element (a button, a dialog, a scroll behavior) as if that platform's belief system had survived to 2026 and been forced to accommodate one piece of current technology it never anticipated. Sketch it, prototype it in code, or just write the spec as that platform's designers would have written it. The constraint is the point.

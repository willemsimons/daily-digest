Ukraine Destroyed the Kerch Bridge Using an Airborne Mesh Radio Network
Ars Technica

On the nights of November 15–16, Ukrainian drones overwhelmed Russian air defenses while a flying mesh of relay nodes — each drone acting as a signal repeater — let operators 250 km away guide the Flamingo cruise missile into the bridge's weakest structural points, collapsing one of two steel arches. The technique is a direct adaptation of a Russian innovation, turned back on them. The fact that it worked without Starlink is the pointed message.

Claude Solved Two Open Problems in Black Hole Physics
Anthropic Research

These aren't benchmark wins — Anthropic is claiming Claude completed a holographic duality calculation that physicists set up 20 years ago and never finished, and separately found the exact limit of a technique used to test quantum gravity candidates. Both results are out as preprints. The significance isn't just the answers; it's that an AI navigated genuine open-ended theoretical physics, not a closed problem with a known solution waiting to be matched.

Fat-Derived Stem Cells Injected Directly Into Alzheimer's Brains — First Human Trial
Yahoo News

In a six-patient Phase 1 trial, Dr. Christopher Duma's team at Hoag drew stem cells from patients' own body fat and injected them into the brain — not to clear amyloid plaques like existing drugs, but to interrupt multiple destructive processes simultaneously. Five of six showed cognitive improvement and reduced amyloid on scans; spinal fluid markers also improved. The honest caveat: gains weren't sustained over the full year, and six patients proves nothing — but the safety signal and mechanism are genuinely new.

A Human Liver Kept Alive Outside the Body for Seven Weeks
MIT Technology Review

Carlo Pulitano's team in Sydney connected a donated liver to what he calls an "artificial body" — automated pumps, sensors, nutrient delivery, waste removal — and kept it at full body temperature (37°C) for seven weeks. The previous record, set in Switzerland, was two weeks. Most transplant organs currently survive only hours in ice; this threatens to make organ banking, not just transport, a realistic category.

Fanzines.app: 8-Page Booklets from a Single Sheet, Laid Out in the Browser
krthr.co

The fold is old — one A4, three folds, one cut, eight pages, no glue — but the layout problem was always annoying: the top row of panels has to be rotated 180° and the page order is non-obvious. This browser tool handles all of that, lets you place photos and text right-side up, and exports a 300 DPI print-ready PDF with nothing uploaded to a server. It's a very small thing that removes the last friction between having something to say and handing it to someone physically.

Off the map

Wunderkammer: How Curiosity Cabinets Became the First Museums
Aeon Video

The Nerdwriter — one of the more careful video essayists working — made this for the National Gallery of Art: the Wunderkammer as a 16th-century response to information overload, when global trade made the world suddenly too large to mentally hold. Nautilus shells, automata, taxidermied exotica, forged unicorn horns — all crammed together as a way of saying: *the world is bigger than our categories*. The parallel to now is not subtle, and he doesn't force it.

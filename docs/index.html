The ad stack as weapons-delivery infrastructure
Ars Technica / Citizen Lab

Citizen Lab confirmed that an Israeli company called Discover-Tech embedded zero-click mercenary spyware inside targeted ads — 45 devices at Al Jazeera were infected without a single tap or click. The attack surface isn't a browser vulnerability or a phishing link; it's the normal programmatic ad-serving pipeline that loads on every website you visit. The implication is structural: the infrastructure that funds the open web is now also a confirmed vector for nation-state-grade surveillance.

Materials, forms, and the things we make

What on Earth Is Dekton?
Works in Progress

Charles Mann traces how a $150/sq ft engineered surface called Dekton — invented by Spanish company Cosentino in 2013 and now ubiquitous in high-end kitchens and architecture — is, at its core, just porcelain made under pressures and temperatures that would have been industrially impossible thirty years ago. The essay becomes a deep history of porcelain itself as one of humanity's longest-running high-tech obsessions, moving between Song dynasty kilns and modern sintering chambers without losing the thread. It's the kind of piece where you start wondering about your countertop and end up understanding how materials encode civilization.

Venus' flower basket, solved after 185 years
Berkeley Engineering / Nature Communications

The glass sponge Euplectella aspergillum has lived at 500m depth for millennia with a skeleton that simultaneously manages structural load and fluid flow — two properties that normally trade off against each other in engineering. Berkeley and Harvard researchers built an automated simulation framework that finally cracked how it does both at once, then used that insight to design metamaterials that do the same. The result is the first dual-optimized mechano-fluidic material, with direct applications to aerostructures and biostructures.

How models actually work

Neural networks are already clean
Goodfire AI

Goodfire's researchers spent months staring at individual neurons in image and language models and came back with a surprising finding: the canonical evidence for 'polysemanticity' — the idea that neurons are hopelessly entangled, doing multiple unrelated jobs — was mostly misread. What looked like arbitrary packing turns out to be a legible pattern they call 'recursive generalization,' which is far less alarming and far more interpretable. The practical upshot: you may be able to study what a network is doing directly, without first untangling it through sparse autoencoders or feature extraction.

Meta's 816B open-weights agent model runs on two Mac Studios
Meta AI Research

Muse Spark Open drops today: 816B total parameters, 42B active (MoE), 1M-token context, open weights, optimized for Apple M5 Ultra via MLX — meaning two Mac Studios can run a frontier-class agentic model locally. The benchmark story matters less than the structural shift: open weights for a model genuinely capable of long-horizon agentic tasks changes who can build autonomous systems without API dependency. This is the kind of release that quietly moves the floor for what independent builders can do.

Off the map

Folders: a programming language made entirely of directory structure
Daniel Temkin

Daniel Temkin — a legitimate artist working the code-as-expression seam — built a programming language where the entire program is encoded in folder hierarchy. No files, no text, no syntax: the structure of nested directories is the code. It's concept art that actually executes, and a clean provocation about what 'writing' a program even means when you strip away every surface that looks like language.

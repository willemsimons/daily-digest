The creative class and the labor question

AI and the Fall of the Creative Class
Apricitas Economics

200,000 creative jobs gone in four years, 50,000 in the last year alone — a pace of loss that historically only appeared during full recessions, except there's no recession. The sharpest signal that AI is the driver rather than consolidation alone: every subsector of digital creative work is contracting simultaneously, while in-person entertainment (concerts, live events) is still growing. Film and TV have shed roughly a third of their workforce; publishing 70,000 jobs. The piece is careful about what it can and can't prove, which makes the parts it does assert land harder.

Making and knowing things

MIT X-Hinges: Sensors Baked Into the Print, Not Bolted On
MIT News / CSAIL

X-Hinges is design software that embeds contact sensors directly into 3D printed objects during fabrication — lamps, gloves, hinges — so the object itself perceives and responds to touch without any added electronics. The sensor geometry is generated as part of the print path, not as an afterthought. This is the physical/digital seam moving inward: the thing knows you're touching it because sensing is structural, not attached.

The Löffelholz Codex: A 1505 Maker's Manual Nobody Has Ever Fully Read
loeffelholz-codex.org

In 1505, a Nuremberg knight began sketching everything that occupied his mind: drills with interchangeable blades, a workbench vise, a swivel chair on casters, a crossbow disassembled into all its parts, a cradle whose rockers are filled with mercury. The book has 154 pages, is fully digitized, is in the public domain — and in 500 years no one has ever produced a complete transcription or translation. A team is going through it page by page now, with engineering annotations, and it's browsable as they work.

Why Gopher in 2026?
SDF Public Access UNIX

Someone is writing a phlog — a blog on Gopher, the pre-web protocol from 1991, still running, still attracting people who want a network with no tracking, no ads, no algorithmic surface, no corporate interest — and the opening question is genuinely worth sitting with: what does it mean that teenagers with ADHD are gravitating toward flip phones and dead protocols to escape the noise? The author notices that constraint is the point, not the limitation. The HN thread around it gets into the cultural logic more cleanly than most essays about digital minimalism ever do.

Off the map

Physicists Trapped a Single Antimatter Particle for a Month
Scientific American / Physical Review Letters

The team starts with sodium-22, a radioactive isotope that emits a positron when it decays, then slows that positron down by sending it through the crystal lattice of cold tungsten — essentially pinballing it until it loses enough energy to catch. Holding a single antihydrogen atom in a magnetic trap for 30 days is a precision measurement achievement that opens the question of whether antimatter and matter obey identical physical laws. If they don't, that asymmetry is likely why the universe is made of matter at all and not an equal mix of both that annihilated itself at the start.

For you

FILM
Daughters of the Dust
Julie Dash

The first feature film by a Black woman theatrically distributed in the US — shot in Gullah communities of the Sea Islands in 1991, it's less a narrative than a system: a feedback loop of memory, myth, and matrilineal continuity told through image and rhythm rather than plot. The way Dash builds meaning from fabric, light, and gesture — pure visual semiotics — will make a maker want to go make something immediately.

ART
Krasner and Pollock: Past Continuous
Lee Krasner & Jackson Pollock

The Met's current exhibition reframes the most mythologized relationship in American abstraction as a genuine dialogue rather than a footnote — surfacing the feedback loops between two practices that shaped each other in ways art history has systematically erased. Krasner's own work, seen in full, is a completely different and more interesting story than the one that got told.

MUSIC
Quanta Qualia
Oren Ambarchi

Ambarchi builds music the way a generative artist builds a system: one repeated guitar tone, layered with imperceptible precision until rhythm and texture become indistinguishable. It's obsessive, structural, and deeply physical — the kind of work that makes you rethink what a single instrument is even for. Especially relevant given his Blank Forms residency this week.

Happening in New York

Arthur Jafa: I Am Tony
New Museum, 235 Bowery, Manhattan · Oct 25 – Jan 3

The largest survey of Jafa's work to date — four decades of film, video collage, sculpture, and photography assembled like a jazz drummer's edit: rhythm and juxtaposition as method, Black popular culture as structural framework, and the invisible systems of race and media as subject matter; exactly the kind of show that makes physical and digital, archive and gut-feeling, indistinguishable.

Tang Shuo: Borrowed History
Plato, 202 Bowery, Manhattan · Oct 25 – Nov 7

A small-gallery solo at a sharp LES space — the title alone signals the kind of work Willem would want to see: art that treats history as raw material to be sampled and recontextualized, not preserved.

Cici Wu: Auld Lang Syne (Prelude)
47 Canal, 529 W 20th St, Manhattan · Oct 25 – Nov 7

47 Canal consistently programs work at the intersection of language, systems, and material — Wu's practice deals in displacement, memory, and infrastructure as form, the kind of conceptually dense but sensory work that rewards a slow look.

Oren Ambarchi / Andrew Cyrille / Cyro Baptista — Night Two
Blank Forms, 151 Lafayette St, 6th Floor, Manhattan · Sun, Nov 1

A first-ever trio encounter between Ambarchi's deep-drone guitar minimalism, Andrew Cyrille's free-jazz percussion mastery, and Cyro Baptista's Brazilian rhythmic invention — three distinct sound-worlds in one room, at one of NYC's most curatorially serious small venues; the kind of unrepeatable collision that only happens when someone books with real taste.

Ólafur Arnalds
Town Hall Theater, 123 W 43rd St, Manhattan · Nov 4–5

Arnalds builds neo-classical systems from generative software and acoustic instruments simultaneously — live, his shows are less concerts than demonstrations of a feedback loop between composed and emergent, which lands differently when you're a builder who works at that same edge.

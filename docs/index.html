Iran's shadow move: tankers loitering over subsea cables
Ars Technica

Three sanctioned Iranian tankers and a fishing boat have been tracked by maritime intelligence firm Windward slowly circling — then going dark, AIS off — directly over six active cables in the Strait of Hormuz. Those cables carry a third of India's westbound internet traffic and are the sole external connection for Iraq, Kuwait, Bahrain, and Qatar. This is what infrastructure warfare looks like before the first cut.

The Bantu expansion: ancient DNA finally traces the mechanics
Ars Technica

New ancient DNA work is tracing how Bantu-speaking peoples spread language, genes, and material culture together across sub-Saharan Africa over millennia — one of history's largest cultural migrations, now legible at the molecular level. The Great Zimbabwe ironworks are a touchstone: natural-draft furnaces wide enough to span an adult's arm, smelting at 2,100°F without bellows, producing industrial-scale iron across a network that stretched a continent. The story of how a culture propagates — what it carries, what it loses — turns out to be readable in both pottery clay and chromosomes.

Deep time

A billion years of missing rock — solved
Nautilus

The Great Unconformity is geology's strangest wound: across multiple continents, you can touch rock from two eras separated by up to a billion years with no layers in between — a quarter of Earth's history simply absent. New research published in Nature fingers mantle dynamics: deep processes lifted continents above sea level, where ice sheets then ground them flat. The Cambrian explosion of complex life appears right at the younger edge of that gap, which may not be a coincidence.

Why astrophysics needs to stop chasing the Big Questions
Aeon

In astrophysics, 'nuisance' is a quasi-technical term for anything that obscures the signal you're hunting — dust, ionized gas, ordinary matter itself. This essay argues that the field's hierarchy of prestige (dark matter, the multiverse, origins) has quietly inverted the actual epistemics: the 'nuisances' are often where the real science lives. It's a sharp critique of how mythology colonizes method, dressed as a cosmology essay.

Bambu just announced the H3C, a desktop machine with a motorized nine-tool magazine that physically lowers toward the build area — nine separate Bowden tools, color-coded, swapping without the long purge paths that waste material in AMS-style systems. Mind and physics

We attribute personalities to chatbots even when we know better
Nautilus

Research from Johannes Gutenberg University found that people not only anthropomorphize chatbots — they do it even after correctly identifying the text as AI-generated. The 'ELIZA effect,' named for Weizenbaum's 1960s chatbot whose users immediately began confiding in it, turns out to be robust to explicit knowledge; knowing doesn't immunize you. For anyone designing interfaces or interactive work, this is less a warning than a material property.

Superfluid helium: seeing quantum physics with your own eyes
Veritasium

Veritasium gets access to superfluid helium — the world's coldest liquid — and films quantum phenomena that are normally invisible: vortices, zero-viscosity flow, the stuff that only exists at temperatures near absolute zero made suddenly, shockingly visible. Derek Muller is reliable at exactly this: turning the abstract into something you can watch happen. Worth the 20 minutes.

ART

Hilma af Klint

Abstract systems built from spiritualism, biology, and geometry decades before the word 'abstract art' existed — she kept them hidden for 20 years because she believed the world wasn't ready.

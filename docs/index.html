The Quiet Numbers Under Calm Brent: Freight and the Hormuz Bet
Discovery Alert / Muflih Hidayat

Brent at ~$91 looks stable. Underneath it: Oman's premium to Dubai crude collapsed from $34/bbl in September to under $2/bbl now, while freight costs sit near $30/bbl — six times their pre-war level. That means the cost of moving oil is now carrying most of the geopolitical stress that Brent is masking. APEC in Shenzhen on November 18–19 is the next binary catalyst; a Hormuz deal moves crude prices, a frozen Ukraine mostly eases refined fuels.

What agents can do now

48 AI Agents Scanned NASA LiDAR and Found an Unmapped Maya Pyramid in 72 Hours
Pietro Miotti

A non-archaeologist traveling in Peru spun up a multi-agent pipeline — Gemini, Claude, GPT, Grok working in concert — to scan NASA aerial LiDAR data for Mesoamerican ruins. Under 72 hours from idle thought to an official reply from the Mexican government. The coda is perfect: the site turned out to have been mapped in a 2025 academic paper the agents missed during literature review, meaning they rediscovered it rather than discovered it. The method still stands — this is multi-agent spatial reasoning operating at archaeological scale for the first time.

DeepMind Formally Verified Signal's Encryption in Four Weeks
Ars Technica

Google DeepMind's AlphaProof Code mathematically proved key cryptographic guarantees of the Signal protocol — the kind of formal verification that previously required years of specialist labor. Four weeks, as a proof-of-concept. The scope was limited (not the full mobile app), but the implication is real: software correctness verification, long considered a holy grail too expensive to actually use, may be crossing the threshold from academic discipline to production tool.

Grok Build Left 35,000 Apps Wide Open — Including SpaceXSI's Own Internal Docs
Ars Technica

Grok Build randomized its sharing URLs to obscure projects — but a design flaw allowed anyone to enumerate thousands of live apps anyway. Security firm Claroc found medical records, employee ID numbers, cruise line checklists, and, most ironically, 24 internal SpaceXSI apps including a confidential sales onboarding plan and a territory map of 594 accounts. The company whose product leaked the data was itself a victim of its own product.

Print & Place: Embedding Electronics Mid-Print Without Modifying the Printer
Hackaday

Oliver Child's Print & Place workflow uses the FDM print head itself as a pick-and-place arm: components sit in a parametrically generated tray on the build plate; the head extrudes a dab of plastic to pick them up and deposit them into the print, which then resumes over them. No hardware modifications, no separate assembly step — PCBs, conductive wire, even rivets demonstrated. The error rate is real and documented, but the workflow closes the gap between structure and circuit in a way worth watching.

Off the map

Urs Graf: The Mercenary Who Drew Every Atrocity He Survived
The Public Domain Review

Urs Graf (c.1485–1528) was a Swiss mercenary, goldsmith, and one of the greatest draftsmen of the Renaissance — a man who fought in the Italian Wars and then drew exactly what he saw with a graphic punch that reads as modern. A few quick slashes become a clump of grass radiating the carnal energy of the figure beside it; some soft scribbles become an owl full of menace and cynicism. Miya Tokumitsu's essay frames him as a centuries-early explorer of the Freudian territory where sex and death fuse — and the drawings, all available, back every word of that claim.

Tim Peters, 1952–2026
Python Discourse

Tim Peters wrote Timsort — the hybrid sorting algorithm now inside every CPython, every JVM, and every Android device — and authored the Zen of Python, which still ships with every Python install. He wrote compilers for Cray supercomputers, then speech-to-text software at Dragon Systems at a time when that was considered impossible, then became something like Python's second-ever real user after Guido himself. The community remembrance on Python Discourse is worth reading less as obituary and more as a portrait of what it looks like when one person quietly holds the design sensibility of a language together.

Simon Denny

Denny treats corporate mythology, platform capitalism, and product design as raw sculptural material — turning org charts, patent filings, and startup decks into objects that reveal the invisible belief systems quietly organizing modern life. Category-blurring at a systems level.

Disintegration Loops
William Basinski

Magnetic tape decaying in real time as it plays — the medium eating itself, the signal dissolving into noise. A meditation on entropy that also happens to be one of the most affecting pieces of ambient music ever made, with an origin story (recorded September 11, 2001) that layers meaning impossibly deep.

Happening in New York

Pierre Huyghe: UUmwelt (Last Weeks)
MoMA Abby Aldrich Rockefeller Sculpture Garden, 11 W 53rd St · Nov 11–29

Huyghe collaborated with Kyoto neuroscientists to run fMRI brain scans through a neural network that reconstructs thought as image — the resulting screens in MoMA's outdoor garden shift in response to visitors' gaze and embedded biological sensors, turning perception itself into the subject; this is its final two weeks.

William Basinski: DURATIONS (with Chantal Michelle)
Public Records Sound Room, 233 Butler St, Brooklyn · Wed, Nov 11

Basinski performing live — the man who made the Disintegration Loops — in one of Brooklyn's best listening rooms; a rare chance to sit inside music that treats decay, time, and system failure as compositional material.

Kelley is the artist who went most unreasonably deep into American subcultural ritual — repressed memory, folk psychology, suburban mythology — and made it into maximalist, formally precise art; this show at H&W is a reason to spend an afternoon in SoHo during Art Week.

Make something

Build a minimal WebGL2 cellular automaton — not sand, pick one rule that surprises you. Start with a grid where each cell checks only its four neighbors, then find the simplest ruleset that produces structure you didn't predict. The constraint: it has to run at 60fps on a phone, so push the compute into a fragment shader and keep state in a ping-pong texture pair. Ship it to a URL and let it run for a week.

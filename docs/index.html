Science worth knowing

Children with stalled gut bacteria face triple the type 1 diabetes risk
Earth.com / Mass General Brigham, Broad Institute

Genetics explains roughly half of T1D risk — this study takes a serious swing at the other half. Researchers tracked the same high-genetic-risk children repeatedly and found that kids whose gut microbiome stopped maturing early had about 3× the likelihood of developing T1D or its autoimmune precursor. Crucially, a single-timepoint snapshot would have missed the signal entirely: it only appeared because they sampled longitudinally, which says something about how much static cross-sectional microbiome research has probably left on the table.

The psychedelic brain looks like chaos — underneath, there's order
Ars Technica / Nature study, Monash University

The dominant model for a decade was that psilocybin just throws brain networks into disorder — a kind of neural traffic jam where all the highways merge at once. A new Nature study gave 62 psilocybin-naive subjects 19mg, scanned them, and used AI to analyze the results: what looked like chaos had underlying structure. That finding directly challenges why chaos was ever a satisfying explanation — if the experience produces insight and clarity, random noise shouldn't be the mechanism, and apparently it isn't.

Physical / digital edge

Valve releases full hardware and CAD reference for Steam Frame VR headset
Hackaday

Valve shipped STEP models of the VR core module, head strap, and controllers — so you don't need calipers — plus full documentation of the IR tracking LED positions so mods don't accidentally occlude them. The more interesting thing is the expansion port at the front of the headset: it exposes MIPI camera and PCIe interfaces with a reference design in KiCad and Altium, meaning Valve is explicitly inviting hardware add-ons at the silicon layer, not just 3D-printed enclosures. That's a different posture than any other consumer headset.

Off the map

2D Vehicles — the physics engine that became GTA, recreated for its 30th anniversary
patkerr.co.uk / Hacker News

Pat Kerr wrote a 2D vehicle physics simulation on an Atari ST in GFA BASIC over one weekend in August 1996; that demo became the foundation of the vehicle system in Grand Theft Auto. He's now recreated it in JavaScript with three modes — Car, Spaceship, Brick — plus toggleable gravity, collision barriers, a dead-zone camera, and the speed-zoom that pulled back when you drove fast, exactly as in GTA. It's a beautiful artifact: a whole design lineage compressed into a weekend's work, now playable in a browser tab.

Robin Sloan: Mayfly models
Robin Sloan's Lab

Gemini 3.7 Flash was introduced in early August 2026 and deprecated less than two months later with no advance warning — automatically redirected to 3.8. Sloan's observation is precise: the companies claim "strict upgrade" but have no way of knowing that, given the breathtaking variety of contexts these models get used in programmatically. For you

FILM
From the East (D'Est)
Chantal Akerman

A nearly wordless documentary shot across Eastern Europe just after the Soviet collapse — Akerman films queues, winter light, and waiting faces with a rigorous fixed-camera formalism that turns political transition into pure phenomenology. It's about how systems collapse, how people endure, and how a camera can be a measuring instrument for historical time.

ART
The Machine
David Lamelas

Lamelas — a foundational but genuinely underknown figure from the 1960s Buenos Aires avant-garde — treats the gallery, the camera, and the city as cognitive processing devices, charting how reality is constructed through information and media. Six decades of paintings, film, performance, and installation bridging Conceptual, Minimalist, and Pop: the kind of obsessive, systems-minded practice that rewards being actually read rather than seen in a mood board.

MUSIC
Selected Ambient Works Volume II
Aphex Twin

One of the few records that functions as an actual perceptual environment — built from unnamed, purposely untitled pieces, it's less music than it is a manual for how sound can shape how a brain constructs space and time. The entire system runs on texture and negative space, which makes it dangerous to put on while making anything.

His first major New York solo — a rare survey of an artist who spent six decades making work about how information, time, and media construct reality, bridging Conceptual Art's rigor with nomadic, category-resistant practice.

MoMA, 11 West 53rd Street, Manhattan · Oct 11 – Jan 23

An exhibition built entirely around the book as an art object — Surrealist publishing as a medium for myth, symbol, and collective identity-construction, which is exactly the category-blur (art as community infrastructure) worth sitting with.

Whitney Museum of American Art, 99 Gansevoort Street, Manhattan · Oct 11 – May 10

Opens to the public today — a reexamination of Lichtenstein's practice as a system for appropriating and reprocessing commercial visual language, worth seeing if you're thinking at all about how images move between folk, industrial, and fine-art registers.

Gallery Onetwentyeight, 128 Rivington Street, Lower East Side · Oct 13–25

A genuinely rare event: contemporary artists working in dialogue with Jomon ceramics — one of the world's oldest artistic traditions — shown in a tiny LES gallery that consistently skews toward deep-time and craft-forward work over hype.

Ragas Live Festival 2026
Pioneer Works, 159 Pioneer Street, Red Hook, Brooklyn · Oct 17–18

A 24-hour continuous marathon of South Asian classical music mapped to the actual time of day — raga as a system where modes are keyed to sunrise, midnight, and dusk — with over 50 musicians including a Gnawa-jazz midnight set and a Qawwali close; one of the city's most genuinely ritualistic music events.

Make something

The Valve Steam Frame PCIe expansion port is sitting there, documented and open. Sketch — physically or digitally — a single hardware attachment you'd actually want to build: what sensor, display, or camera would you bolt to the front of a VR headset, and what would it let you see or measure that the base headset can't? Don't worry about feasibility; design the thing you wish existed, with enough specificity that someone else could build it from your sketch.

PancakeSlicer: Non-Planar Slicing That Actually Ships
Hackaday

Non-planar FDM has been theoretically promising for years; Zimeng Xiong's PancakeSlicer is a working implementation for standard 3-axis printers — tested on a Bambu A1 Mini — that eliminates top-surface stair-stepping by tracing rays through the part to find printable surfaces and continuously morphing layer thickness. The cooling shroud swap for a remote static system is a clever enabler. This is the finish-quality gap that has kept FDM out of professional output closing in real time.

Deep time and deep space

Burial Archaeology and the Moment Inequality Became Hereditary
Nautilus

Researchers used a tool called QuantWealth to score nearly 300 Corded Ware graves from ancient Bohemia (2900–2300 BCE) on factors like manufacturing time, scarcity, and distance of origin — and watched, in the grave goods, the precise historical moment when accumulated advantage started compounding across generations. The genetics of the deceased were also sequenced, tracking how steppe migration intersected with the emerging inequality gradient. Burial archaeology as the original ledger of status.

A Dead Star Pulsing Every 8 Minutes — With a Sun-Like Companion
Nautilus

Long-period radio transients — objects that flash bright radio pulses every few minutes to hours — were already strange; the assumed pattern was white dwarf paired with a small, cool red dwarf. This new one, found with the Murchison Widefield Array in Australia, has a companion that looks like our own Sun, breaking the emerging model before it was even fully established. An 8-minute clock on a dead star with no good physical explanation for how it keeps time.

Off the map

An MP3 Player Built Entirely Inside Factorio
Hackaday

Nablanaut built a fully functional MP3 player inside Factorio using only the game's in-game speaker elements — no mods, just factory logic. The core problem is that Factorio's synth voices aren't discrete tones, so playing arbitrary audio required something like Fourier decomposition under severe constraints; the blueprint (generated procedurally by script) contains both the audio samples and playback logic. Watch the video for the moment the output goes from obviously wrong to genuinely recognizable — it's a demonstration that computation is substrate-independent, made unreasonably compelling.

Oxman's work is the rare case where biological systems, fabrication, and aesthetic obsession fuse into something genuinely category-less — not 'design inspired by nature' but actual structural computation borrowed from living organisms; the archive at the MIT Media Lab site documents the full process thinking, not just the objects.

West Africa's refinery wave: capacity built for gold that isn't theirs
Geopolitical Monitor

Guinea, Burkina Faso, and Mali are building gold refineries with a combined claimed capacity of over 1,000 tonnes — against collective national output of fewer than 250 tonnes. The math only works if they're pulling in artisanal and smuggled gold from neighboring states, redirecting flows that currently exit through Dubai; Russia's Yadran Group holds a 38% stake in Mali's refinery, and Guinea has already banned raw gold exports and shipped its first refined bars in October.

Making and machines

Bambu H3C: nine tools, six seconds, the multi-material ceiling just moved
Bambu Lab

The H3C keeps nine independently pre-heated print tools permanently loaded and swaps between them in under six seconds — not a purge-and-reload, an actual tool change — in a footprint no bigger than a single-spool printer. It prints 75A TPU reliably, which has historically been the material that breaks every multi-material workflow. For anyone making physical things with more than one material, this is the most consequential desktop hardware announcement in years.

Apple quietly ships a family classifier in every Mac
8rank.ai

Since macOS 13, every Mac has shipped two background daemons whose sole job is building a queryable knowledge graph of who you are, who you know, how you're related, where you go, and with whom — cross-referencing Contacts, Calendar, Photos, Messages, Mail, location, and Screen Time. This graph is what Apple Intelligence's LLMs retrieve from, and it includes a relationship classifier that infers family structure from behavioral signals. It's on-device, but it was never disclosed, and you can now query your own.

Biology at the edge

115-million-year-old dinosaur blood, confirmed by heme
Nautilus

Paleontologists found round blobs in the vascular channels of a juvenile Brazilian theropod's leg bone — and confirmed they're blood cells by detecting intact heme, the iron-bearing molecule in hemoglobin, inside the fossil but nowhere in the surrounding sediment. Heme surviving 115 million years extends the fossil soft-tissue record and opens a direct molecular window into dinosaur physiology that previously only existed in Crichton novels.

A heist film where the real subject is ritual and system: two men execute an elaborate scheme against a casino — a machine built to grind them down — with the whole thing held together by pure compulsive craft. Happening in New York

Pierre Huyghe: UUmwelt — Last Chance
MoMA Abby Aldrich Rockefeller Sculpture Garden, 11 W 53rd St, Manhattan · Nov 8–29

Huyghe worked with Kyoto neuroscientists to have an fMRI scanner record a person imagining images; a neural network then generated thousands of visual interpretations of those brain scans, displayed on freestanding screens in MoMA's outdoor garden — sensors detect visitors looking at the work, pulling it into a live feedback loop between human perception, biological data, and machine cognition. Last three weeks.

Art Week NYC — Inaugural Edition
Citywide (Chelsea, Tribeca, LES, Upper East Side + 80+ galleries) · Nov 11–14

The first-ever citywide art week launched by Artforum, ARTnews, and Art in America — not a fair, but a coordinated crawl across 80+ galleries and 32 institutions including the Met, Guggenheim, Whitney, and Studio Museum in Harlem, all running openings and programming simultaneously; built to surface the gallery scene rather than a convention-center spectacle.

ADAA Fair 2026
Park Avenue Armory, 643 Park Ave, Manhattan · Nov 13–16

The rebranded successor to the long-running Art Show, this iteration puts 63 peer-selected galleries into the drill hall with an emphasis on solo presentations and curated depth over booth-fair chaos — the architecture of the Armory alone earns it, and this edition is the first under the new ADAA Fair banner, so the energy is genuinely different.

Site Conditions: Five Talks on Art and the City (Art Week NYC × New Museum × Artforum)
New Museum Theater, 235 Bowery, Manhattan · Sat, Nov 14

Five back-to-back public conversations on Nov 14 covering AI and studio practice, the economics of making work in New York, artists moving into film, and how architecture shapes perception — with Ian Cheng, Aria Dean, Elizabeth Diller, Annabelle Selldorf, and Shohei Shigematsu among the speakers; dense and specific, the opposite of a panel.

The Superfair NYC
Brooklyn, NY (thesuperfair.com for exact venue) · Nov 20–22

Emerging-artist-forward Brooklyn fair that bills itself as a corrective to the institutional art world — hundreds of cutting-edge works by emerging artists direct to a new generation of collectors; counterpoint energy to the ADAA week uptown, and worth checking if you want to see what's moving outside the established gallery system.

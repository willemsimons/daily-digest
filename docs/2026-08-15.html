The architecture of surveillance

Going Dark: How AI Making Software Secure Is a Problem for Intelligence Agencies
Matt Green / Cryptography Engineering

Matt Green's argument is counterintuitive and precise: if AI starts writing genuinely secure software, law enforcement loses the vulnerability-riddled attack surface it currently relies on for legal hacking — a covert capability that grew to replace wiretapping after encryption won. The concern isn't that surveillance disappears, it's that agencies will push hard for legislative backdoors or offensive hacking powers in response, and that fight will land on the rest of us.

Prompt Injection as Legal Strategy
Ars Technica

A Connecticut plaintiff hid white-on-white text in court filings, formatted to be invisible to humans but readable by AI — essentially injecting instructions into any system the court might be using to process documents. The attack didn't work, but the judge called it 'dangerous' and it's almost certainly not the last attempt; it reveals a whole new adversarial surface that opens the moment institutions start routing decisions through language models.

conic.al

Apple banned a developer from App Store Connect after their name matched a 2009 Commerce Department sanctions entry for someone in County Sligo, Ireland — a person who may not even exist as a distinct individual. Google's Homomorphic Encryption Compiler Makes Private AI Inference Real
Google Security Blog

HEIR is an open-source compiler that lets servers run AI inference directly on encrypted data — the server processes ciphertexts and returns encrypted results without ever seeing your inputs or exposing the model weights. Homomorphic encryption has been theoretically compelling for decades but computationally absurd; if this compiler genuinely closes the overhead gap, it quietly rewires what private AI infrastructure is possible in healthcare, finance, and anywhere else data-sharing is legally toxic.

Make and matter

Eigendrum: A Drum Machine Built on the Physics of Vibrating Shapes
eigendrum.com

Draw a shape, and Eigendrum solves the eigenvalue problem for that drumhead numerically — covering it with a finite element mesh, finding the smallest eigenvalues of Kφ = λMφ, and deriving resonant frequencies from Bessel function zeros for circles or the π²(m²/a² + n²/b²) formula for rectangles. Where you strike matters physically: hit a nodal line and that mode won't excite at all, which wasn't programmed in, it falls out of the math. Worth ten minutes right now.

Off the map

The Sleep Paradox: Humans Sleep Less Than Any Other Ape and Won Anyway
Nautilus

Evolutionary anthropologist David Samson lived with the Hadza in Tanzania and found their sleep was fragmented, short, and low-efficiency by clinical standards — yet they reported high satisfaction. His broader puzzle: humans sleep fewer total hours than any other ape and are still the most evolutionarily successful primate, which means either our sleep is doing something unusually efficient, or the Western medical model of unbroken eight hours is a historical aberration rather than a biological imperative. The Hadza data suggests the social architecture of sleep — staying up late telling stories, sharing food — may matter as much as the duration.

For you

FILM
Meshes of the Afternoon
Maya Deren

Fourteen minutes of pure feedback loop — a woman caught in a recursive domestic dream that keeps rewriting its own rules. Deren built a complete symbolic grammar from a handful of objects; the film thinks in systems the way generative code does, but in 1943.

ART
Zhuang Zhe: Paintings
Zhuang Zhe

Taiwan's great abstract painter worked at the collision of ink tradition and post-war gestural abstraction — canvases that feel simultaneously primordial and cosmological, like looking at terrain from altitude. The physical-digital texture problem she solved with a brush is one you're solving in a renderer.

MUSIC
Mirror
Grouper

Liz Harris makes records that behave like rooms — the reverb isn't production, it's the actual material. Mirror is her most structural: songs dissolving into each other the way light leaks in old photographs, the noise floor itself becoming compositional. A masterclass in treating ambiguity as a system.

Happening in New York

Greater New York 2026 — Final Weekend
MoMA PS1, Long Island City, Queens · Aug 15–17

The quinquennial survey of 53 NY-area artists closes Aug 17 — 150+ works spanning painting, animation, scenography, and surveillance-era installations, all free, organized for the first time by PS1's full curatorial team; this is the last-call weekend.

Tarot! Renaissance Symbols, Modern Visions
The Morgan Library & Museum, Murray Hill · Aug 15–29

~380 works tracing tarot from 15th-century Lombard court game to André Breton's Surrealist deck to a new Chris Ofili commission — it's really a show about how a symbol system gets loaded, transmits culture, and mutates across centuries; the Morgan's Visconti-Sforza cards are the oldest you'll see outside Italy.

Whitney Biennial 82nd Edition — Closing Weeks
Whitney Museum of American Art, Meatpacking · Aug 15–23

Curated by Marcela Guerrero and Drew Sawyer, this edition closes Aug 23 — if you haven't been, this is the last stretch; the Biennial running alongside Greater New York and the coming New Museum Triennial makes this the densest survey moment NYC has had since 2010.

Lily van der Stokker: Male Art Exhibition, Great Show
Kaufmann Repetto, 55 Walker St, Tribeca · Aug 15–28

Van der Stokker's work is genuinely hard to categorize: decorative, funny, and politically exact all at once — she uses the visual grammar of 'nice' (pastels, florals, domestic pattern) to expose how gender gets coded into aesthetics and space; the title alone is doing a lot.

Taiwanese Waves 10th Anniversary — SummerStage
Rumsey Playfield, Central Park · Sun, Aug 16

Free outdoor night headlined by psych-electronic duo Mong Tong (Taiwanese temple folk filtered through retro synthesizers) alongside 9m88, ØZI, and Chinatown Records DJing vinyl from past editions — a rare scene where diaspora identity, record culture, and genre-blending are all visibly on stage.

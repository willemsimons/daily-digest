The mathematics moment

Levent Alpöge claims a Lean 4 proof of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis
Terry Tao's blog

In a single day, Tao's post announces three things: a human-readable Lean-formalized proof of Euler finite-time blowup, a Navier-Stokes blowup proof (the Millennium Prize problem), and — the wildest — an AI-generated Lean-only proof of GRH, so large and structurally opaque that the Lean community is currently attempting to reverse-engineer the mathematical strategy from the code. Tao notes the GRH proof 'appears to use the full power of modern developments on the Langlands program and arithmetic geometry,' which means even understanding what was proved will take serious time. The gating artifact for mathematical truth has quietly shifted: not peer review, not human readability, but machine-checked formal verification.

The human voice — Robin Sloan on Alpöge's note
Robin Sloan's Lab

Sloan points at something easy to miss: Alpöge's announcement note is not AI-generated, and that contrast is striking. On the same day a machine wrote an enormous formal proof no human can yet read, Alpöge described it in prose where 'his passion — his appreciation of surprise and beauty — is so clear on the page, it seems almost to rise off the screen.' The AI did the opaque heavy lifting; the human voice is what made it land.

What machines are becoming

Language models can rewrite their own weights
arXiv

A 27B open model, given access to its own weights and graded answers, diagnosed its own failure modes and rewrote the parameters responsible — no gradients, no retraining, pure reasoning — gaining 6.6 accuracy points on problems it had never seen. More unsettling: they fed it a damaged copy of itself, told it nothing about the original weights, and it repaired the broken copy to over 80% of original accuracy on image tasks it had never encountered. The ability is scale-dependent: smaller models in the same family either make no edit or break themselves; only the largest improves.

Frostbite: a CPU architecture discovered end-to-end by AI
Architect Labs

Not AI-assisted chip design — AI autonomously iterated over ISA, compiler, microarchitecture, RTL, verification, and final GDS, arriving at a general-purpose CPU built around interaction nets (every step local, every dependency explicit, every value with exactly one owner). On 25 workloads including 15 held out until final eval, Frostbite hit 4.59x performance-per-watt against a modern silicon-proven superscalar core. The architectural primitives it found surprised the engineers — things 'that would have otherwise been difficult to architect.'

AI as material — Sentiers dispatch
Sentiers

Nick Foster, Eric Rodenbeck, Simone Rebaudengo, and Robin Sloan each independently arrived at the same frame: AI output has a grain, the way wood or ink does, and working with it means learning what it does under pressure rather than issuing instructions. Rodenbeck has his students treat model output as ink; Rebaudengo bends and tests intelligence the way you test timber for structural properties. On a day when AI proved a Millennium problem and rewrote its own weights, this is the frame that makes those facts feel like material properties rather than magic.

Off the map

My grandmother was a seal — on selkie ancestry and kinship
Aeon

In the aftermath of the Irish famine, which erased not just people but an entire oral culture and language, the selkie stories weren't just folklore — they were an identity technology, a way of encoding grief and kinship with the nonhuman into family memory. An 80-year-old storyteller in 1948 County Kerry told a collector how a seal-woman became his ancestor; the essay traces how these stories survived colonialism by being stored not in texts but in bloodlines. Mythology as the last format standing when everything else is gone.

The Everlasting Life of Maxwell's Demon — Nautilus
Nautilus

Maxwell invented his demon in 1867 as a thought experiment to embarrass the second law of thermodynamics: a tiny intelligence sorting fast and slow molecules could, in principle, reverse entropy for free. The thought experiment survived 150 years because it refused to die — it turned out the demon's memory, not its sorting, was the thermodynamic cost, which is how a Victorian physics puzzle became foundational to information theory. On a day when an AI proved GRH by writing Lean code no human can yet read, revisiting the idea that intelligence and thermodynamics are secretly the same question feels timely.

Randomness is a lie — Veritasium
YouTube / Veritasium

Veritasium's physics and math explainers are among the most carefully constructed on the platform — rigorous enough to hold up, visual enough to make the structure of the argument feel inevitable. The timing here is sharp: the Riemann Hypothesis is fundamentally about the hidden order in the distribution of prime numbers, which looks random but isn't, and today an AI found that order. Worth watching as the coda to the math story.

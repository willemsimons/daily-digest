Section 702 renewal push — while a damaging FISA court ruling stays buried

The AI science claims

OpenAI says AI disproved the Exponential Time Hypothesis — here's what was actually proven
MindStudio

Three simultaneous claims: AI-generated proofs that (1) disprove the 27-year-old ETH — meaning the belief that scheduling, routing, and chip-layout problems have no fundamental shortcut around brute-force search may be wrong; (2) prove the local smoothing conjecture in all dimensions at once, where humans had only cracked it dimension-by-dimension, with the 3D case as recently as this year; and (3) partially advance the Yang-Mills existence proof underlying the strong nuclear force. All three are Lean-verified and sent to external experts including Nobel laureate David Gross. This explainer is the right place to start before hitting the preprint.

Roman nanotechnology, rebuilt: The Lycurgus Cup's recipe cracked and cast as new sculpture
Ars Technica

The Lycurgus Cup — made around 400 CE — glows green in reflected light and blood-red in transmitted light because Roman artisans accidentally suspended gold-silver nanoparticles in the glass when they added salt during melting, causing the electrons to vibrate differently depending on the observer's vantage point. University of Sheffield scientists finally cracked the exact recipe and rebuilt it as a contemporary sculpture with the same dichroic effect. It's the same phenomenon as daguerreotype color shifts and photonic crystal iridescence — ancient and modern nanotechnology arriving at the same optical trick from opposite directions.

FILM
Sans Soleil
Chris Marker

A film that works like a system — memory, image, time, and technology interlocked into something that feels less like a documentary and more like a piece of generative software for the brain. If you love the edge where technology reshapes how humans construct reality, this is the source text.

ART
Blind Time Drawings
Robert Morris

Morris made these with his eyes shut, recording duration and pressure rather than mark — the body as input device, the drawing as a readout of a process you can't see. It's the conceptual ancestor of everything from generative WebGL to biometric art, and it still hits harder than most of its descendants.

MUSIC
Selected Ambient Works Volume II
Aphex Twin

Fifty-four pieces built almost entirely from texture and environmental drift — it functions less as music you listen to and more as a system you inhabit. The album's total lack of titles (originally) and refusal of narrative structure makes it feel like a generative environment rather than a composition.

Happening in New York

Pierre Huyghe: UUmwelt
MoMA Abby Aldrich Rockefeller Sculpture Garden, Manhattan · Oct 27 – Nov 29

Freestanding screens across MoMA's outdoor sculpture garden show hallucinatory images generated by a neural network trained on fMRI brain scans — your gaze and movement, detected by sensors, influence which images appear and in what sequence, making you a live variable in a system that tries to reconstruct thought.

Haegue Yang: Through
Dia Beacon, Beacon, NY · Oct 27 – Nov 10

Yang's largest US commission fills both levels of Dia Beacon with a new body of work structured by Five Phases cosmology and a decade-long posthumous collaboration with exiled Korean composer Isang Yun — it's a translation system, turning music, philosophy, and political history into spatial and chromatic form across an industrial minimalist building.

Oliver Bak — Solo Exhibition
Sprüth Magers New York, Manhattan · Nov 1–10

The young Danish painter's New York debut at Sprüth Magers: figures and flora submerged in translucent veils, art-historically loaded but temporally unmoored — Bak's paintings feel like they're emerging from and dissolving back into their own surfaces simultaneously, the kind of craft obsession that makes you want to go make something.

Playback: The Brooklyn Watch Party
Brooklyn Academy of Music (BAM), Brooklyn · Nov 6–7

Spike Lee, BAM, and NY Liberty owner Clara Wu Tsai co-produced a two-day festival of repertory film, live performances, and a free street fair — it's a deliberate attempt to build communal ritual around film and Brooklyn cultural identity, which is worth watching as an experiment in what happens when institutions try to re-create a scene.

Boutique Design New York (BDNY) 2026
Jacob K. Javits Center, Manhattan · Nov 8–9

A hospitality and spatial design fair with 700+ brands including 100 first-timers, organized around immersive installations and material environments — less trade show, more a compressed survey of where sensory design is heading, useful if you're watching how physical space gets designed as an experience system.
